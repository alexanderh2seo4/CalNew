## Four priorities

1. **Repair strict chemistry vocabulary and rescreen cached candidates before any collection.** There are 400 rejected records whose title contains exact-word scent/fragrant/scented plus chemical/profile/composition/synthesis/intensity evidence (58 lack abstracts). A separate olfactometr*/olfactive title bucket has 363 rejected records (213 lack abstracts; 12 say olfactive), with 761 distinct candidates in their union. These are bounded review leads, not projected admissions. Clear reviewed positives include 10.3390/molecules24091773 (219 floral GC-MS components), 10.1104/pp.116.2.599 (benzenoid ester scent biosynthesis), 10.1002/cbdv.200890087 (novel fragrant perfumery molecules), 10.1002/chem.202201037 (woody odorant synthesis), 10.1021/jf902946v (44 Indian cress odorants) and 10.1002/jssc.200500509 (GC-O threshold/intensity methodology). Add precise aroma contexts with chemical evidence; do not grant blanket scent, fragrance plant, insect or GC-O inclusion. Historical no-abstract chemistry has additional class-vocabulary gaps: e.g. 10.1021/jf00081a044, olfactive alkylpyrazines, contains neither a currently recognized odor token nor chemical-class token. Estimated useful yield: dozens to a few hundred is plausible in this cache, but there is no representative sample or measured yield, and many candidates must remain excluded.

2. **Replace collateral exclusion matches with narrow evidence exceptions for actual ligand and aroma assays.** The global clinical/neural gate rejects a paper when the abstract mentions `anosmia` or `olfactory bulb` unless narrow title conditions rescue it. 10.1093/chemse/bjac037 measures human muscone thresholds/intensity and OR5AN1 activity; 10.1371/journal.pgen.1009564 links named odor ligands to human perception; 10.1016/j.neuron.2013.10.021 identifies muscone receptor selectivity including human OR5AN1. These are molecular/perception research. Ten cached leads nominated by receptor/musk title plus named aroma ligand abstract include these four metadata records (one is a preprint of the same study), but also genuine generic neural learning and drug-derivative negatives. Separately the generic-sensing gate rejects 10.1016/j.chroma.2026.466921 (11 agarwood sesquiterpenes plus receptor docking) and 10.1007/s10068-023-01455-2 (Osmanthus odor-active fractions/intensities) solely because they also use an electronic nose. Rescue requires actual chemical/ligand endpoint evidence; Parkinson/COVID smell screening, generic sensor performance and odor cues in memory studies remain excluded. Likely small high-quality recovery, with no reason to admit the 6,268 broad clinical/neural-context negatives as a group.

3. **Add new focused Crossref perfume chemistry families after gate repair, with a finite pilot and manual diagnostic.** Two 50-row ranked probes show a real source gap. `query.title=musks` returned 50 rows: 34 DOI identities absent from the cache, 16 present, 8 already accepted. New central leads include 10.1002/ejoc.201301107 (dienone musks synthesis/olfactory properties), 10.1093/chemse/17.5.573 (3D benzenoid musk model), and historical nitro/polycyclic musk synthesis. The provider total 323 is only a ranked candidate count. A bounded completion of this small stream is more defensible than more generic volatile/reference requests, but expected admissible yield is tens, not hundreds established by evidence. `query.title=scent` returned 50, 43 uncached, but its earliest ranks are mainly ambiguous titles, literature, medicine and scent marketing; only one uncached title is immediately self-contained scent-volatiles chemistry, 10.1016/j.bse.2011.12.018. Its 3,681 ranked total must not be counted as papers or used as a yield estimate. Prefer focused `floral scent compounds`, `fragrant molecules`, `olfactive properties`, `musk synthesis`, and `structure olfactory` streams; use exact DOI deduplication, original type/date gates and abstract ambiguity rules. No blanket broad scent crawl is recommended on these results.

4. **Use the newly verified chemistry hubs to nominate different exact DOI neighborhoods, rather than extend exhausted low-yield tails.** Once candidate titles/abstracts pass scope, retrieve the real deposited references of the newly recovered perfumery synthesis/GC-O/structure-odor papers. They provide chemistry-specific starting points absent from a source graph dominated by biomedical odor mentions. The current first-page musk probe already supplies retained real reference arrays for proof/reuse; neither references nor an uncached DOI establish relevance. Prioritize titled molecular chemistry/odor relationships and rescreen metadata; stop if pilot new accepted yield stays low. Candidate scope is the deduplicated true DOI endpoints of those few new hubs, not the provider hit estimate or all reference rows. Yield cannot be defensibly estimated until the new hub sets are enumerated. This route changes the starting evidence, unlike running the same 4,000-request tail again.

## Exact offending gates at audit time

- `pipeline/atlas.py`, `RX['aroma']`: recognizes aroma/fragranc/perfum/olfactophor/odorophor, but not scent or fragrant.
- `RX['odor']`: recognizes odour/odor, `olfactor\w*`, `olfaction`, smell; omits `olfactive` and `olfactometr*` even though `RX['potency']` recognizes olfactometry.
- `classify`: central-title/incidental rule requires title aroma/odor or flavor+chem, otherwise two aroma matches in full text. A single legitimate perfumery word in the abstract cannot rescue an explicitly floral-scent title.
- Global outscope rule tests the entire abstract for anosmia/olfactory bulb but rescues only strong title aroma, title chemical+odorant, or title potency+odor; specific named ligand assays can fail it.
- Generic sensing rule allows only a small explicit aroma/odorant title vocabulary. `odor-active`, `olfactory`, GC-O and actual chemically characterized oils do not independently rescue the title.
- `pipeline/supplement.py`, `central`: omits scent/fragrant as explicit context.
- Shared provider missing-abstract tests use the same incomplete aroma/odor/chem vocabulary, so repairing one source only is insufficient. Keep source-specific date/type tests intact.
