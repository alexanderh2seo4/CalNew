# Academic research groups (2018–2026) on ML prediction of odor potency (ODT, intensity, odor strength, odorous-vs-odorless)

Research current as of 26 September 2026. Every factual line carries an inline source. Items I could not verify are in **Gaps**. Where a figure is reported by a secondary source (search snippet, news page) rather than the paper itself, it is flagged **uncertain**.

## Q1. Threshold-regression groups: Hou et al. 2026, Li et al. 2025, Wu/Wang/Yu 2026, Sun/Jia/Xi 2026, Toropov, Oliveira 2023, Huang 2024

### Takeaway
The ODT-regression field is dominated by small, single-lab efforts on curated datasets of 0.3k–1.8k compounds. The headline accuracies (R² 0.83–0.94) all come from **random** splits on descriptor/fingerprint + gradient-boosting pipelines; the only group that reports **scaffold-split** ODT numbers (Wu/Wang/Yu, UCL + Metanovas) shows dramatically worse generalization (normalized MSE ≈ 0.60 vs 0.21 for vapor pressure on the same backbone), which is the single most important methodological caveat in this literature.

### Cited Findings

**Hou, Zhou, Shi, Wang, Liu, Ji, Zhang, Mao 2026 — Jiangnan University (Wuxi, China) + Jiangnan University (Shaoxing) Industrial Technology Research Institute + Dept. of Food Science and Technology, National University of Singapore**
- Paper: "Machine learning reveals structural determinants of odor detection thresholds and identifies high-potency food odorants", *npj Science of Food* 10(1), published 3 September 2026, DOI 10.1038/s41538-026-01125-7, PMCID PMC13558726 — [Europe PMC record](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=%22structural%20determinants%20of%20odor%20detection%20thresholds%22&resultType=core&format=json)
- Task: regression of **aqueous** (water-matrix) ODT. Dataset: 1,003 compounds curated from van Gemert's *Odor Thresholds* (2011 edition) plus post-2011 peer-reviewed literature; air and oil matrices excluded; duplicates merged by canonical SMILES; multiple reported values aggregated by geometric mean within consistent order-of-magnitude ranges — [PMC13558726](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13558726/)
- Features: **121 retained molecular descriptors** (physicochemical, topological, electronic — molecular size, polarity, H-bonding capacity, connectivity). Morgan fingerprints (radius 2, 2048 bits) used additionally for chemical-space visualization — [PMC13558726](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13558726/)
- Models benchmarked: **nine** — XGBoost, GBDT, LightGBM, Random Forest, SVM, CatBoost, KNN, MLP, AdaBoost. **XGBoost won.** Hyperparameters via GridSearchCV + 5-fold CV (values in their Table S6, not in main text) — [PMC13558726](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13558726/)
- Split: **random 80/20, `random_state = 42`. No scaffold split.** — [PMC13558726](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13558726/)
- Metrics: train R² 0.9935; **test R² 0.8268**, RMSE 0.8323, MAE 0.6394, MSE 0.6939; CV R² 0.7980 ± 0.0394, RMSE 0.8268 ± 0.0753, MAE 0.6045 ± 0.0448 — [PMC13558726](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13558726/)
- External validation on **177 independently measured compounds**: 87.0% of predictions within 10-fold deviation, 97.7% within 100-fold — [Europe PMC abstract](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=DOI:%2210.1038/s41538-026-01125-7%22&resultType=core&format=json)
- Prospective use: model-guided screening of **1,041 candidate flavour molecules** prioritized **102** predicted low-ODT compounds; **12** commercially available ones went to human sensory verification — of those, eight predicted thresholds fell within 10-fold of measurement and four within 100-fold — [Asia Pacific Food Industry write-up](https://www.apfoodonline.com/industry/machine-learning-high-potency-aroma-compounds-food/)
- SHAP: top descriptors PEOE_VSA1, NHOHCount, and molecular-weight-related BCUT descriptors; higher PEOE_VSA1/NHOHCount/TPSA → negative SHAP → higher predicted ODT (weaker odorant). Electronic/topological descriptors outranked MolLogP — [PMC13558726](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13558726/)
- Code/data: https://github.com/Hvard24/odt-prediction-ml (curated datasets, training scripts, prediction workflows). **No license stated in the text.** Stack: Python 3.7, scikit-learn 1.3.2, XGBoost 2.1.4, LightGBM 4.5.0, CatBoost 1.2.8. Y-randomization (n=100) run as a negative control. **No hardware or training-time disclosure.** — [PMC13558726](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13558726/)

**Li Qiao, Li Shaofang, Luo Jie, Yuan Honglun 2025 — Hainan University (State Key Laboratory of Tropical Crop Breeding) + Yazhouwan National Laboratory, Sanya, China**
- Paper: "Predictive Models of Odor Contribution and Thresholds for Volatiles in Identification of Novel Crop Aroma Compounds", *Metabolites* 15(11):747, November 2025, DOI 10.3390/metabo15110747, PMCID PMC12654368 — [PMC12654368](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12654368/)
- Two tasks: (a) aroma-**contribution** classification from 31,459 odor-descriptor annotation records; (b) **odor-threshold regression** on **716 volatile compounds**. Sources include FlavorNet, PubChem and specialized olfactory resources; thresholds standardized to mg/L and modelled as −log₁₀(mg/L) — [PMC12654368](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12654368/)
- Features: ECFP4 (1024 bit), ECFP6 (1024 bit), MACCS (167 bit), plus physicochemical descriptors (MW, logP, TPSA, molar refractivity). Model families: RF, GBDT, MLP, GCN — [PMC12654368](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12654368/)
- Best threshold model: **ECFP4–GBDT, R² = 0.94, RMSE = 0.44** overall; RMSE stratified by threshold tertile: low 0.17, mid 0.38, high 0.64. Best contribution model: ECFP6–GBDT, macro-F1 0.732 / weighted-F1 0.912 — [PMC12654368](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12654368/)
- Split: **"Murcko scaffold-aware greedy split"**, default validation fraction 0.2; majority class downsampled to 5,000 for the classification task — [PMC12654368](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12654368/)
- Hyperparameter search (5-fold CV): RF n_estimators 200–600, max_depth 10–30; GBDT n_estimators 300–900, max_depth 4–8, lr 0.03–0.1, subsample/colsample/lambda variants; MLP hidden (512,128)/(256,128)/(256,64); GCN hidden dim 64–256, dropout 0.1–0.5 — [PMC12654368](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12654368/)
- Code/data: Zenodo archive https://zenodo.org/records/17559514 (code, curated SMILES/targets/splits, trained models, full results) with a GitHub mirror. **License not stated in the extracted text. No compute disclosure.** — [PMC12654368](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12654368/)

**Shuang Wu (UCL), Meijie Wang and Lun Yu (Metanovas Biotech, San Francisco) 2026 — the only scaffold-split ODT numbers**
- Paper: "Safe Multitask Molecular Graph Networks for Vapor Pressure and Odor Threshold Prediction", arXiv:2601.16426, 23 January 2026. Affiliation: Shuang Wu, Dept. of Civil, Environmental and Geomatic Engineering, University College London; Meijie Wang and Lun Yu, Metanovas Biotech Inc. — [arXiv:2601.16426 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.16426)
- Datasets: **VP** — 1,852 unique molecules / 9,260 temperature-resolved rows from the open PUFFIN repository (median 5 distinct temperatures per molecule). **ODT ("OP")** — **1,042 molecules**, 598 air + 444 water records, harvested by targeted web scraping of publicly accessible regulatory and literature tables; multiple reports per (molecule, medium) aggregated as log-space median — [arXiv:2601.16426](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.16426)
- Architecture: GINE and PNA (degree-aware scalers) backbones; **20-dim atom features ("A20")**: element, **degree**, formal charge, hybridization, aromaticity, ring membership, total H count, chirality-center flag; **17-dim edge features ("E17")**: bond order, conjugation, ring membership, stereochemistry, ring-size indicators. Temperature injected by late fusion (standardized Kelvin concatenated after graph pooling). Separate lightweight linear heads for VP, OP-air, OP-water; targets standardized with training-fold statistics — [arXiv:2601.16426](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.16426)
- Training: "safe multitask" schedule — phase 1 VP-only for 30 epochs, phase 2 activates OP with a 90-epoch linear λ warm-up to λ = 1e-3, optional gradient detaching for the OP head; cosine-annealed LR, global gradient clipping at 5.0, residual connections + batch norm, per-task validation checkpointing. Loss: MSE on standardized targets for VP; MSE (default) or Huber δ = 1.5 with winsorization for OP. OP preprocessing: 2–2.5% quantile winsorization (training fold only) and robust median/MAD standardization — [arXiv:2601.16426](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.16426)
- Split: **Bemis–Murcko scaffold split 80/10/10**, capacity-aware bin packing, **three independent scaffold partitions (A–C)** × **5 random seeds** each — [arXiv:2601.16426](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.16426)
- Metrics (best Safe-MT PNA): **VP** normalized MSE 0.208 ± 0.004, MAE 0.341, R² 0.904, RMSE 8.45e3 Pa; single-task PNA VP MSE 0.210 ± 0.005, R² 0.901. **OP** normalized MSE 0.605 ± 0.017, MAE 0.608 (single-task PNA: 0.598 ± 0.020 / 0.602) — i.e. the multitask gain lands on VP, and ODT under scaffold split stays roughly 3× worse in normalized MSE than VP — [arXiv:2601.16426](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.16426)
- Compute: **1:10–1:40 (h:mm) wall-clock per scaffold split on a single GPU**; 120–160 epochs to best checkpoint; VP MSE coefficient of variation 1.92% for Safe-MT. Seeds and PyTorch/RDKit/CUDA versions recorded in run logs; authors pledge to release dataset builders, scaffold splits, training commands and plotting utilities but **no repository URL was given in the version I read** — [arXiv:2601.16426](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.16426)

**Yuanxi Huang et al. 2024 — Hunan University (China) + Case Western Reserve University (USA)**
- Paper: "Predicting Odor Sensory Attributes of Unidentified Chemicals in Water Using Fragmentation Mass Spectra with Machine Learning Models", *Environmental Science & Technology* 58(26):11504–11513, 2024 — [ACS page](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acs.est.4c01763) / [PubMed 38877978](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38877978)

**Andrey Toropov et al. (Istituto di Ricerche Farmacologiche Mario Negri, Milan — CORAL software lineage)**
- "Odor threshold prediction by means of the Monte Carlo method", *Ecotoxicology and Environmental Safety*, 2016 — [PubMed 27500544](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27500544/)

Best model: training **n = 523, r² = 0.647, RMSE = 1.18**; calibration **n = 191, r² = 0.610, RMSE = 1.03** — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11030-017-9800-5) / [PubMed 29209954](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29209954/)

**Luis M. C. Oliveira, Vinícius V. Santana, Alírio E. Rodrigues, Ana M. Ribeiro (LSRE-LCM / ALiCE, Faculty of Engineering, University of Porto, Portugal) and Idelfonso B. R. Nogueira (Dept. of Chemical Engineering, NTNU, Norway) 2023**
- Paper: "A framework for predicting odor threshold values of perfumes by scientific machine learning and transfer learning", *Heliyon* 9, e20813, 2023, DOI 10.1016/j.heliyon.2023.e20813, PMCID PMC10589844 — [PMC10589844](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10589844/)
- Very small data: threshold source is "Odor Thresholds for Chemicals with Established Occupational Health Standards" (2nd ed.), originally 295 molecules, filtered to perfume-industry compounds → **final set of only 45 molecules**; the lowest reported ODT was taken per compound. GCN pretraining set: **273 molecules** with semantic odor descriptors scraped from chemical-property databases and a perfume-ingredient database — [PMC10589844](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10589844/)
- Architecture: GCN trained on descriptors → 128-dim embedding → feedforward net with 3 hidden layers (480, 352, 416 neurons), lr 1e-3, dropout 0.5. Benchmark (no transfer learning): 3 hidden layers (448, 512, 416) on ECFP4 input, lr 1e-3 — [PMC10589844](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10589844/)
- Metrics: transfer-learning model **MSE 0.0179** (cross-validation average) vs benchmark **0.0220** vs arithmetic-mean baseline **0.0218**. **No R²/RMSE/MAE reported; no code/data availability statement; no compute disclosure.** Note the benchmark barely beats predicting the mean — with 45 molecules these numbers carry little weight — [PMC10589844](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10589844/)
- **Still active, and now on intensity:** the same Nogueira/Ribeiro line produced **VIANA** in 2026 (see Q2).

### Inferences
- Hou (R² 0.83, random split, water) and Li (R² 0.94, scaffold-aware split, 716 compounds) are not comparable: different matrices, different target transforms, different data-curation strictness, and Li's split is scaffold-aware while Hou's is not. Li's R² = 0.94 on 716 compounds with a scaffold-aware split is, taken at face value, the single strongest published ODT-regression result — but it is in a low-impact venue and the surprisingly tight RMSE (0.44 log units) relative to the known ~1-log-unit reproducibility of threshold measurements deserves scepticism.
- The UCL/Metanovas scaffold-split ODT result is the best evidence that random-split ODT R² values in the 0.8–0.94 range substantially overstate out-of-distribution performance.
- The MS²-based line (Huang 2024 → Sun/Jia/Xi 2026) is a distinct research programme: it targets *unidentified* chemicals in environmental/water samples, so its value proposition is applicability rather than accuracy.

## Q2. Intensity / dose–response and odor-strength groups: Monell/CSHL, Leipzig, POI-3DGCN, VIANA

### Takeaway
Two genuinely distinct approaches now exist for intensity: Monell/CSHL built a **physical dose–response framework** (real olfactometry, Hill curves, DNN on transport descriptors) that reaches r ≈ 0.87 for single molecules and generalizes to mixtures, while Leipzig (Westermayr) attacked **ordinal odor strength** from database labels on 2,393 molecules with the most rigorous reproducibility package in the field. Both are markedly more honest about uncertainty than the ODT-regression papers.

### Cited Findings

**Robert Pellegrino & Joel D. Mainland 2025 — Monell Chemical Senses Center + University of Pennsylvania, with Cold Spring Harbor Laboratory, Haverford College, Arizona State University / Osmo, and Ajinomoto**
- Paper: "A quantitative framework for predicting odor intensity across molecules and mixtures", bioRxiv 10.1101/2025.08.08.668954 (posted August 2025), PMCID PMC12363845; also deposited in the CSHL repository — [PMC12363845](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12363845/) / [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.08.08.668954v1) / [PubMed 40832259](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40832259/)
- Author list and affiliations: Robert Pellegrino (Monell), Khristina Samoilova (CSHL), Yusuke Ihara (Monell/Ajinomoto), Matthew Andres (Monell), Vijay Singh (Haverford College), Richard C. Gerkin (Arizona State University / Osmo Labs), Alexei Koulakov (CSHL), Joel D. Mainland (Monell / Penn) — [PMC12363845](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12363845/)
- Psychophysics: **62 odorants**, ≥7 intensity-spaced concentrations each, **434+ single-molecule datapoints**; **36 panelists** (20 female, ages 20–55; ≥15 per session); delivery by sealed Nalophan gas-sampling bags (closed system); ratings on the generalized Labeled Magnitude Scale. Mixtures: **260 mixtures** (binary, ternary, 5- and 10-component) and **7,623 ratings** — [PMC12363845](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12363845/)
- Single-molecule model: **127 Dragon physicochemical descriptors** (vapor pressure, boiling point, MW, MLogP, geometric descriptors) → **5-layer DNN, 300 units/layer, batch norm, ReLU, MSE loss**. A **10-feature simplified model** emphasizing physical-transport parameters plus descriptors most correlated with Hill-equation coefficients performs essentially identically. Mixture model: end-to-end DNN on concentration-weighted component features, compared against Competitive Binding and Primacy Coding biophysical models — [PMC12363845](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12363845/)
- Training/eval: single molecules — **10-fold CV stratified by molecular identity**; mixtures — 90/10 train–test with 10-fold CV; data augmentation by sampling fitted Hill curves at 300 points per molecule; SGD with momentum, lr 1e-3, 80 epochs for the mixture model — [PMC12363845](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12363845/)
- Metrics: single molecules **RMSE 7.1, r = 0.87** on held-out data (shuffled control RMSE 20.4, p < 0.001); simplified 10-feature model RMSE 7.2, r = 0.87. Mixtures: DNN **RMSE 7.5**; Primacy model r = 0.85 / RMSE 8.6; shuffled control RMSE 15.8. Real-world transfer: single-molecule predictions vs GC-O ratings **r = 0.62** (p < 0.001); mixture DNN predicting omission-test outcomes **r = 0.55** (p < 0.001) — [PMC12363845](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12363845/)
- Code/data: analysis files referenced in supplementary materials; **no repository link and no compute disclosure** in the version I read — [PMC12363845](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12363845/)
- **Group is very much active and publicly defending the paradigm**: Mainland/Pellegrino published "Response to Doty 2025, predicting odor from structure is useful" in *Chemical Senses* (DOI 10.1093/chemse/bjag003) and there is a further "Response to Comments of Pellegrino et al." in the record — an ongoing 2025–2026 methodological dispute about whether structure→odor prediction is useful — [Chemical Senses](https://academic.oup.com/chemse/article-abstract/doi/10.1093/chemse/bjag003/8489487) / [PubMed 41709413](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41709413/)

**Peter Fichtelmann & Julia Westermayr 2026 — Wilhelm-Ostwald Institute of Physical and Theoretical Chemistry, Leipzig University, Germany**
- Paper: "Machine learning for smell: ordinal odor strength prediction of molecular perfumery components", *RSC Advances* 16(28):25696–25704, 2026, DOI 10.1039/D6RA01805J, PMCID PMC13182554, PubMed 42158754; preprint arXiv:2512.08683 — [PMC13182554](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13182554/) / [RSC](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2026/ra/d6ra01805j) / [arXiv:2512.08683](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.08683)
- Task: **4-class ordinal odor strength** — odorless / low / medium / high. Dataset: **2,393 molecules** (1,678 from The Good Scents Company, 715 from PubChem); low class ≈ 12%, medium is the majority; inter-annotator agreement Cohen's κ = 0.83, Krippendorff's α = 0.81 — [PMC13182554](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13182554/)
- Representations (seven): 217 RDKit descriptors; Morgan, MACCS, RDKit, topological-torsion and atom-pair fingerprints; ChemBERTa-2 (pretrained on 77M SMILES); ChemProp MPNN; CheMeleon (pretrained ChemProp) — [PMC13182554](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13182554/)
- Models: Logistic Regression, Random Forest, XGBoost, MLP, and **CORAL** (consistent rank logits, an ordinal-regression architecture). Hyperparameters via **Optuna, 100 trials, TPE sampler with a 25th-percentile pruner**; values in Tables S5–S14 — [PMC13182554](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13182554/)
- Split: 80/20 **stratified random** split plus 10-fold CV × 10 repetitions; **molecules with ≥80% Tanimoto similarity forced into the same fold to prevent leakage** (a similarity-aware, though not scaffold-based, protocol) — [PMC13182554](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13182554/)
- Metrics: primary metric **macro MSE** (appropriate for ordinal targets), plus micro MSE, macro/micro F1, ROC AUC and confusion matrices; **72% overall correct classification**. The best configuration was an ensemble of MLP + Random Forest + XGBoost on RDKit descriptors. SHAP and dimensionality reduction identified **molecular shape, size and polarity** as primary drivers — [PMC13182554](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13182554/) / [RSC](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2026/ra/d6ra01805j)
- Code/data: https://github.com/peter-fichtelmann/odor-strength ; Zenodo DOI 10.5281/zenodo.17660448 (v1.0.7); **license: Creative Commons Attribution 3.0 Unported**; includes web scraping, cleaning, training, SHAP and an interactive prediction notebook. Compute: **Leipzig University Computing Center resources; no hardware model or wall-clock time disclosed** — [PMC13182554](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13182554/)
- Westermayr's group is active and broader than olfaction (she is also indexed as an active author across MDPI chemistry venues) — [author profile](https://sciprofiles.com/profile/4220435)

**POI-3DGCN, 2022 (Chinese group; institution *uncertain* from sources reachable to me)**
- Paper: "POI-3DGCN: Predicting odor intensity of monomer flavors based on three-dimensionally embedded graph convolutional network", *Expert Systems with Applications*, 2022 — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S095741742200416X)
- Framing: the paper explicitly equates "odor intensity activity of monomer flavors" with the **odor threshold** (lowest perceivable concentration), i.e. it is an ODT-regression paper using a 3D GCN. **VIANA 2026 — Luana Queiroz, Ícaro Bernardes, Ana M. Ribeiro, Bernardo Aguilera-Mercado, Idelfonso B. R. Nogueira (NTNU/Porto line, continuing the Oliveira 2023 work)**
- Paper: "VIANA: character Value-enhanced Intensity Assessment via domain-informed Neural Architecture", arXiv:2604.01365; also on SSRN (abstract 6545771) and in a ScienceDirect journal (S2666821126002243, which blocked my fetch with HTTP 403) — [arXiv:2604.01365](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.01365) / [SSRN 6545771](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6545771)
- Architecture: a "tri-pillar" fusion of (1) molecular structure via **GCN**, (2) semantic odor character via **Principal Odor Map (POM) embeddings**, and (3) **Hill's law as an architectural inductive bias** — a sigmoidal dose–response function enforcing physicochemical plausibility across a wide concentration range — [arXiv:2604.01365](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.01365)
- Reported: **peak R² = 0.996 and test MSE = 0.19**, described as significantly outperforming baseline structural models — **treat with strong caution**: an R² of 0.996 on perceived odor intensity is far outside what any olfactory psychophysics dataset supports, the two numbers are mutually hard to reconcile, and I could not reach the paper's split protocol or dataset description. **Uncertain** — [search summary of arXiv:2604.01365 / ScienceDirect](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.01365)
- The same lab has a related ChemRxiv preprint, "Advancing Odor Classification Models Enhanced by Scientific Machine Learning and Mechanistic Model: Probabilistic Weight Assignment for Odor Intensity Prediction and Uncertainty Analysis for Robust Fragrance Classification" (2023) — [ChemRxiv](https://chemrxiv.org/doi/full/10.26434/chemrxiv-2023-mtfrc)

**Industrial dose–response precedent (not academic, but the methodological reference point)**
- "Method for Predicting Odor Intensity of Perfumery Raw Materials Using Dose–Response Curve Database", *Industrial & Engineering Chemistry Research*, 2019 — [ACS](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acs.iecr.9b01225)

### Inferences
- Monell/CSHL's r ≈ 0.87 is achieved on only 62 molecules but with **controlled physical stimulus delivery and per-molecule stratified CV**, and it degrades gracefully to r ≈ 0.62 on real GC-O data — this honest generalization number is arguably the most trustworthy potency result in the literature.
- The fact that Pellegrino's 10-feature transport-descriptor model matches the 127-feature model is the same conclusion Mayhew 2022 reached for odorous-vs-odorless (see Q3): **physical transport into the mucus/receptor dominates potency**, and high-capacity models are not buying much.
- VIANA's R² = 0.996 and the Li et al. R² = 0.94 are the two results a report writer should hedge hardest on.

## Q3. Odorous-vs-odorless and perception models that include potency: Mainland/Monell, Abraham, receptor→perception, Sobel lab, Lötsch/Hummel, LSB@TUM

### Takeaway
"Odorous vs odorless" is essentially a solved and interpretable problem (Mayhew/Mainland 2022: volatility + hydrophobicity; ROC AUC ~0.97 in follow-up work), and the frontier has moved to receptor-mediated models (MolOR, Cell Systems 2026) and multimodal foundation models (NOSE 2026) that treat threshold and intensity as benchmark tasks rather than standalone goals.

### Cited Findings

**Emily J. Mayhew, Joel D. Mainland et al. 2022 — Monell Chemical Senses Center (Mayhew is now at Michigan State University; *uncertain*), with Arizona State University co-authorship**
- Paper: "Transport features predict if a molecule is odorous", *PNAS* 119(15), 4 April 2022, DOI 10.1073/pnas.2116576119 — [PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2116576119) / [PubMed 35377807](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35377807/) / [ASU record](https://asu.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/transport-features-predict-if-a-molecule-is-odorous/)
- Dataset: **>1,900 molecules**, chemically diverse, labelled odorous (84%) / odorless (16%) — a strongly imbalanced binary potency-adjacent task — [PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2116576119)
- Mechanistic framing (four criteria: volatile enough to reach air; non-volatile/hydrophilic enough to sorb into the mucus; hydrophobic enough to enter a receptor pocket; activate ≥1 receptor) and the central finding that a **simple, interpretable model using only the first three transport criteria** reliably classifies odorous vs odorless — i.e. reaching the receptor, not receptor activation, is the dominant barrier — [PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2116576119)
- Code: the Mainland lab's public repo is https://github.com/joelmainland/odor-prediction — [GitHub](https://github.com/joelmainland/odor-prediction)
- Follow-on work on the same task: a November 2024 ChemRxiv study on odor classification with imbalanced-data techniques reports a logistic-regression **ROC AUC of 0.9701** for odorous vs odorless on a curated GoodScents+Leffingwell set of **4,983 molecules including odorless entries** — [ChemRxiv PDF](https://chemrxiv.org/engage/api-gateway/chemrxiv/assets/orp/resource/item/6726033df9980725cfdb7843/original/odor-classification-exploring-feature-performance-and-imbalanced-data-learning-techniques.pdf)
- Related Monell-adjacent work: "Drawing the Borders of Olfactory Space" (bioRxiv 2020) — [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2020.12.04.412254v2.full)
- The 2026 general-purpose foundation-model paper by **Yikun Han, Yi Wang, Neil Mankodi, Stephen Yang, Ambuj Tewari** (arXiv:2608.25893, 26 August 2026) explicitly includes **odorous-versus-odorless classification** as one of four downstream transfer settings, alongside cross-dataset descriptor prediction, enantiomer evaluation and mixture discriminability. They fine-tune **Uni-Mol2** on the GS-LF benchmark and report matching or exceeding the state-of-the-art olfaction-specific baseline; 3D representations distinguish stereoisomers better than 2D, but stereochemical perception remains open. **No odor-threshold or intensity task in this paper** — [arXiv:2608.25893](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25893)

**Abraham's ODT dataset is the de facto benchmark for potency in ML papers**
- The NOSE benchmark uses "Odor Detection Threshold (Abraham et al., 2012)" with **268 samples**, log(1/ODT) spanning −2.78 to 6.11, scored by Pearson correlation — [arXiv:2604.10452v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.10452v2)
- Michael H. Abraham (UCL) and J. Enrique Cometto-Muñiz (UCSD) are the linear-free-energy-relationship lineage behind this data; I did not verify a specific 2018–2026 paper from them, so their current activity is a **gap**.

**NOSE 2026 — Yanyi Su & Jun Cheng (Xiamen University, State Key Laboratory of Physical Chemistry of Solid Surface; AI4EC Lab, IKKEM) with Hongshuai Wang & Zhifeng Gao (DP Technology)**
- Paper: "NOSE: Neural Olfactory-Semantic Embedding with Tri-Modal Orthogonal Contrastive Learning", arXiv:2604.10452v2 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.10452v2)
- Architecture: molecules as the hub modality connecting **receptor sequences** and **odor descriptions**, kept orthogonal. Frozen encoders — **Uni-Mol** (3D-aware) for molecules, **ESM-2 650M** for receptor sequences (trainable projection), **Qwen3 Embedding 8B with LoRA** for odor text. Hard orthogonalization by Gram–Schmidt projection plus a soft orthogonality loss; symmetric bidirectional InfoNCE with soft weighting for weak positives (1.0 / 0.5 / 0); ResMLP Pre-LN adapters (12-layer inverted bottleneck for descriptions, high-dropout bottleneck for receptors) — [arXiv:2604.10452v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.10452v2)
- Pretraining data: **3,877 receptor–molecule activation pairs** (Pred-O3, OlfactionBase, M2OR, literature) and **88,512 cleaned description–molecule pairs** covering **9,513 unique molecules** from 11 databases (Arctander, Good Scents, OlfactionBase, Flavornet, FlavorDB, AromaDb, IFRA, Sigma-Aldrich, …) out of ~140,000 raw entries, LLM-augmented with DeepSeek to **2,567,558 weak-positive pairs** — [arXiv:2604.10452v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.10452v2)
- Potency-relevant downstream tasks and results (Pearson r, mean ± sd over seeds 42–44): **odor detection threshold (Abraham, 268 molecules) — NOSE 0.84 ± 0.03**, vs Uni-Mol 0.78 ± 0.05, GIN 0.72 ± 0.02, Morgan 0.60 ± 0.18. **Intensity** — Keller & Vosshall (474 molecules, 0–100): NOSE 0.42 vs ChemBERTa 0.39, Uni-Mol 0.27; **Sagar et al. (160 molecules, −1..1): NOSE 0.47** vs ChemBERTa 0.45, Uni-Mol 0.37; **Ravia et al. (248 molecules, 0–100): NOSE 0.49** vs ChemBERTa 0.47, Uni-Mol 0.31. Also GS-LF descriptor classification (4,814 molecules, 138 descriptors) and mixture perception (Ma et al. 2021, 6,660 observations from 222 binary mixtures) — [arXiv:2604.10452v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.10452v2)
- A separate search summary of NOSE reports the threshold task as **R² 0.652, MAE 0.711, Pearson 0.836**, beating GCN, AttentiveFP, GIN, Morgan, POM, Uni-Mol and ChemBERTa — consistent with the 0.84 Pearson above — [search summary](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.10452)
- Split: **random with fixed seed 42**; three runs (seeds 42–44); pretraining and evaluation sets share molecules but never task-specific labels. **Not scaffold-split.** — [arXiv:2604.10452v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.10452v2)
- Code/data: https://github.com/Xianyusyy/NOSE . Licenses: MIT for Uni-Mol, ESM-2, POM, ChemBERTa and Pyrfume; Apache 2.0 for Qwen3 and M2OR; **Pred-O3 is CC BY-NC 4.0, academic research only** — [arXiv:2604.10452v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.10452v2)
- **Compute (the most complete disclosure I found anywhere in this literature):** pretraining on a **single NVIDIA A800-80G with bfloat16, ~10 hours**; downstream tasks on A100-80G / A800-80G / RTX 4090D, **2–3 minutes per run**, with a full hyperparameter sweep of **480 configurations × 3 seeds = 1–3 days per task**. Batch sizes 1,536 (descriptor pairs) and 128 (receptor pairs); lr 1e-4 with 20-epoch warm-up; temperature 0.07; λ_orth 2.0, λ_mol-desc 2.0, λ_mol-rec 1.0; early-stopping patience 10 — [arXiv:2604.10452v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.10452v2)

**Receptor → perception deep learning, Cell Systems 2026 (MolOR)**
- Paper: "Mapping the combinatorial coding between olfactory receptors and perception with deep learning", *Cell Systems*, 2026 (article S2405-4712(26)00193-6) — [Cell Systems](https://www.cell.com/cell-systems/fulltext/S2405-4712(26)00193-6) (my direct fetch was refused with HTTP 403)
- Method (**from the search summary — uncertain**): **MolOR** maps odorants to olfactory-receptor activation profiles using **cross-attention between a molecular GNN and protein-language-model embeddings of receptors**, then improves percept prediction by using the predicted OR profiles as auxiliary features — [search summary of Cell Systems record](https://www.cell.com/cell-systems/fulltext/S2405-4712(26)00193-6?rss=yes)
- Matsunami (Duke) is not lead author on that paper but is an author on related 2026 work using **AlphaFold3-predicted receptor structures with deep learning** to predict OR–ligand interactions — the "Decoding Smell from Receptor Structure" bioRxiv preprint — **author attribution uncertain** — [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.04.22.720159v1)
- Reference point for this line: the Osmo/Google "principal odor map" paper, *Science* 2023 — [Science](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.ade4401)

**Noam Sobel's lab (Weizmann Institute — Kobi Snitz, Aharon Ravia)**
- Their flagship result is **perceptual similarity/distance**, not potency: perceptual similarity estimates for **49,788 odorant pairs** from **199 participants** smelling **242 multicomponent odorants**, used to refine a structure→perception model that predicted new 14- and 100-blend sets; published in *Nature* (2020) — [Weizmann Wonder Wander](https://wis-wander.weizmann.ac.il/life-sciences/measure-smell) / [Weizmann Pure record](https://weizmann.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/a-measure-of-smell-enables-the-creation-of-olfactory-metamers) / [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2020-11-year-old-digital-aromas.html)

**Jörn Lötsch (Goethe University Frankfurt) & Thomas Hummel (TU Dresden) — human-threshold psychophysics with ML, not molecular potency**
- Their ML work is on **olfactory subtest patterns in patients**, not structure→potency. Unsupervised ML applied to Sniffin' Sticks subtest results in **10,714 subjects across nine olfactory pathologies**, with results pointing to the particular importance of the **olfactory threshold** subtest as a distinct component — [Scientific Reports srep35688](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep35688) / [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2451830118300712)

### Inferences
- The NOSE numbers give the cleanest cross-method comparison available for potency: on the Abraham ODT benchmark the ceiling is Pearson ≈ 0.84 (R² ≈ 0.65) with a 2026 tri-modal foundation model, while on human-rated **intensity** the ceiling is Pearson ≈ 0.42–0.49. That gap — threshold is learnable from structure, perceived intensity much less so — is the central quantitative story.
- The Abraham-268 R² ≈ 0.65 sits almost exactly where Toropov's 2018 CORAL model sat (r² 0.61–0.65) on a different ~900-compound set, and where Sun/Jia/Xi report (R² ≥ 0.65). A report writer should read the 0.83–0.94 cluster as "random split, curated matrix-homogeneous data" and the 0.61–0.65 cluster as "harder or held-out conditions."
- LSB@TUM and Sobel are best characterized as **data providers and adjacent-problem labs**, not ODT-modelling groups.

## Q4. DREAM challenges: 2017 intensity sub-challenge and the 2024/2025 Olfactory Mixtures Prediction Challenge

### Takeaway
The 2017 challenge established random forests on Dragon descriptors as the intensity baseline (winner: Yuanfang Guan's lab at Michigan); the 2024/2025 mixtures challenge ended in a four-way tie whose winners almost all built on **Principal Odor Map embeddings + gradient boosting**, and the organizers' ensemble beat every individual entry (median RMSE 0.08, Pearson 0.57). Mixtures target perceptual *distance*, not potency, but the architectural convergence on POM-embeddings-plus-boosting is the field's current default recipe.

### Cited Findings

**DREAM Olfaction Prediction Challenge 2017 (data: Keller & Vosshall, Rockefeller — 49 individuals rating 476 molecules)**
- 22 teams competed on the Keller/Vosshall psychophysical dataset (49 subjects, 476 structurally and perceptually diverse molecules); 21 perceptual attributes predicted including **intensity**, from **4,884 Dragon descriptors** — [GigaScience gix127](https://academic.oup.com/gigascience/article/7/2/gix127/4750780) / [dream-olfaction.github.io](https://dream-olfaction.github.io/)
- **Winner of sub-challenge 1 (individual responses), 2nd in sub-challenge 2 (population): Hongyang Li, Bharat Panwar, Gilbert S. Omenn, Yuanfang Guan — Dept. of Computational Medicine and Bioinformatics, University of Michigan** — [GigaScience gix127](https://academic.oup.com/gigascience/article/7/2/gix127/4750780)
- Their model: **random forest with 100 decision trees**; training on 407 molecules (338 train + 69 leaderboard), test on 69 held-out molecules; individual and population ratings blended as y = α·y_individual + (1−α)·y_population with **α = 0.2**; plus **11,786 binary "name-based" features** built from 4-letter sliding windows over molecule names. Evaluation metric: Pearson correlation. They report that the **top 5 features perform nearly identically to all 4,884**, and that random forest beat linear regression, ridge regression and SVM for intensity and the other attributes — [GigaScience gix127](https://academic.oup.com/gigascience/article/7/2/gix127/4750780)
- Code: https://github.com/Hongyang449/olfaction_prediction_manuscript , with data/code snapshots archived in GigaDB — [GigaScience gix127](https://academic.oup.com/gigascience/article/7/2/gix127/4750780)
- A secondary write-up confirms the winning DREAM algorithm was a random forest, chosen for large feature spaces and high inter-individual rating variability — [Medium/TDS](https://medium.com/data-science/smells-like-machine-learning-progress-611a2851acec); the organizers' own paper is Keller et al., "Predicting human olfactory perception from chemical features of odor molecules" (*Science* 2017), preprint at [bioRxiv 082495](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/082495.full.pdf)

**DREAM Olfactory Mixtures Prediction Challenge 2024/2025 — organizers Vahid Satarifard (Yale) and Pablo Meyer (IBM Research), with a consortium spanning Yale, University of Michigan, Boston University, KU Leuven and The Rockefeller University**
- Community paper: "High-Fidelity Tuning of Olfactory Mixture Distances in the Perceptual Space of Smell Through a Community Effort", bioRxiv 10.64898/2025.12.13.694160 — [bioRxiv full text](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2025.12.13.694160v1.full)
- Scale: **26 teams** over three months, minimizing prediction error on a hidden test set of **46 mixture pairs**; training data **507 mixture pairs** built from **168 unique mono-molecules** and **731 unique mixtures** drawn from three published studies and standardized to a 0–1 perceptual-distance scale — [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2025.12.13.694160v1.full) / [Synapse syn53470621](https://www.synapse.org/Synapse:syn53470621)
- **Four-way tie for first place**, with these architectures/features (SHAP importances as reported): **D2Smell** — pair model labels as primary features (mean |SHAP| 0.11 ± 0.019); **UMich CASI** — 138 semantic labels from the Principal Odor Map, top contributors "Fruity", "Green & Herbal", "Nutty, Fatty & Roasted"; **ChemSenSim Lab** — full POM embeddings + olfactory receptor responses + experiment type (POM embedding importance 0.06 ± 0.014); **belfaction** — 30 principal components of the POM embedding plus statistical descriptors, with absolute-difference features most important (0.10 ± 0.004) — [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2025.12.13.694160v1.full)
- Additional ensemble members: **PL21** (Dragon descriptors, 0.081 ± 0.040) and **Tamarin** (Deep-nose + Mordred features, 0.06 ± 0.042) — [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2025.12.13.694160v1.full)
- Metrics: the six top teams' ensemble reached **median RMSE 0.08 and median Pearson 0.57** on the 46-pair hidden test set, outperforming every individual submission; top individual teams were **below RMSE 0.1**. Versus prior state of the art the ensemble **reduced RMSE by 0.035 ± 0.004 and raised Pearson by 0.22 ± 0.05** on average. On an independent 50-pair validation set the ensemble scored **RMSE 0.13** with correlation comparable to the POM baseline — [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2025.12.13.694160v1.full)
- Code/data: https://github.com/Satarifard/DREAM-olfactory-mixtures-prediction-challenge (all datasets, models, evaluation scripts, figures) — [GitHub](https://github.com/Satarifard/DREAM-olfactory-mixtures-prediction-challenge); challenge infrastructure and scoring at [Sage-Bionetworks-Challenges/olfactory-mixtures-prediction](https://github.com/Sage-Bionetworks-Challenges/olfactory-mixtures-prediction)
- The **University of Michigan team led by Ambuj Tewari** publicly confirms its 4-way tie for first, with a three-step method: pretrained single-molecule embeddings as upstream information → **exponential transformation to derive the mixture embedding** → a **boosting method** to learn perceptual distance — [U-M Statistics news](https://lsa.umich.edu/stats/news-events/all-news/search-news/olfactory-mixtures-prediction-challenge-winners.html)
- **KU Leuven (Itec) also claims first place** in the challenge — consistent with the four-way tie — [KU Leuven Itec](https://itec.kuleuven-kulak.be/first-place-in-the-dream-olfactory-mixtures-prediction-challenge/)
- Earlier related architecture from the same ecosystem: blend-level embeddings from a **GIN GNN fed into XGBoost** produced competition-winning ratings prediction, published as "Deep Learning for Odor Prediction on Aroma-Chemical Blends", *ACS Omega* — [ACS Omega](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acsomega.4c07078)
- **Tewari's group is still active on olfaction as of August 2026** (the Uni-Mol2 foundation-model transfer paper, arXiv:2608.25893) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25893)

### Inferences
- Guan's 2017 finding that 5 features match 4,884, Mayhew's 2022 three-transport-criteria model, and Pellegrino's 2025 10-feature model all point the same way: potency-adjacent olfactory targets are low-dimensional, and the returns on representation learning are mostly in *descriptor* tasks, not potency.
- The 2024/25 mixtures challenge shows the field's default 2026 recipe: a pretrained olfactory embedding (POM, Uni-Mol, GIN) + gradient boosting on engineered pair/difference features. That recipe has not yet been systematically applied to ODT with a scaffold split — a visible open gap.

### Gaps
- I did not find per-team RMSE/Pearson values for the four first-place mixtures teams individually (only "below 0.1" and the ensemble figures), nor any compute disclosures for challenge participants.
- The 2017 challenge's *intensity-specific* winning correlation is not isolated in what I read: the GigaScience paper reports Pearson correlation as the metric over 21 attributes, and I did not obtain the intensity-only number. Whether a different team won on intensity alone is unresolved.

### Inferences
- **The single most defensible claim about "best potency accuracy":** nobody has yet reported a strong ODT result on a strict scaffold/out-of-distribution split. The only strict-scaffold ODT numbers in the literature (UCL/Metanovas) are reported in normalized MSE, not R², and are roughly 3× worse than the vapor-pressure task on the identical backbone — so the 0.83–0.94 R² cluster should be read as within-distribution performance.
- **Split terminology matters and differs:** "scaffold-aware greedy split" (Li), "Tanimoto ≥80% same-fold" (Westermayr), "molecule-stratified CV" (Pellegrino) and "Bemis–Murcko 80/10/10 × 3 partitions" (Wu) are four different strictness levels, only the last of which is the standard MoleculeNet-style protocol.
- **Cross-matrix comparison is invalid:** water ODTs (Hou, Li, Huang), air ODTs (Toropov, Sun/Jia/Xi, and 598 of Wu's 1,042), and perceived-intensity scales (Monell, NOSE benchmarks) are three separate problems with different intrinsic noise floors; the Yang 2026 review documents **11 distinct matrices** across the threshold literature — [PMC13420774](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13420774/)
- **Reproducibility leaders:** Westermayr (GitHub + Zenodo + CC-BY-3.0 + interactive notebook) and NOSE (GitHub + per-dependency licensing + explicit GPU hours) set the bar; Pellegrino/Mainland and Wu/Wang/Yu do not provide repository links in the versions I read.

### Gaps
- No group in this survey reports a **cost or carbon** figure. Only two disclose compute at all: Wu/Wang/Yu ("1:10–1:40 per scaffold split, single GPU", GPU model unstated) and NOSE (single A800-80G, ~10 h pretraining; A100/A800/4090D downstream, 2–3 min per run, 1–3 days per full sweep). All classical-ML ODT papers (Hou, Li, Toropov) disclose nothing beyond library versions.
- I could not resolve whether Li et al.'s R² = 0.94 survives a stricter split; their "greedy" scaffold assignment is not described in enough detail in what I could read to judge leakage.

## Overall gaps and cautions for the report writer

- **Unverified items from the assignment:** "Hassan 2025 (874 VOCs RF)" — no author/venue confirmed; "Cheng 2024 (ridge)" — not found; "Current Biology 2025 human OR screen" — not found; "Smellability" as a named model/tool — not found (the odorous/odorless literature exists, but under other names).

- **Two metrics in this survey are implausible on their face and should be hedged or omitted:** VIANA's R² = 0.996 for odor intensity, and (to a lesser degree) Li et al.'s R² = 0.94 / RMSE 0.44 for ODT, which is tighter than the reported reproducibility of threshold measurement itself.
- **Compute reporting is nearly absent across the field.** Only NOSE and Wu/Wang/Yu disclose anything; no group reports dollar cost, energy, or emissions.
