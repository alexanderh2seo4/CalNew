# Receptor-Level and Structure-Based Approaches as an Intermediate Stage (molecule → OR activation → perception/potency)

Research current as of 2026-09-27. Items marked **[uncertain]** could not be verified in a primary source within this research pass.

## OR structures, AlphaFold models, pharmacophores, docking and MD

### Takeaway
Experimental OR structures exist but are extremely sparse (~5 unique receptors, mostly *consensus* sequences, plus insect ORs), while AlphaFold2/3 gives usable transmembrane-bundle models for essentially the whole family; the structure-based literature supports pocket-level pharmacophores and modest-hit-rate virtual screening, not quantitative affinity/potency prediction.

### Cited Findings
- **OR51E2 (human), the first odorant receptor structure**: cryo-EM of active OR51E2 bound to propionate in complex with heterotrimeric Gs, 3.1 Å map; propionate sits in an *occluded* pocket bounded by TM3, TM4, TM5, TM6; mutating the pocket shifts the fatty-acid chain-length recognition spectrum, i.e. selectivity comes from tight packing rather than a single polar anchor — Billesbølle et al., *Nature* 2023 — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-05798-y), [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10580732/); coordinates PDB **8F76** (OR51E2 + propionate + miniGs399, 3.10 Å) — [RCSB 8F76](https://www.rcsb.org/structure/8F76)
- **OR52 family**: "Understanding the molecular mechanisms of odorant binding and activation of the human OR52 family", Choi et al., *Nature Communications* 2023; structures include **8HTI** (consOR52 + octanoic acid, 2.97 Å) and apo states **8J46**, **8W77** — [Nat Commun](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-023-43983-9); PDB IDs/resolutions as tabulated in — [Chemical Senses review (Nicoli et al. 2025)](https://academic.oup.com/chemse/article/doi/10.1093/chemse/bjaf038/8306567)
- **Consensus-OR structures (de March et al. 2024)**: consOR51 apo (**8UXV**, 3.20 Å), consOR1 + L-menthol (**8UXY**, 3.30 Å), consOR2 + S-carvone (**8UY0**, 3.20 Å), consOR4 + 2-methylthiazoline (**8UYQ**, 3.50 Å) — [Chemical Senses review](https://academic.oup.com/chemse/article/doi/10.1093/chemse/bjaf038/8306567)
- **Coverage is the bottleneck**: the 2025 structural-bioinformatics review counts **8 experimentally resolved OR structures covering only 5 unique ORs, 4 of which are consensus (not native) sequences**, against ~391 functional human ORs; it also states only ~12% of human ORs have been experimentally deorphanized — [Nicoli et al., *Chemical Senses* 2025](https://academic.oup.com/chemse/article/doi/10.1093/chemse/bjaf038/8306567)
- **A 2026 human OR structure with a covalent mechanism**: "Structural decoding of reversible covalent linkage of odorants in human olfactory receptor OR6A2", *Cell* (2026 issue, S0092-8674(25)01430-8) — relevant because a covalent/Schiff-base mechanism for aldehyde odorants breaks the assumption that docking scores approximate equilibrium binding — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0092867425014308) **[uncertain: full text not retrieved; authors/metrics unverified]**
- **Insect ORs (structurally unrelated to vertebrate ORs — ion channels, not GPCRs)**: Orco cryo-EM (homotetramer) — Butterwick et al., *Nature* 2018 — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-018-0420-8); MhOR5 from *Machilis hrabei* solved apo, + eugenol, + DEET at 3.3 Å, homotetramer, ~18% sequence identity to Orco yet near-identical fold; MhOR5 responds to tens of chemically diverse compounds (a genuinely broadly tuned receptor) — Del Mármol, Yedlin, Ruta, *Nature* 2021 — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-03794-8)
- **AlphaFold2 quality for ORs**: AF2 gives "accurate structural models for virtually all members of the OR family", with high confidence in TM helices but **medium-to-low confidence in extracellular loops**; ECL2 in particular "could not be properly modelled, hampering the use of advanced MD simulations". Useful models generally require conformational sampling/refinement (induced-fit docking, MD) rather than direct use of the raw prediction — [Nicoli et al., *Chemical Senses* 2025](https://academic.oup.com/chemse/article/doi/10.1093/chemse/bjaf038/8306567)
- **AF3 accuracy benchmarked against cryo-EM**: in the Matsunami-lab structure→function work, AlphaFold3 OR–miniGαs complex predictions (5 seeds per receptor) reproduced the OR51E2 cryo-EM structure to **<1 Å backbone RMSD** — [Lu, Vihani, … Matsunami 2026, Research Square preprint](https://www.researchsquare.com/article/rs-9947738/v1)
- **Inactive-state modelling needs ML/MD, not AF alone**: "Machine Learning-Based Modeling of Olfactory Receptors in Their Inactive State: Human OR51E2 as a Case Study" — AF2 models plus ML/MD used to generate inactive conformations — [J Chem Inf Model 2023 / PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10207261/)
- **Pocket pharmacophore derived by modelling + mutagenesis (Leibniz-LSB@TUM / Di Pizio group)**: OR5K1 (pyrazine receptor) orthosteric site mapped by three rounds of induced-fit docking on both a homology model and an AF2 model; predicted contacts L104^3.32 and L255^6.51 (conserved in 98% / 96% of OR5K1 orthologs across 51 species) were confirmed by alanine mutagenesis — Nicoli, Haag, Marcinek, He, Kreißl, Stein, Di Pizio, *J Chem Inf Model* 63(7):2014, 2023 — [ACS](https://pubs.acs.org/jcisd8/article/63/7/2014/850362/Modeling-the-Orthosteric-Binding-Site-of-the-G), [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2022.06.01.494157v1.full)
- **Model-derived pockets agree with the experimental ones**: the review's key validation statement is that "binding site residues identified in the modeling work … overlap with the orthosteric binding site identified by the experimental structures" — [Nicoli et al. 2025](https://academic.oup.com/chemse/article/doi/10.1093/chemse/bjaf038/8306567)
- **Docking hit rates actually achieved on ORs/TAARs** (all prospective, all binary hit/no-hit): OR51E2 screen of >2,500 metabolites with combined scoring functions → 24 agonists + 1 antagonist (Abaffy et al. 2018); TAAR5 screen of 18,969 filtered compounds → 29 tested, 3 novel antagonists (**~10% hit rate**, Nicoli et al. 2023b); OR5K1 iterative induced-fit docking validated by mutagenesis — [Nicoli et al. 2025](https://academic.oup.com/chemse/article/doi/10.1093/chemse/bjaf038/8306567)
- **MD/dynamics reviews**: "Deciphering olfactory receptor binding mechanisms: a structural and dynamic perspective on olfactory receptors" — [Front Mol Biosci 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/molecular-biosciences/articles/10.3389/fmolb.2024.1498796/full); "Computational modelling of olfactory receptors" — [ScienceDirect 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0304416525000704); a mechanism-level MD case study: OR9Q2 activation by 4-methylphenol — [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12609840/)

### Inferences
- The pocket is *occluded* and selectivity is packing-driven (OR51E2), so a **shape/volume-plus-hydrophobic-complementarity pharmacophore** is the physically motivated abstraction for ORs — closer to a steric-fit filter than to the H-bond-donor/acceptor pharmacophores typical of drug targets. This is a good fit for a pharmacophore stage in a potency pipeline, but it also means docking *scoring functions* (tuned on nanomolar drug–protein complexes) are being applied far outside their calibration range.
- Because 4 of 5 solved receptors are consensus sequences, any structure-derived pharmacophore is currently *subfamily-level* (class I acids, OR52, OR51) rather than receptor-specific. Receptor-specific pockets must come from AF2/AF3 + induced-fit refinement.
- AF3's sub-1 Å agreement on OR51E2 is one data point on the one receptor whose structure was in the training window; it should not be read as family-wide docking-grade accuracy. Pocket *identification* is reliable; pocket *side-chain rotamer detail*, which is what docking scores depend on, is not.

### Gaps
- No published retrospective docking benchmark for ORs (no enrichment factors, no AUC on M2OR actives vs decoys, no pose-RMSD statistics against 8F76/8HTI) was found. Comparative accuracy of AutoDock Vina vs Glide vs DiffDock on ORs specifically is **not documented** in anything located here.
- No located source reports OR docking scores correlating with measured EC50 values.
- 2024–2026 *native* human OR structures beyond OR51E2 and OR6A2: not confirmed; the count above may be behind the very latest depositions.

## ML models of OR activation: architectures and measured accuracy

### Takeaway
Receptor-activation prediction is a solved-enough *binary classification* problem (AUROC ~0.9+ reported by several groups on different data), but no located model predicts EC50/potency as a regression — the field's benchmark is "does this molecule activate this OR", which is the wrong output type for a potency pipeline without an added dose-response stage.

### Cited Findings
- **M2OR dataset (the field's reference resource)**: 75,050 bioassay experiments covering 51,395 distinct OR–molecule pairs; **3,100 responsive (agonist) pairs (6%) vs 48,295 non-responsive (94%)**; 768 unique molecules (407 non-stereoisomeric, 176 "sum of isomers", 168 specific isomers, 17 mixtures) and 1,246 unique protein sequences across 11 mammalian species (*H. sapiens* 66% of experiments, *M. musculus* 33%); dose-response measurements are prioritised over screening data when aggregating a pair — Lalis, Hladiš, Abi Khalil, Briand, Fiorucci, Topin, *Nucleic Acids Research* 52(D1):D1370, 2024 — [NAR](https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/52/D1/D1370/7327067), [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10767820/); data at [m2or.chemsensim.fr](https://m2or.chemsensim.fr/), code/data [GitHub chemosim-lab/M2OR](https://github.com/chemosim-lab/M2OR) and Zenodo doi:10.5281/zenodo.8385484. Note: the NAR article itself is CC-BY; the repo license should be checked separately (**user states Apache-2.0 for the repo — plausible but not verified here**).
- **Hladiš et al. (ChemoSim / Topin lab), ICLR 2023 — "Matching receptor to odorant with protein language and graph neural networks"**: combines a **protBERT [CLS]** receptor embedding with a molecular **graph neural network**; trained on **46,700 OR–molecule pairs**; the lab states it "outperforms state-of-the-art drug-target interaction models" and handles compound mixtures; output is **binary activation, not EC50** — [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=q9VherQJd8_), [ICLR poster](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2023/poster/11500), [code: MatejHl/Receptor2Odorant](https://github.com/MatejHl/Receptor2Odorant), [ChemSenSim ML page](https://lab.chemsensim.fr/index.php/machine-learning/)
- **MolOR (Chithrananda, Amores, Yang — Microsoft Research Cambridge / UC Berkeley), *Cell Systems* 2026 (bioRxiv 2024)**: GCN molecular encoder + **ESM-2** receptor embeddings fused by **learned cross-attention** over atom-wise × residue-wise representations; no structures required; trained on **46,700 M2OR pairs / 1,237 receptor sequences** — [bioRxiv full text](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.09.16.613334v1.full), [Cell Systems](https://www.cell.com/cell-systems/fulltext/S2405-4712(26)00193-6?rss=yes), [code: microsoft/olfaction](https://github.com/microsoft/olfaction)
- **Lu, Vihani, … Matsunami (2026) structure-based OR activation model**: AlphaFold3 OR–miniGαs models → binding-cavity residues extracted → **32×32×32 Å voxel grid at 1 Å**, each voxel annotated with reduced **ESM-2** residue features → **3D CNN**, multi-label prediction of which odorants activate each OR. Data: **349 ORs responding to ≥1 of 48 diverse odorants**, measured **in vivo in mouse by pS6-IP-Seq**, 70/15/15 split **at the receptor level**. Performance: **macro-averaged AUROC 0.959**; mean Pearson **r = 0.71** across the 48 odorants (41/48 with r > 0.6); mean **F1 = 0.67** (32/48 above 0.6). Sequence-only baseline (Grantham distance) was near-useless against perceptual space (r² = 0.007). Integrated-gradients attribution identified cavity-constricting vs cavity-expanding residues, and Or2t43 mutagenesis confirmed predictions (C5.42/C5.43 → loss of response to tert-butyl mercaptan; D4.53 → *increased* response, matching a negative attribution) — [Research Square preprint, 14 June 2026](https://www.researchsquare.com/article/rs-9947738/v1), [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.04.22.720159v1), [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13278317/), [PubMed 42326533](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42326533/)
- **DeepOlf (Gupta et al.)**: DNN on odorant molecular features/fingerprints + OR features; reported **94.83% accuracy for odorant vs non-odorant** and **99.92% accuracy for odorant–OR interaction**, outperforming SVM/RF/k-NN and the ODORactor server — [IEEE/ACM TCBB 2020](https://dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.1109/TCBB.2020.3002154), [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32750862/). **Treat the 99.92% with suspicion**: on a dataset that is ~94% negatives, accuracy is a near-vacuous metric and no AUROC/AUPRC on a held-out receptor split was found.
- **Achebouche et al. 2022, "Application of artificial intelligence to decode the relationships between smell, olfactory receptors and small molecules"**, *Scientific Reports* 12 — graph-convolutional NN linking odorants, ORs and percepts — [Sci Rep](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-022-23176-y)
- **Mol-PECO** is a *molecule → percept* model (Coulomb-matrix/positional-encoding graph model), **not** an OR-activation model — relevant as the direct-path baseline, not as the receptor stage — [arXiv 2305.12424](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.12424)
- **Insect-OR ML as a proxy that worked prospectively**: SVM screen of 3 million molecules for SlitOR25 → 11 novel agonists from 32 candidates tested (**~28–34% hit rate**), Caballero-Vidal et al., *Sci Rep* 2020 — [ChemSenSim ML page](https://lab.chemsensim.fr/index.php/machine-learning/)
- **ChemSenSim lab's own summary is explicit**: of their released tools (GNN-CLS, M2OR query/binarised combinatorial code, DREAM mixtures ensemble, SlitOR SVM), "**None explicitly report EC50/potency predictions; all focus on binary activation classification or perceptual distance modeling**" — [ChemSenSim ML page](https://lab.chemsensim.fr/index.php/machine-learning/)

### Inferences
- Two distinct model families now exist: **sequence-only (protBERT/ESM + GNN, trained on M2OR)** and **structure-informed (AF3 pocket voxels + ESM-2 CNN, trained on in vivo mouse data)**. The structure-informed one reports the higher headline number (AUROC 0.959) but on a *much easier* task shape — 48 odorants, receptor-level split, single concentration, binary labels — so the two numbers are not comparable.
- For a potency pipeline, the useful design is: use an M2OR-trained sequence×molecule model for the **full 400-OR activation vector** (cheap, covers orphans), and use structure/pharmacophore models only for the **handful of receptors that matter for the target odorant class** (where mutagenesis-validated pockets exist: OR51E2/OR52 acids, OR5K1 pyrazines, OR5A2/OR5AN1 musks).
- The severe class imbalance in M2OR (6% actives, 768 molecules only) is the binding constraint on any receptor stage. With 768 molecules, a receptor stage generalising to a fresh 5,000-molecule library is an extrapolation, not an interpolation.

### Gaps
- No AUROC/AUPRC figures for Hladiš et al. were retrievable (OpenReview PDF behind a browser check; the ICLR paper's exact numbers are therefore **unreported here**).
- No located model reports **EC50 regression RMSE** for OR activation. If the user needs pEC50 regression, it appears to be unbuilt as of now — the M2OR dose-response subset would have to be extracted and modelled directly.
- The exact number of M2OR pairs carrying EC50 values is not stated in the NAR paper's retrievable text.

## Does an OR-activation intermediate actually improve perception/potency prediction?

### Takeaway
The evidence is real but **modest and mixed in magnitude**: receptor activities predict percepts well and sometimes beat chemical features on narrow, well-matched compound sets (R² 0.687 vs 0.514), but the one large-scale test of *predicted* receptor activations as features for 152 descriptors gained only **+0.6% AUROC** over a molecule-only baseline.

### Cited Findings
- **Kowalewski & Ray, *iScience* 2020, "Predicting human olfactory perception from activities of odorant receptors"**: ML-screened ~0.5 million compounds for ligands of **34 human ORs**, extracted enriched structural motifs, and showed OR activity predicts many of **146 perceptual qualities**; the headline claim is that "biologically relevant OR activity is often superior to chemical features for modeling odor percepts", and that each percept is predictable from **very few ORs** — [PubMed 32731170](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32731170/), [eScholarship full text](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/8g1801td)
- **MolOR (Cell Systems 2026)**: coupling *predicted* receptor activations with molecular features "improves odor percept prediction across 152 perceptual descriptors"; quantitatively, using all **845 HORDE receptors** gave "**a 0.6% increase to AUROC across 5 seeds**" vs molecule-only, paired t-test **t = 5.91, p = 0.004**. Perception data: **5,862 molecules × 152 descriptors** (GoodScents + Leffingwell). The paper explicitly **does not predict odor intensity** and does not model concentration, mixtures or chirality — [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.09.16.613334v1.full)
- **Ihara et al. 2025, *Chemical Senses* (bjaf002), "Predicting human olfactory perception by odorant structure and receptor activation profile"**: receptor-based elastic-net on **6 ORs × 11 concentrations** reached **R² = 0.687 ± 0.059** on validation vs **R² = 0.514 ± 0.078** for an XGBoost model on 3D shape + pharmacophore fingerprints (941 odorants); set was 23 eugenol/vanillin-related compounds rated by 20 trained panellists. Authors attribute the receptor model's edge to the narrow odor space "that can be efficiently narrowed down to a limited number of ORs" — [Chemical Senses](https://academic.oup.com/chemse/article/doi/10.1093/chemse/bjaf002/7994243)
- **Structure-level receptor representations align with perception without seeing chemistry**: receptor-embedding distances (smeLLMap, from the AF3+CNN model) correlate with human perceptual space from OpenPOM at **r² = 0.112, p = 6.37e-31**, and kNN neighbours in smeLLMap are more perceptually similar than chance (p = 6.46e-96) — a weak but highly significant receptor→percept signal — [Lu, Vihani, … Matsunami 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13278317/)
- **Ray lab also modelled insect + human olfaction jointly**: "Machine Learning Based Modelling of Human and Insect Olfaction" — [bioRxiv 2023](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.12.25.573309v1.full.pdf)
- **Structure-based (docking) route to perception**: ~**400 human ORs modelled with AlphaFold2 via LocalColabFold on a single NVIDIA RTX 3090**, each odorant represented as a **409-dimensional docking-score vector**, with odor *similarity* inferred by statistical comparison of those vectors — i.e. a deliberately *relative* framework rather than absolute descriptor prediction — [ACS Omega 2025, 10(35):39933](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acsomega.5c04324), [ChemRxiv preprint](https://chemrxiv.org/doi/full/10.26434/chemrxiv-2025-fhthr)

### Inferences
- The pattern across these studies is consistent and important for the user's design: **a receptor stage helps most when the odor space is narrow and the relevant ORs are known and measured** (Ihara: 6 measured ORs, one chemical family, +33% relative R²), and helps **marginally when the receptor activations are themselves predicted across a broad chemical space** (MolOR: +0.6% AUROC). The intermediate is not free information — it is a *transfer* of information from receptor assays, and its value scales with how much real assay data covers the target chemistry.
- For **potency specifically**, none of these papers is a direct precedent: Kowalewski/Ray and MolOR predict descriptor presence/absence; Ihara predicts rated intensity but on 23 compounds. The receptor stage's strongest claim on potency comes from the threshold literature below, not from the ML-perception literature.
- The measured-vs-predicted asymmetry argues for a **hybrid**: use the Mainland 2015 matrix (73 odorants × 511 ORs) as *ground-truth* receptor features wherever the molecule is in it or close to it, and fall back to a predicted activation vector otherwise, with a flag distinguishing the two.

### Gaps
- No study located that predicts **odor detection threshold (potency) from a full predicted OR activation vector** and compares against a direct molecule→threshold model. This is the specific experiment the user's pipeline would be the first to run, as far as this search found.
- Sharma et al. and Chae et al. (named in the assignment) were not located as distinct, verifiable papers in this pass; "A System-Wide Understanding of the Human Olfactory Percept Chemical Space" ([PubMed 33640959](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33640959)) is from the same Ray-lab line but its receptor-vs-chemical comparison was not read.

## Combinatorial coding vs potency: does the most sensitive receptor set the threshold?

### Takeaway
Yes — in mouse the behavioural detection threshold is set by the **single most sensitive receptor**, with no summation from other receptors, and in humans single-OR genotype shifts move detection thresholds by **40–60-fold** in the direction and roughly the magnitude predicted by in vitro EC50. This is the strongest available justification for a receptor-intermediate *potency* model, and it implies a **min-over-receptors (max-sensitivity) readout**, not an average.

### Cited Findings
- **Dewan, Cichy, Zhang, Miguel, Feinstein, Rinberg, Bozza, *Nature Communications* 9:2887 (2018), "Single olfactory receptors set odor detection thresholds"** — [Nat Commun](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-018-05129-0), [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6056506/), [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/341099.full.pdf). Key numbers:
  - Deleting all TAARs (ΔT2-9) raised the phenylethylamine threshold from **5.0 × 10⁻¹² M to 9.6 × 10⁻¹¹ M (18.4-fold)** and isopentylamine from **5.9 × 10⁻¹⁰ M to 5.8 × 10⁻⁹ M (~10-fold)**.
  - Single deletions: ΔTAAR3 → isopentylamine 1.5 × 10⁻⁹ → 9.4 × 10⁻⁹ M (6.3-fold); ΔTAAR4 → phenylethylamine 5.9 × 10⁻¹² → 4.0 × 10⁻¹¹ M (6.9-fold); ΔTAAR5 → trimethylamine 2.6 × 10⁻⁸ → 8.6 × 10⁻⁸ M (4-fold).
  - Deleting the canonical OR **Olfr1440 (musk receptor)** raised the muscone threshold 2.6-fold (3.8 × 10⁻¹¹ → 9.8 × 10⁻¹¹ M).
  - **"Behavioral threshold is set solely by the most sensitive receptor, with no contribution from other highly sensitive receptors."** Deleting the *second*-most-sensitive receptor produced no behavioural change (TAAR3 for PEA; TAAR4 for isopentylamine).
  - **Glomerular EC50s from awake imaging** track the behavioural threshold: TAAR3/isopentylamine 4.1 × 10⁻¹⁰ M vs TAAR4 9.9 × 10⁻⁹ M (24-fold); TAAR4/PEA 1.4 × 10⁻¹¹ M vs TAAR3 1.9 × 10⁻¹⁰ M (8-fold); TAAR5/TMA 3.1 × 10⁻⁸ M vs TAAR4 4.7 × 10⁻⁷ M (20-fold). **Concordance between the most sensitive glomerulus and behavioural threshold was within 2–5-fold.**
  - **Receptor number does not buy sensitivity**: overexpressing TAAR4 ~7-fold (18.5 glomeruli vs the normal 2.5) did **not** lower the behavioural threshold — sensitivity is limited at the receptor, not by neuron count.
- **Human musk receptors — the best in vitro↔in vivo potency calibration available**: Emter, Merillat, Dossenbach, Natsch, *Chemical Senses* 49 (2024), "A trilogy of human musk receptors: linking receptor activation, genotype, and sensory perception" — three receptors **OR5A2, OR5AN1, OR1N2**; OR5A2 EC50s: (S)-galaxolide **0.1 µM**, serenolide 0.47 µM, ethylene brassylate 0.46 µM, thibetolide 3.02 µM; the **P172L** variant drops potency ~50–65-fold (galaxolide 6.02 µM, serenolide 30.78 µM) and homozygous P172L carriers showed **40–60-fold higher sensory detection thresholds** for selective OR5A2 ligands. Across **50 macrocyclic lactone derivatives**, group threshold vs in vitro EC20% correlated at **r² = 0.673** (individual panellists r² 0.672–0.843) with a **slope ≈ 1.0–1.35** — i.e. near-unit log-log transfer from receptor potency to perceptual potency within a chemical series — [Chemical Senses](https://academic.oup.com/chemse/article/doi/10.1093/chemse/bjae015/7642668)
- **OR5AN1 / muscone**: OR5AN1 (68% identity to mouse MOR215-1) responds to muscone with **EC50 32.1 µM** in luciferase assay (Sato-Akuhara et al., *Neuron* 2016 line of work) — [Neuron/ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0896627313009252), [J Neurosci 2016 single-receptor-deletion study](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/36/16/4482); variant EC50s for muscone: reference **11.45 µM**, L289F **9.192 µM**, G3R/L289F **12.81 µM**, and subjects homozygous for the more sensitive **L289F** allele had **lower muscone detection thresholds** and rated macrocyclic musks as more intense — [Chemical Senses bjac037 (2022)](https://academic.oup.com/chemse/article/doi/10.1093/chemse/bjac037/6980822)
- **OR7D4 / androstenone**: OR7D4 **RT/WM** subjects as a group had **higher detection thresholds** to steroidal odours and a greater incidence of specific anosmia to androstenone than RT/RT — [PMC3342276](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3342276/); functional variability across the human OR repertoire — Mainland et al., *Nature Neuroscience* 2014 "The missense of smell" — [Nat Neuro](https://www.nature.com/articles/nn.3598)
- **OR10G4 / guaiacol**: OR10G4 genotype explains **>15% of variance in perceived intensity** and **>10% of variance in perceived valence** for guaiacol — Trimmer et al., *PNAS* 2019, "Genetic variation across the human olfactory receptor repertoire alters odor perception" — [PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1804106115), [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/212431v1.full)
- **OR11H7 / isovaleric acid**: the intact allele of **OR11H7P** responds to isovaleric acid in *Xenopus* oocytes, but human hyperosmia to isovaleric acid is a complex trait with contributions beyond the receptor — Menashe et al., *PLOS Biology* 2007 — [PLOS Biol](https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pbio.0050284). The PNAS repertoire study **could not test this association** due to poor genotyping frequency, and reported more generally that **single-OR genetic variation affected intensity and pleasantness more than detection threshold**, with no OR passing its threshold-association significance bar for isovaleric acid — [PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1804106115)

### Inferences
- **A direct design consequence**: potency should be modelled as `threshold ≈ f(min over ORs of EC50_ij)` — a **soft-min / log-sum-exp over the predicted receptor sensitivity vector** — rather than a sum or mean of activations. Dewan et al. is explicit that summation across receptors does not occur, and that glomerular count does not matter.
- **Two apparently conflicting human findings are reconcilable**: Emter/Natsch get near-unit-slope EC50→threshold transfer, while Trimmer/PNAS find single-OR variation moves *intensity* more than *threshold*. The difference is that the musk work looks **within a chemical series at the known dominant receptor**, whereas the repertoire-wide GWAS-style study looks at arbitrary odorant–OR pairs where the genotyped OR may not be the most sensitive one. This supports a **per-odorant-class, receptor-targeted** potency model over a generic all-ORs regression.
- The Dewan 2–5-fold concordance sets a realistic **accuracy ceiling** for a receptor-driven threshold predictor: even with perfect knowledge of the most sensitive receptor's sensitivity, expect ~0.3–0.7 log units of irreducible error, and that is in mouse with in vivo glomerular EC50s (not heterologous-assay EC50s).

### Gaps
- No source found that systematically correlates **human OR EC50 (heterologous assay) against human odor detection thresholds across many unrelated chemical classes** — the musk series (r² 0.673, n=50 within one series) is the only quantitative calibration located.

## Bridging the µM-EC50 → ppb-threshold gap

### Takeaway
The gap is real (ORs assay in µM–mM while thresholds are ppb/ppt) and is attributed to a mix of **assay artefacts** (heterologous expression efficiency, hydrophobic cellular accumulation, no mucus phase), **air↔mucus partitioning**, and **downstream amplification/integration**; crucially, the *rank order* of EC50 still tracks thresholds, so the practical bridge is a **monotone calibration with physicochemical covariates**, not a mechanistic model.

### Cited Findings
- **The scale of the problem and a partial fix**: a median EC50 of ~**10⁻⁴ M** across published human OR activation data, with an improved high-expression assay system achieving ~**100-fold improvement to 1.6 × 10⁻⁶ M**; psychophysical measures correlated with **EC50 at r = 0.87** and with **receptor efficacy at r = −0.91** against median odor detection thresholds — reported in "Decoding human olfaction by high heterologous expression of odorant receptors detecting signature odorants", *Current Biology* 2025 — [Cell/Current Biology](https://www.cell.com/current-biology/fulltext/S0960-9822(25)01211-4). **[uncertain: full text returned HTTP 403; these values come from search-result summarisation of that paper and its surrounding literature and must be verified against the PDF before being used as calibration anchors — in particular the exact n, and whether r = 0.87 is on log-transformed values]**
- **Hydrophobic accumulation confounds in vitro potency**: the musk paper notes galaxolide's in vitro potency (0.1 µM) versus its extremely low sensory threshold (**0.14 ng**), and attributes the discrepancy to "**cellular accumulation of this highly hydrophobic molecule**" in vitro — a known toxicological artefact that changes the effective local concentration at the cell surface — [Chemical Senses bjae015](https://academic.oup.com/chemse/article/doi/10.1093/chemse/bjae015/7642668)
- **Air–mucus partitioning is a distinct, modellable term**: air–nasal-mucus partition coefficients are recognised as crucial and hard to measure in vivo; **they cannot be substituted by air–water coefficients** because hydrophobic odorants are considerably more soluble in mucus than in water — "Prediction on Air–Nasal Mucus Partition Coefficients of Odor Compounds", *ACS Omega* 9(29):31328, 2024 — [ACS Omega](https://pubs.acs.org/acsodf/article/9/29/31328/410013/Prediction-on-Air-Nasal-Mucus-Partition), [PubMed 39072110](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39072110/)
- **Sorption produces a chromatographic gradient along the epithelium** that co-varies with receptor position, i.e. the air→receptor delivery term is spatially structured, not a single scalar — "Tuning to Odor Solubility and Sorption Pattern in Olfactory Epithelial Responses", *J Neurosci* 34(6):2025, 2014 — [J Neurosci](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/34/6/2025); sniff-dependent airflow/deposition — [Chemical Senses 42:683](https://academic.oup.com/chemse/article/42/8/683/4084230)
- **Receptor number is not the amplifier**: 7-fold more TAAR4-expressing neurons gave no threshold improvement, so the amplification between receptor occupancy and percept is **not** convergence/glomerular summation — Dewan et al. 2018 — [PMC6056506](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6056506/)
- **Allosteric modulation exists and shifts apparent potency**: "Allosteric modulation of a human odorant receptor", *Current Biology* 2023 — [Cell PDF](https://www.cell.com/current-biology/pdfExtended/S0960-9822(23)00304-4); patents exist for identifying OR positive allosteric modulators — [USPTO 11921105](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11921105)
- **Deorphanisation state of the art / EC50 reporting practice**: "The state of the art of odorant receptor deorphanization: A report from the orphanage", *J Gen Physiol* 143(5):527 — [JGP](https://rupress.org/jgp/article/143/5/527/43283/The-state-of-the-art-of-odorant-receptor); "A status report on human odorant receptors and their allocated agonists", *Chemical Senses* 2024 — [Chemical Senses bjae037](https://academic.oup.com/chemse/article-abstract/doi/10.1093/chemse/bjae037/7821270)

### Inferences
- A defensible pipeline bridge is a **two-term log-linear calibration**:
  `log10(threshold_air) ≈ a·log10(EC50_min) + b·log10(K_air–mucus or a Henry/logP surrogate) + c·(assay-system indicator) + d`
  Term 1 is justified by the musk series (slope ≈ 1.0–1.35, r² 0.673) and by the reported EC50↔threshold rank correlation; term 2 by the air–mucus partition literature; term 3 because the same receptor's EC50 shifts ~100-fold between low- and high-expression assay systems, meaning **M2OR EC50 values are only comparable within an assay family** — assay type must be a covariate, and M2OR does record assay details.
- The ~100-fold assay-system effect plus the hydrophobic-accumulation artefact together mean absolute µM EC50 numbers should be treated as an **arbitrary-offset, assay-dependent scale**. Fitting an intercept per assay type (and per lab where possible) is likely to matter more than any model-architecture choice in the receptor stage.
- Since hydrophobicity enters *both* the artefact term (cellular accumulation inflating in vitro potency) and the delivery term (mucus solubility), logP will be partially confounded. Expect the calibration to be poorly identified unless fit within chemical classes or with the assay indicator included.

### Gaps
- The Current Biology 2025 correlation figures (r = 0.87 / r = −0.91) are **unverified** — the paywall blocked the primary text. These are the single most useful numbers for the bridge and should be re-checked first.
- No published end-to-end model was found that predicts an **air-phase detection threshold (ppb) from receptor EC50 plus partitioning**. The bridge appears to be an open modelling problem rather than a solved transformation.

## Feasibility and compute: docking at scale vs ML receptor models

### Takeaway
An all-pairs docking campaign (~5,000 molecules × ~400 ORs) has already been shown to be technically doable on modest hardware (one group modelled ~400 AF2 ORs on a single RTX 3090 and built 409-dimensional docking-score vectors), but there is **no published evidence that OR docking scores are quantitatively reliable**, so ML receptor models dominate on both cost and demonstrated accuracy; docking's defensible role is interpretability and pharmacophore extraction on a handful of receptors.

### Cited Findings
- **Precedent for the exact scale proposed**: ~**400 human ORs** built with AlphaFold2 via **LocalColabFold on one NVIDIA RTX 3090**, then docked against odorants to yield a **409-dimensional docking-score vector per odorant**, with odor similarity inferred by statistically comparing those vectors — the authors deliberately framed the output as *relative* (similarity) rather than absolute, *ACS Omega* 10(35):39933, Sept 2025 — [ACS Omega](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acsomega.5c04324), [ChemRxiv](https://chemrxiv.org/doi/full/10.26434/chemrxiv-2025-fhthr)
- **Docking-based OR screening hit rates, where prospectively tested**: ~10% (TAAR5, 3/29) and 25/2,500+ metabolites flagged as actives for OR51E2 — both required induced-fit docking / multi-template homology modelling and combined scoring functions, i.e. substantial per-receptor manual setup rather than a turnkey pipeline — [Nicoli et al., Chemical Senses 2025](https://academic.oup.com/chemse/article/doi/10.1093/chemse/bjaf038/8306567)
- **Model preparation, not docking, is the bottleneck for accuracy**: raw AF2 models needed "conformational sampling and refinement protocols"; ECL2 could not be modelled properly, which "hamper[ed] the use of advanced MD simulations" — [Nicoli et al. 2025](https://academic.oup.com/chemse/article/doi/10.1093/chemse/bjaf038/8306567)
- **The cheaper structure-informed alternative**: the Matsunami-lab model needs only **5 AF3 predictions per receptor once**, then extracts binding-cavity residues into a 32³ voxel grid for CNN inference — inference cost is trivial per molecule–receptor pair and reported AUROC 0.959 on 349 ORs × 48 odorants — [Research Square](https://www.researchsquare.com/article/rs-9947738/v1)
- **Pure-sequence ML needs no structures at all**: MolOR's GCN + ESM-2 cross-attention "predict[s] receptor-odorant binding **without requiring explicit structural data**", and scales to all **845 HORDE receptors** — [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.09.16.613334v1.full)
- **Insect/other-species data as proxies**: M2OR already pools **11 mammalian species** (66% human, 33% mouse experiments) in a single sequence-conditioned dataset, which is what lets protein-language models transfer across species — [NAR](https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/52/D1/D1370/7327067); the Matsunami model is trained **entirely on mouse in vivo data** yet its receptor embedding space correlates with *human* OpenPOM perceptual space (r² = 0.112, p = 6.37e-31), while the authors caution that mouse→human translation "should be interpreted with appropriate caution" — [PMC13278317](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13278317/); insect ORs are **ion channels with an unrelated fold** (Orco/MhOR5 tetramers), so they are useful as *methodological* proxies (ML screening, broad tuning) but not as structural templates for human ORs — [Nature 2021](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-03794-8), [Nature 2018](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-018-0420-8)

### Inferences
- **Cost framing (inference, not a cited benchmark)**: 5,000 molecules × 400 ORs = 2 × 10⁶ docking runs. At a typical single-CPU-core Vina run of ~10–60 s per ligand–receptor pair with modest exhaustiveness, that is ~10⁴–10⁵ core-hours — days on a few hundred cores, but weeks-to-months on a workstation. Induced-fit/ensemble docking (which the OR literature says is *necessary* for accuracy) multiplies this by the number of pocket conformations, typically 5–50×, which pushes an accuracy-adequate campaign out of single-workstation reach. **These are order-of-magnitude estimates, not published figures.**
- The published OR docking work never validates *scores* against potency — only binary hits and pose/mutagenesis agreement. So a 2M-docking matrix would produce a feature vector of unknown calibration. Combined with the ACS Omega group's own choice to use only *relative* comparisons of docking-score vectors, the reasonable read is that docking scores are usable as a **similarity fingerprint**, and not as a surrogate for EC50.
- **Recommended allocation for the user's pipeline**: (1) primary receptor stage = M2OR-trained sequence×molecule model (ESM/protBERT + GNN) producing a 400-OR activation/sensitivity vector; (2) potency readout = soft-min over that vector plus the physicochemical calibration in the previous section; (3) docking/pharmacophore reserved for the 5–20 receptors that dominate the target odorant classes, where a solved structure or a mutagenesis-validated model exists (OR51E2, consOR51/52 for acids, OR5K1 for pyrazines, OR5A2/OR5AN1 for musks, OR7D4 for steroids); (4) Mainland 2015 (73 × 511) as a held-out *measured-activation* validation set for the receptor stage, never as training data for the potency head.
- Because M2OR contains only 768 distinct molecules, a receptor stage will be the narrowest part of the pipeline for novel chemistry. It is probably worth treating the receptor vector as an **auxiliary feature / multi-task target** (train the potency model to also predict OR activation) rather than as a hard bottleneck in series — this captures the +0.6%-to-+33% benefit range observed without inheriting the receptor model's coverage limits.

### Gaps
- No published wall-clock or cost figures for large-scale OR docking campaigns (the ACS Omega paper's compute time per docking was not retrievable — 403 on both publisher and ChemRxiv).
- No head-to-head comparison of docking-derived vs ML-derived OR activation vectors as features for perception or potency prediction was found.
- Whether AF2/AF3 OR models are adequate for *scoring* (as opposed to pose placement) is not answered by any located benchmark; the field's own practice (induced-fit, ensemble docking, MM-PBSA rescoring) implies the answer is no for raw models.
