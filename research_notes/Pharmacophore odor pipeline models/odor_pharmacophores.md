## 1. Classic odor pharmacophores / "osmophore" literature — which are quantitative, which relate to potency, what data they were built on

### Takeaway
The classic structure–odour-relationship (SOR) literature is overwhelmingly
**qualitative and quality-oriented, not potency-oriented**: osmophore/olfactophore
models were built to explain *which molecules smell of musk/sandalwood/amber* from
small hand-curated sets of synthesised analogues, and they encode a spatial
arrangement of one polar "osmophoric" group plus hydrophobic bulk. Potency (odour
threshold) enters these papers as reported anecdotal threshold values for individual
star molecules (e.g. Javanol) rather than as a regressed dependent variable.

### Cited Findings
- The osmophore is classically "associated with a functional group such as carbonyl,
  hydroxyl, occasionally an ether or a limited variety of heteroatomic homologues";
  most odorous molecules contain "both a strong hydrophobic and a relatively weak
  polar region, with the latter being the osmophore" — [Chemical Classification and Structure–Odour Relationships (Springer chapter)](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-94-011-3826-0_9)
- The physicochemical requirements for a sensory-active molecule are set by the
  "location, molecular architecture and physiological medium of the chemoreceptor",
  and the practical upper molecular-weight limit for odorants is ~300 Da — [Chemical Classification and Structure–Odour Relationships](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/978-94-011-3826-0_9.pdf)
- Rossiter's canonical review "Structure−Odor Relationships" is Chem. Rev. (ACS),
  DOI 10.1021/cr950068a — [Chemical Reviews](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/cr950068a)
  (full text 403-blocked; contents not verifiable here)
- Kraft, Bajgrowicz, Denis & Fráter, "Odds and Trends: Recent Developments in the
  Chemistry of Odorants" (2000) is the review that "presents structure–odor
  correlations and olfactophore models for main odor notes of perfumery, including
  musky", and introduces new "trendsetters and captive odorants" — [Semantic Scholar record](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Odds-and-Trends:-Recent-Developments-in-the-of-Kraft-Bajgrowicz/ebf04f5ae56e4e964cf480562ddaa9f4dde810c3);
  [SciSpace record, ~300 citations](https://scispace.com/papers/odds-and-trends-recent-developments-in-the-chemistry-of-4l0dvi5teh)
- Musk olfactophore: work on macrocyclic and linear alicyclic musks "resulted in an
  olfactophore model which makes it likely that macrocyclic and linear alicyclic musks
  address the same set of olfactory receptors" — search-level summary citing Kraft's
  musk-design work, [‘Brain Aided’ Musk Design, Kraft, Chem. Biodivers. 2004](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/cbdv.200490150)
  (full text not retrievable; the unifying-receptor-set claim is reported at
  search-summary level only and should be re-checked against the paper)
- Kraft & Fráter published explicitly on the **enantioselectivity** of musk odour
  sensation, i.e. chirality-dependent musk perception — [Enantioselectivity of the musk odor sensation, Chirality 2001](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/chir.1050)
- Sandalwood olfactophore: Bajgrowicz & Fráter (Givaudan) proposed a sandalwood
  olfactophore; in 1997 they discovered the extremely powerful sandalwood odorant
  later named Javanol. New campholenic-aldehyde derivatives with **rigidifying
  cyclopropane rings** were prepared, and their SOR data "together with consolidated
  previous knowledge on structurally different sandalwood-smelling compounds" allowed
  new sandalwood-olfactophore models to be proposed — [Chiral recognition of sandalwood odorants, Enantiomer 2000 (PubMed 11126862)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11126862/)
- Continuing sandalwood SOR work in the Buchbauer/Wolschann orbit: Stappen, Höfinghoff,
  Buchbauer & Wolschann, "Structure-Activity Relationships of Sandalwood Odorants:
  Synthesis of a New Campholene Derivative" (2010) — [DOI 10.1177/1934578X1000500902](https://doi.org/10.1177/1934578x1000500902)
- Modern receptor-level counterpart for musk: a single odorant receptor was shown to
  sense **four structural classes** of musk compounds — [An odorant receptor that senses four classes of musk compounds, Current Biology 2022](https://www.cell.com/current-biology/fulltext/S0960-9822(22)01686-4);
  molecular-mechanism work on human musk receptors OR5AN1 and OR1A1 with (R)-muscone —
  [PMC5924878](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5924878/)
- A dedicated olfactory receptor + neural pathway for "highly selective sensing of musk
  odors" has been characterised — [Neuron commentary](https://www.cell.com/neuron/comments/S0896-6273(13)00925-2)
- Bioisosterism (a pharmacophore-adjacent medicinal-chemistry concept) has been applied
  to reveal "new structure-odor relationships" — [bioRxiv 567701](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/567701.full.pdf)

### Background-knowledge items (flagged, not sourced in this session)
- **[unverified in this session]** Ohloff's "triaxial rule" for ambergris odour: an
  oxygen function held axially on a decalin-type skeleton with specified axial
  substituents is required for the ambra note; it is a geometric/qualitative rule, not
  a regression model.
- **[unverified in this session]** Beets' "profile-functional group" concept (the
  origin of the osmophore idea) — odour is determined by the molecular profile plus the
  position/orientation of the functional group at the receptor interface.
- **[unverified in this session]** Bajgrowicz's sandalwood olfactophore is usually
  drawn as a hydroxyl (H-bond donor/acceptor) at a defined distance and orientation
  from a bulky, branched hydrophobic cage; nitro-, macrocyclic- and polycyclic-musk
  olfactophores are usually presented as superposable hydrophobic volumes with one
  polar centre.
- **[unverified in this session]** Sell's "On the unpredictability of odor" (Angew.
  Chem. 2006) is the standard counterweight: it argues that odour is not reliably
  predictable from structure, that multiple receptors and their combinatorial code
  break single-pharmacophore logic, and that apparent olfactophores are partly
  artefacts of the chemical series chosen.

### Inferences
- Classic olfactophores are **quality classifiers with an implicit potency bias**: the
  training sets are synthetic programmes aimed at strong odorants, so the molecules
  used to build them are almost all low-threshold compounds. That makes them poor
  sources of *negative* potency examples, which is exactly what a potency regressor
  needs.
- Because these models are family-scoped (musk, sandalwood, amber, woody), their
  natural ML translation is a **family-specific expert / prior**, not a global feature
  set. A global pharmacophore fingerprint dilutes precisely the information that made
  them useful.
- The persistent motif across families — one polar centre plus a specified hydrophobic
  volume and a distance/orientation constraint — is a **3-to-4-point pharmacophore**.
  That is the smallest unit worth trying as an engineered feature.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve the actual olfactophore geometries (distances in Å, feature
  types, tolerance radii) for musk, sandalwood, amber, vetiver, green, marine (Calone)
  or thiol families; all the primary venues (Angew Chem, Chem Biodivers, Helv Chim
  Acta, Chem Rev) blocked automated fetching.
- Found **no** evidence of a quantitative, threshold-regressing classic olfactophore
  for vetiver, green, marine/Calone or sulfur/thiol notes. Thiol potency appears in the
  literature via 3D-QSAR (section 2) rather than via a named olfactophore.
- Could not confirm whether any classic olfactophore paper reports a fitted statistic
  (q², r², classification accuracy) at all.

## 2. 3D-QSAR (CoMFA, CoMSIA, pharmacophore hypotheses) applied to odour thresholds and intensity

### Takeaway
3D-QSAR on *odour quality* exists and goes back to the mid-1990s; 3D-QSAR on *odour
threshold* exists but is rare, small, and reported qualitatively. The best-documented
threshold case is Polster & Schieberle's alkanethiol series, where CoMFA reproduced
thresholds "in good agreement with experiment" but no q²/r² could be recovered from
the abstract. The largest and most rigorous fragrance 3D-QSAR (98 ambergris compounds)
is a **classification** study, not a threshold regression.

### Cited Findings
- **Alkanethiols (potency, CoMFA).** Polster & Schieberle, *J. Agric. Food Chem.*
  2015, 63(5), 1419–1432: multiple homologous alkanethiol series were synthesised;
  "odor thresholds were strongly influenced by steric effects: In all homologous series
  a minimum was observed for thiols with five to seven carbon atoms", chain elongation
  beyond that caused an **exponential increase** in threshold, and tertiary
  alkanethiols had clearly lower thresholds than primary or secondary. A CoMFA-based
  3D-QSAR model "was able to simulate the odor thresholds of alkanethiols in good
  agreement with the experimental results". Spectroscopic data for 46 sulfur compounds
  were reported — [Europe PMC record / J. Agric. Food Chem. 10.1021/jf506135c](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/jf506135c);
  [PubMed 25608797](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25608797)
- The same body of work states CoMFA can "elucidate three-dimensional structural
  features of odorant derivatives, revealing that steric and electrostatic features are
  correlated with human olfactory detection threshold values", and that such models can
  be used to design new odorants by specifying steric and electrostatic requirements —
  search-level summary of the above JAFC paper.
- **Odour quality, CoMFA.** A quantitative 3D model for odour quality was built with
  CoMFA and published in *Chemical Senses* 1996, 21(2), 201 — [Chemical Senses](https://academic.oup.com/chemse/article-abstract/21/2/201/346918)
  (abstract-only access; dataset size and statistics not retrievable)
- **Ambergris, combinatorial QSAR (largest fragrance 3D-QSAR found).** Kovatcheva,
  Golbraikh, Oloff, Xiao, Zheng, Wolschann, Buchbauer & Tropsha, *J. Chem. Inf. Comput.
  Sci.* 2004: 98 ambergris fragrance compounds with complex stereochemistry; seven
  descriptor collections (MOE, **CoMFA**, CoMMA, Dragon, VolSurf, MolconnZ, plus
  chirality topological descriptors) crossed with multiple correlation methods.
  **kNN + CoMFA descriptors was the best approach overall**, with correct
  classification rates ≥ 0.7 on both training and test sets for all dataset divisions —
  [J. Chem. Inf. Model. 10.1021/ci034203t](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/full/10.1021/ci034203t?mobileUi=0);
  [PubMed 15032539](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15032539/)
- A broader methodological review of this line of work exists as "Prediction of
  perception using structure–activity models" — [Academia.edu copy](https://www.academia.edu/31104245/Prediction_of_perception_using_structure_activity_models)
- **Pharmacophore hypotheses for odour quality (modern, largest set found).**
  "Decoding Seven Basic Odors by Investigating Pharmacophores and Molecular Features of
  Odorants", *Current Bioinformatics* 2022, 17(8): 1,136 odorants across seven odours
  (floral, fruity, minty, nutty, pungent, sweet, woody); odorants were mapped to the
  best pharmacophore hypothesis per odour set, and **19 structural features** were
  proposed to discriminate the seven odours; the authors report a shared spatial
  distribution of odour features across classes — [Bentham Science](https://www.benthamdirect.com/content/journals/cbio/10.2174/1574893617666220519111254);
  [EurekaSelect](https://www.eurekaselect.com/public/index.php/article/123762)

### Inferences
- The ambergris study is the strongest available evidence that **3D field descriptors
  (CoMFA) can beat 2D/topological descriptor families on a fragrance endpoint** —
  crucially, on a set with "complex stereochemistry" where chirality topological
  descriptors were needed. That is the single most encouraging precedent for adding
  3D/pharmacophore information to an odour model, but it is a quality classification
  at n=98, not a threshold regression.
- Polster & Schieberle's exponential threshold rise with chain length and the C5–C7
  optimum is a **non-monotonic, shape-driven potency relationship** — the kind of
  effect an alignment-dependent field/pharmacophore model captures naturally and a
  bag-of-substructures fingerprint captures only by memorising the series. This is a
  concrete argument that pharmacophore/steric features could help specifically on the
  scaffold-transfer axis where the user's GBT models drop to R² 0.4–0.65.
- Classic 3D-QSAR requires a common alignment and therefore only works within a
  congeneric series. A global odour-threshold dataset (thousands of compounds, dozens
  of scaffolds) has **no common alignment**, so CoMFA/CoMSIA cannot be lifted directly;
  only alignment-free pharmacophore descriptors (section 3) transfer.

### Gaps
- q², r², r²_pred for the alkanethiol CoMFA model: not present in the abstract and the
  ACS full text returned HTTP 403. **Do not quote numbers for this model.**
- Dataset size and statistics for the *Chemical Senses* 1996 CoMFA odour-quality model:
  abstract paywalled.
- Found **no** CoMFA/CoMSIA threshold study for pyrazines, lactones, musks or
  sandalwood odorants despite targeted searching; either these do not exist in the
  indexed literature or they are in venues (Flavour Fragr. J., Helv. Chim. Acta) not
  surfaced by these searches. Treat "pyrazine threshold CoMFA" as unconfirmed.
- No 3D-QSAR study of *suprathreshold intensity* (psychophysical intensity functions,
  Stevens exponents) was found at all.
- Whether any of these 3D-QSAR models were validated on a scaffold/cluster split:
  unknown. The ambergris paper used "all divisions of the dataset", which is external
  validation but not explicitly scaffold-based.

## 3. Pharmacophore feature representations for ML — do they beat standard descriptors/GNNs?

### Takeaway
Pharmacophore representations help most as a **complement** to circular fingerprints,
not a replacement: the strongest benchmark evidence is that ErG (2D reduced-graph
pharmacophore) earns its place inside a *combination* with ECFP + Avalon + descriptors
in a CatBoost model validated across 22 TDC ADMET tasks. Pharmacophore-constrained
GNNs (PharmHGT) report real but modest gains over atom-graph baselines. Notably, the
two most relevant recent olfaction ML papers used **no pharmacophore features at all**.

### Cited Findings
- **ADMET benchmark (best combination evidence).** A systematic investigation of
  regression algorithms and fingerprints found **gradient-boosted decision trees
  (CatBoost) with a combination of ECFP + Avalon + ErG fingerprints plus ~200 molecular
  properties** to be most effective, validated across **22 Therapeutics Data Commons
  ADMET benchmarks** — [ADMET property prediction through combinations of molecular fingerprints, arXiv:2310.00174](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.00174)
- **ErG mechanics.** ErG "describes a molecular structure by defining its
  pharmacophoric points and the topological distance between them", setting bits from
  pairwise pharmacophore-type × distance combinations, and uses **fuzzy incrementation**
  which "favours retrieval of actives with different core structures (scaffold
  hopping)" — [ErG: 2D Pharmacophore Descriptions for Scaffold Hopping](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/7346901_ErG_2D_Pharmacophore_Descriptions_for_Scaffold_Hopping)
- **Composite-representation practice.** Recent multimodal work combines Morgan +
  PubChem + pharmacophore ErG fingerprints as complementary views, with ErG
  contributing pharmacophoric/spatial pattern information — [Multi-Level Fusion Graph Neural Network for Molecule Property Prediction, arXiv:2507.03430](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.03430)
- **PharmHGT (pharmacophore-constrained heterogeneous graph transformer).** Uses a
  "pharmacophoric-constrained multi-view molecular representation graph" to extract
  information from functional substructures and reaction information that
  "have not been fully exploited by prior atom-based molecular graph representation".
  Reported improvements over the best baselines: **up to +1.55% ROC-AUC and 0.272 RMSE**
  — [Pharmacophoric-constrained heterogeneous graph transformer model, Communications Chemistry 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42004-023-00857-x);
  [PMC10070395](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10070395/)
- **Pharmacophore-GNN integration, earlier.** "Integrating concept of pharmacophore
  with graph neural networks for chemical property prediction and interpretation",
  *J. Cheminform.* 2022 — [J Cheminform 10.1186/s13321-022-00634-3](https://jcheminf.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s13321-022-00634-3);
  [PubMed 35927691](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35927691/)
- **Pharmacophore features as the primary representation in a task-specific model.**
  A pharmacophore-based ML model was built to predict ligand selectivity for E3 ligase
  binders — [PMC10448689](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10448689/)
- **Available implementations.** RDKit-family and other pharmacophore featurizers
  (including Gobbi-style 2D pharmacophore fingerprints, Pharm2D, ErG, CATS) are
  catalogued and callable in molfeat — [molfeat featurizer list](https://molfeat.datamol.io/featurizers);
  a public reference implementation of nearest-neighbour similarity analysis comparing
  ECFP against **Gobbi** pharmacophore fingerprints exists — [RDKit_NN_Analysis_ECFP_Gobbi_FPs](https://github.com/lhm30/RDKit_NN_Analysis_ECFP_Gobbi_FPs)
- **Generative/3D pharmacophore side (for completeness).** PGMG encodes spatially
  distributed chemical features with a GNN and decodes molecules with a transformer,
  requiring only a pharmacophore hypothesis as input, and uses a latent variable to
  handle the many-to-many pharmacophore→molecule mapping — [Nature Communications 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-023-41454-9);
  [arXiv:2207.00821](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.00821). **PhoreGen** is a diffusion-based
  method generating complete 3D structures under a pharmacophore model, operating
  directly in 3D and supporting **directed** pharmacophore features (unlike PGMG) —
  search-level summary; primary PhoreGen citation not retrieved. **CReM-pharm** does
  de novo 3D pharmacophore-based design with synthetic-accessibility awareness —
  [J. Cheminform. 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13321-026-01195-5)

### Negative / null evidence on the olfaction side (important)
- **Comparative fingerprint study for odour decoding (Communications Chemistry 2025).**
  Suh, Hong & Park, *Commun. Chem.* **8**, 278 (2025), DOI 10.1038/s42004-025-01651-7:
  8,681 unique odorants from ten curated sources, multi-label prediction of **200 odour
  descriptors**, models RF / XGBoost / LightGBM. Representations compared were only
  functional-group SMARTS fingerprints (FG), molecular descriptors (MD), and Morgan
  structural fingerprints derived from 3D MolBlocks (ST). **No pharmacophore
  fingerprint (Gobbi, Pharm2D, ErG, CATS) was included.** Results: ST-XGB best at
  AUROC 0.828 / AUPRC 0.237; ST-LGBM 0.810 / 0.228; ST-RF 0.784 / 0.216; MD-XGB 0.802
  / 0.200; FG-XGB worst at 0.753 / 0.088 — [PMC12462479](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12462479/)
- **Odour-strength (potency-adjacent) ML, 2026.** Fichtelmann & Westermayr,
  *RSC Advances* 2026: 2,393 molecules with **ordinal** odour-strength labels
  (odorless / low / medium / high) from The Good Scents Company (1,678) and PubChem
  (715); inter-source agreement Cohen's κ = 0.83, Krippendorff's α = 0.81.
  Featurisations: 217 RDKit descriptors; Morgan, MACCS, RDKit, topological-torsion and
  atom-pair fingerprints; ChemBERTa-2 (77M SMILES), ChemProp MPNN, CheMeleon
  foundation model. **No pharmacophore features were used.** Models: logistic
  regression, RF, XGBoost, MLP, CORAL ordinal MLP. Split: 80/20 stratified, 10×10-fold
  CV, with compounds ≥80% Morgan Tanimoto similarity forced into the same split to
  prevent leakage. Best ensemble (three RDKit-descriptor models): **72% accuracy** on
  the 4-class task; external validation against Keller et al. intensity ratings
  correlated "reasonably" but degraded at high dilution (10⁻¹) —
  [PMC13182554](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13182554/);
  [RSC Advances DOI 10.1039/D6RA01805J](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2026/ra/d6ra01805j);
  [arXiv:2512.08683](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.08683)
- **Threshold regression state of the art (structure- and MS²-based).** Sun, Jia & Xi:
  structure-based model trained on **1,741 compounds** with odour detection thresholds
  spanning 10⁻⁹ to 10⁸ mg/m³ achieved **test R² ≥ 0.65**; the MS²-based threshold model
  reached accuracy 0.81 — [SSRN preprint](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7070562);
  related published work on predicting odour sensory attributes from fragmentation mass
  spectra — [Environ. Sci. Technol. 10.1021/acs.est.4c01763](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acs.est.4c01763)
- **Other threshold-ML precedent.** "A framework for predicting odor threshold values
  of perfumes by scientific machine learning and transfer learning" — [Heliyon / ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2405844023080210)

### Inferences
- The ADMET evidence is the closest methodological analogue to the user's setup
  (GBT + descriptors on a sparse, scaffold-diverse regression endpoint), and there the
  winning recipe **already includes a pharmacophore fingerprint (ErG) alongside ECFP**.
  That is the single best justification for trying ErG on odour thresholds, and it
  costs almost nothing to test.
- ErG's fuzzy incrementation is explicitly a **scaffold-hopping** device. Scaffold
  generalisation is exactly where the user's models fail (R² 0.8 → 0.4–0.65). So the
  mechanism of benefit and the observed failure mode line up — this is the most
  defensible hypothesis to test, though no one has tested it on odour thresholds.
- PharmHGT's gains (≈1.5 ROC-AUC points) are real but small. Expect pharmacophore-aware
  GNN architecture changes to be a **second-order** improvement; feature concatenation
  is the higher-expected-value first move.
- The two most recent odour ML papers independently skipped pharmacophore features.
  This means (a) there is a genuine, publishable gap, and (b) there is **no published
  baseline showing pharmacophore features help on odour**, so any claim of benefit must
  be established from scratch on the user's own splits.
- Suh et al.'s FG-fingerprint result (AUPRC 0.088, far worst) is a caution: a
  **coarse, hand-defined chemical-feature vocabulary underperforms badly** on odour.
  Pharmacophore fingerprints are exactly that kind of coarse vocabulary unless the
  distance/geometry channel carries the information. Use the distance-binned variants
  (Pharm2D, ErG, CATS) rather than plain feature-count vectors.

### Gaps
- No head-to-head benchmark of pharmacophore fingerprints vs ECFP **on any olfactory
  endpoint** was found. This appears genuinely absent from the literature.
- Could not find CATS-specific benchmark results in this session (searches returned
  ErG and Gobbi but not CATS comparisons).
- MoleculeNet / Polaris leaderboard entries broken down by pharmacophore-vs-ECFP
  representation: not retrieved.
- Whether Principal Odor Map (POM) embeddings have ever been compared against or fused
  with pharmacophore features: not found.
- PhoreGen's primary publication and its reported metrics were not retrieved; treat
  the PGMG-vs-PhoreGen comparison as search-summary level and **uncertain**.

## 4. Olfaction/receptor work that actually used pharmacophore models, and the conformational-flexibility & chirality problem

### Takeaway
Pharmacophore modelling *is* used in olfaction, but almost entirely on the
**receptor-ligand discovery** side (finding agonists for a single OR), not for
population-level potency. The flexibility problem is not a nuisance detail: there is
direct published evidence that **molecular flexibility, not shape alone, predicts
whether enantiomers smell different** — which undermines naive rigid-pharmacophore
matching for odorants.

### Cited Findings
- **OR-specific pharmacophore screening.** A two-stage approach for OR1A2 first used a
  pharmacophore model based on **atomic property field (APF)** to virtually screen
  5,942 human metabolites, then ran structure-based virtual screening against a 3D
  homology model of OR1A2. OR1A2 had only **13 known ligands** at the time —
  [PMC8244491](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8244491/);
  [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2665928X20300210)
- **OR5K1.** Experimental data guided investigation of OR5K1 ligand binding modes in
  the orthosteric site, combining AI-driven structure prediction with homology
  modelling — reported in [Computational modelling of olfactory receptors (review, 2025)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0304416525000704);
  [PubMed 40441539](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40441539/)
- That review states the OR computational toolkit spans homology modelling, docking,
  MD, free-energy calculations, **pharmacophore modelling (both ligand-based and
  structure-based)**, virtual screening and ML-based prediction — same source.
- **OR2W1** interaction modelling — [PMC8042298](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8042298/)
- **High-potency agonists from virtual screening.** vHTS produced "high potency
  olfactory receptor agonists ... molecular probes for receptor structure and olfactory
  function" — [PMC2652502](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2652502/) — i.e. a
  precedent that structure/ligand-based models can optimise *receptor potency*
  (EC₅₀-like), the closest receptor-level analogue of threshold.
- **ML for ectopic OR agonists.** "Machine Learning Assisted Approach for Finding Novel
  High Activity Agonists of Human Ectopic Olfactory Receptors" — [PMC8583936](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8583936/)
- **Perceived odour quality of ligands for one human OR** was related to molecular
  structure — [PubMed 18603653](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18603653/)
- **Combinatorial code framing.** "The perception of thousands of odors by a few
  hundreds of olfactory receptors results from combinatorial coding, in which one
  receptor recognizes multiple odorants and an odorant is recognized by a specific group
  of receptors" — search-level summary consistent with the Bentham pharmacophore paper,
  [Decoding Seven Basic Odors](https://www.benthamdirect.com/content/journals/cbio/10.2174/1574893617666220519111254)
- **Flexibility vs enantiomer discrimination (key result).** Brookes, Horsfield &
  Stoneham, *J. R. Soc. Interface* 2008, 6(30), 75–86: 428 enantiomer pairs (856
  molecules) from the Leffingwell database; 450 molecules classed flexible, 404 rigid,
  2 uncertain. **Flexible enantiomers consistently smell different; rigid ones smell
  the same** — described by the authors as counter-intuitive for shape-based olfaction
  models. Type-2 (different-smelling) pairs: 41%. Type-1 prevalence 5% under strict
  criteria, 59% under lenient criteria. B3LYP/6-31G* chair↔boat barriers 0.07–0.27 eV
  (cyclohexane reference 0.28 eV, matched within 0.02 eV) — [PMC2610320](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2610320/)
- **Enantiomer potency spreads (concrete numbers).** (−)-cis-rose oxide detection
  threshold ≈ **0.2 µg/L** vs (+)-cis ≈ **50 µg/L** — a **250-fold** potency difference;
  (S)-(−)-α-damascone threshold 1.5 ppb vs (R)-enantiomer 100 ppb ≈ **65-fold** —
  search-level summary drawing on [Rose oxide (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rose_oxide)
  and a cycloaliphatic-ketone patent, [US5041678](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/5041678).
  **Flag as needing a primary-literature check** (secondary/patent sourcing).
- Humans can significantly discriminate carvone enantiomers, but the retrieved sources
  do not give the threshold ratio — [Olfactory Discrimination Ability of Human Subjects for Ten Pairs of Enantiomers, Chemical Senses 1999](https://academic.oup.com/chemse/article-abstract/24/2/161/312273)
- Enantiomers that are perceptually indistinguishable can still produce **different
  peripheral vs central electrophysiological responses** — [Sci. Rep. 2017](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-017-09594-3);
  dorsal OR populations show "supersensitive detection and discrimination of
  enantiomers", evidence for hierarchical odour coding — [Sci. Rep. 2015](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep14073)

### Inferences
- The Brookes et al. flexibility result argues against **single-conformer** 3D
  pharmacophore fingerprints for odorants: for the flexible half of odour space, the
  perceptually relevant object is a conformer *ensemble*, and enantiodiscrimination
  arises where that ensemble differs. Practical consequence: if 3D pharmacophore
  features are used, generate a conformer ensemble (e.g. ETKDG + MMFF, 20–100
  conformers) and pool features across it (max/mean per bit), rather than using a
  single low-energy conformer.
- Two-orders-of-magnitude enantiomeric threshold spreads mean **any achiral
  representation has an irreducible error floor** on potency. Standard ECFP without
  chiral flags and standard 2D pharmacophore fingerprints are both achiral. The
  ambergris study needed explicit chirality topological descriptors to work (section 2),
  which is convergent evidence.
- OR-level pharmacophores are per-receptor. Because perception is combinatorial, a
  single global "odour pharmacophore" for potency is theoretically ill-posed; a
  **bank of receptor-family or odour-family pharmacophore hypotheses, used as a feature
  vector of fit scores**, is the theoretically coherent translation.
- Very few ORs have enough ligand data (OR1A2: 13 ligands) to build reliable
  ligand-based pharmacophores. So a receptor-indexed pharmacophore feature bank is
  currently limited to a handful of ORs (OR1A1, OR1A2, OR2W1, OR5AN1, OR5K1, OR7D4 area).

### Gaps
- Could not confirm any study using pharmacophore fingerprints as ML features for
  **odour descriptor or threshold prediction** on a large dataset. The Bentham
  seven-odour paper is the closest (pharmacophore hypotheses for quality, n=1,136) but
  it is hypothesis-generation, not a benchmarked predictive model; its reported
  statistics could not be retrieved.
- OR7D4 pharmacophore-specific work was not surfaced; the OR7D4 literature found by
  these searches is functional/genetic rather than pharmacophoric. **Unconfirmed.**
- Doszczak's and Sell's specific pharmacophore contributions could not be retrieved
  (paywalled / not surfaced).
- Exact (R)- vs (S)-carvone and nootkatone threshold values: not found in an accessible
  primary source.
- No source found that quantifies how much of odour-threshold variance is attributable
  to stereochemistry across a large dataset.

## 5. Concrete ways to integrate pharmacophores into an odour-potency model

### Takeaway
There is no published odour-specific recipe, so the defensible plan is to port the
**ADMET-validated recipe** (ECFP + Avalon + ErG + ~200 descriptors → CatBoost,
validated on 22 TDC benchmarks) to odour thresholds, and to judge it exclusively on
scaffold/cluster splits, since scaffold transfer is both the stated weakness and the
only place pharmacophore features have a mechanistic reason to help.

### Cited Findings (basis for each recipe)
- The concatenation recipe is not speculative — it is the validated ADMET winner:
  CatBoost + (ECFP, Avalon, ErG) + ~200 molecular properties across 22 TDC ADMET tasks
  — [arXiv:2310.00174](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.00174)
- ErG is designed for scaffold hopping via fuzzy incrementation — [ErG paper](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/7346901_ErG_2D_Pharmacophore_Descriptions_for_Scaffold_Hopping)
- Featurizers for the concatenation (Gobbi/Pharm2D, ErG, CATS, and 3D pharmacophore
  variants) are available off-the-shelf — [molfeat](https://molfeat.datamol.io/featurizers)
- Multi-view fusion of Morgan + PubChem + ErG is an established pattern in current
  GNN work — [arXiv:2507.03430](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.03430)
- If an architectural change is preferred over concatenation, PharmHGT provides the
  reference implementation and expected effect size (≤ +1.55% ROC-AUC, 0.272 RMSE) —
  [Commun. Chem. 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42004-023-00857-x)
- Family-specific expert models have direct fragrance precedent: the ambergris study
  achieved ≥0.7 classification on 98 compounds of one odour family using CoMFA fields
  + chirality descriptors, beating other descriptor families — [10.1021/ci034203t](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/full/10.1021/ci034203t?mobileUi=0)
- A "pharmacophore-similarity-to-known-potent-odorants" feature has an analogue in the
  APF pharmacophore prefilter used before docking for OR1A2 (5,942-compound screen) —
  [PMC8244491](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8244491/)
- Precedent for pharmacophore features as the primary representation in a selectivity
  ML model — [PMC10448689](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10448689/)
- For evaluation discipline: Fichtelmann & Westermayr enforced that compounds with
  ≥80% Morgan Tanimoto similarity stay in the same split, an explicit anti-leakage
  protocol worth copying — [PMC13182554](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13182554/)
- Realistic target to beat: structure-based threshold model, 1,741 compounds, test
  R² ≥ 0.65 — [SSRN 7070562](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7070562)

### Inferences — ranked integration recipes
1. **Concatenate ErG (+ Gobbi/Pharm2D 2D pharmacophore FP) with the existing
   descriptor block, keep the GBT.** Lowest cost, only recipe with cross-domain
   benchmark support, directly targets scaffold transfer. Report Δ on scaffold split
   separately from random split; a gain on random split alone means nothing here.
2. **Add chirality-aware features.** Given 65–250× enantiomeric threshold spreads and
   the ambergris paper's reliance on chirality topological descriptors, use
   chirality-flagged ECFP plus explicit stereo descriptors. Without this, no
   pharmacophore variant can represent enantiomer potency differences at all.
3. **Conformer-ensemble 3D pharmacophore features (Pharm2D/3D over an ensemble).**
   Justified by the flexibility–enantiodiscrimination result; pool bits across 20–100
   ETKDG/MMFF conformers (max- and mean-pooling as two feature blocks). Higher cost,
   moderate expected payoff, and the flexibility evidence says single-conformer
   versions are the wrong call.
4. **Family-specific pharmacophore experts + a gate.** Build or transcribe
   olfactophore hypotheses for musk, sandalwood, amber, thiol families; compute a
   fit/overlay score per hypothesis; feed the score vector as ~10–30 dense features to
   the global model (soft version), or route family members to dedicated regressors
   (hard version, ambergris-style). This is where the classic literature's information
   actually lives.
5. **Pharmacophore similarity to a reference set of known ultra-potent odorants**
   (Javanol, thiols, damascones, Calone) as a small block of max/top-k similarity
   features. Cheap, interpretable, and mirrors the APF-prefilter idea.
6. **Pharmacophore-based applicability domain.** Use distance in pharmacophore space
   (not ECFP space) to the training set to flag out-of-domain predictions. This is a
   near-free way to convert the scaffold-split weakness from a silent error into a
   reported abstention, which may be more valuable operationally than a small R² gain.
7. **Architecture change (PharmHGT-style pharmacophore-constrained GNN, or fusing
   pharmacophore features into a POM-style GNN)** last: expected effect size is small
   relative to the engineering cost, and POM's demonstrated strength is on quality
   multi-label tasks rather than potency regression.

### Gaps
- No published evidence exists on whether any of recipes 1–7 improve **odour
  threshold** prediction. All are extrapolations from adjacent domains and must be
  treated as hypotheses to be tested on the user's own scaffold splits.
- No published pharmacophore-based applicability-domain study for odorants was found;
  recipe 6 is an inference, not a citation.
- Effect sizes cannot be forecast: the ADMET paper's benefit from ErG is reported as
  part of a winning *combination*, and no ablation isolating ErG's individual
  contribution was retrieved. **Do not quote an expected ΔR² for adding ErG.**
- Whether POM/GNN embeddings and pharmacophore features are complementary or redundant
  on odour tasks is entirely open.
