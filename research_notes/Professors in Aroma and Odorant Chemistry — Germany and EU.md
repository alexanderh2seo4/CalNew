# Professors in aroma, flavor, and odorant chemistry — Germany and EU

**Research date:** 26 September 2026  
**Scope:** Germany and EU member states. This is a curated research map, not a complete census. I verified each main-list entry against a university or public research-institute page and favored current investigators whose work explicitly involves aroma/flavor compounds, volatile odorants, their formation or synthesis, chemical analysis, or chemosensory effects. Rank labels follow the institution where available; “assistant professor” and “associate professor” are included and identified as such.

## Executive summary

This search identified **42 professor-level academics or professor-equivalent group leaders across 11 countries**: Germany plus France, Austria, Belgium, Denmark, the Netherlands, Italy, Spain, Portugal, Sweden, and Poland. The strongest concentration is in food and beverage aroma chemistry. Fine-fragrance ingredient synthesis is a smaller niche; the clearest dedicated perfume/aroma chemistry group found is at Université Côte d’Azur in Nice.

For the odorant-potency and molecular-odor work in this vault, begin with **Andrea Büttner (FAU), Uwe Meierhenrich (Université Côte d’Azur), Vicente Ferreira and Ana Escudero (Zaragoza), Erich Leitner (TU Graz), Mikael Agerlin Petersen and Beatriz Quintanilla Casas (Copenhagen), Erica Liberto (Turin), and Henryk Jeleń (Poznań)**. For the most direct access to key-food-odorant methods and a specialist odorant database, also contact **PD Dr. Martin Steinhaus** at Leibniz-LSB@TUM; his rank is not professor, so he is listed separately below.

### Fit labels

- **A — Direct:** current work explicitly identifies, measures, synthesizes, biosynthesizes, or models aroma compounds / odorants.
- **B — Strongly related:** active work on flavor formation, volatile profiles, food/wine aroma, or sensory chemistry, often in a particular product system.
- **C — Adjacent:** useful for odor perception, receptors, food-matrix effects, or natural-product synthesis, but less centered on aroma-chemical characterization itself.

## How to use this map

1. If the goal is **fine-fragrance molecules**, start with Meierhenrich’s Nice group and then contact synthetic terpene/natural-product chemists such as Gulder. In the institutions reviewed, food aroma and flavor chemistry is much more common than academic synthesis of commercial perfumery ingredients.
2. If the goal is **odor thresholds or potency**, prioritize labs that combine chemical identification with sensory or psychophysical measurements. The best evidenced fits here are Büttner, Ferreira/Escudero, Leitner, the Copenhagen aroma group, Liberto, Jeleń, and the Leibniz-LSB@TUM aroma group.
3. If the goal is **new chemical data**, ask a group about its measurement protocol, available anonymized or publishable datasets, sample coverage, and reuse terms. The Leibniz-LSB@TUM database is especially relevant, but its terms require permission for systematic download or reuse in similar products; access to a searchable record should not be assumed to grant bulk-data rights.

## Coverage limits

This is a high-relevance contact map, not an exhaustive directory of every food chemist, sensory scientist, perfumer, or olfaction laboratory in Europe. I left out broad food-science groups with no clear current aroma/odorant activity, researchers whose academic rank or current affiliation was uncertain, and non-EU countries such as the United Kingdom and Switzerland. A few entries (marked **C**) are deliberately included as adjacent expertise and should be treated as secondary leads.
