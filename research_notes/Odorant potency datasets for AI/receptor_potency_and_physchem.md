# Receptor-level odorant potency datasets and free physicochemical/volatility datasets

Scope: (A) odorant–olfactory-receptor activity data with quantitative potency (EC50, dose–response, activation amplitude); (B) free physicochemical property data (vapor pressure, Henry's law, logP, boiling point, Abraham descriptors) usable as ML features or for threshold unit conversion. Verified September 2026. Uncertain items are explicitly marked.

## (A) Which odorant–olfactory-receptor datasets give quantitative potency and are free?

### Takeaway
M2OR is the only large, curated, openly downloadable OR–odorant dataset that systematically records quantitative potency (EC50 where a dose–response was run, plus screening concentration for every experiment); its scale is 75,050 bioassay experiments / 51,395 distinct OR–molecule pairs, but only ~6% of pairs are agonist (active) pairs and the EC50-bearing subset is a small minority of that. Everything else in the ecosystem is either a smaller primary screen (Mainland 2015, Trimmer 2019), a binary/aggregated resource (OlfactionBase, OdorDB, DoOR), a dead resource (ODORactor), or a general GPCR database with thin OR coverage (ChEMBL, GtoPdb, GLASS).

### Cited Findings

**M2OR (Lalis, Hladiš, Abi Khalil, Fiorucci, Briand, Topin — Nucleic Acids Research 2024, 52:D1370–D1379)**
- Contains 75,050 bioassay experiments covering 51,395 distinct OR–molecule pairs — [M2OR, NAR 2024 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10767820/); [OUP](https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/52/D1/D1370/7327067)
- 768 unique molecules (176 "sum of isomers", 407 non-stereoisomeric, 168 specific isomers, 17 mixtures); 1,246 unique OR protein sequences from 11 mammalian species; ~66% human, ~33% mouse — [M2OR (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10767820/)
- Only ~6% of pairs are agonist pairs (≈3,100 active); 48,295 pairs are recorded as non-responsive. Inclusion of explicit negatives is a distinguishing feature vs earlier databases — [M2OR (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10767820/)
- Quantitative fields: "M2OR includes either screening concentration or EC50 for all gathered experimental data," and for dose–response measurements the EC50 goes into the `Value`/`Unit` columns — [M2OR (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10767820/); [M2OR GitHub README](https://github.com/chemosim-lab/M2OR)
- Compiled from 42 scientific articles, with experimental metadata: cell line (HEK293T, Hana3A, ScL21, yeast, Xenopus oocytes, native OSNs, NxG108CC15, cancer lines), G-protein used (Golf, Gα16, Gα15/16, Gαq), replicate counts, mutations — [M2OR (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10767820/)
- Assay-type mix: ~41% of experiments are luciferase assays in Hana3A cells; also cAMP, Ca2+, SEAP, GFP fluorescence, and electrophysiological membrane measures (intensity/conductance) — [M2OR (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10767820/)
- Identifiers: molecules by standardized InChIKey plus CAS, name, SMILES, PubChem CID; receptors by full protein sequence (primary key), gene name, UniProt ID, species, and documented mutations — [M2OR (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10767820/)
- Curation caveats stated by the authors: 27 contradictory dose–response pairs and 246 screening inconsistencies were excluded from the aggregated tables — [M2OR (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10767820/)
- Access: web interface https://m2or.chemsensim.fr/ (search by molecule, receptor, or batch query; also offers a "binarized combinatorial code of olfaction" download); raw data + scripts at https://github.com/chemosim-lab/M2OR and Zenodo https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.8385484 — [ChemSenSim lab page](https://lab.chemsensim.fr/index.php/machine-learning/); [M2OR (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10767820/)
- Licensing: the GitHub repository carries an **Apache-2.0** license; the GitHub snapshot file is `M2OR_20230428.csv` and the README states the maintained, up-to-date data is on the web portal (i.e. the GitHub CSV lags the site) — [M2OR GitHub](https://github.com/chemosim-lab/M2OR). The NAR paper itself is open access under **CC-BY 4.0** — [M2OR (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10767820/)
- The database is molecular-level only; it does not link receptor activity to perceptual thresholds — [M2OR (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10767820/)
- Associated ML release: Hladiš, Lalis, Fiorucci, Topin, "Matching receptor to odorant with protein language and graph neural networks," ICLR 2023 — code at https://github.com/MatejHl/Receptor2Odorant — [Receptor2Odorant GitHub](https://github.com/MatejHl/Receptor2Odorant)

**M2OR-derived status report (Lalis, Hladiš, Abi Khalil, Deroo, Marin, Bensafi, Baldovini, Briand, Fiorucci, Topin — Chemical Senses 49:bjae037, 2024)** — the single most useful quantitative summary of human OR potency coverage:
- 1,057 unique human OR sequences from 436 gene names; 550 human ORs deorphanized (~52% of tested sequences); ~88% of documented human OR sequences remain orphan — [Chemical Senses bjae037](https://dx.doi.org/10.1093/chemse/bjae037)
- EC50 distribution: activities cluster micromolar–millimolar; 87% of EC50 values are covered by a 500 µM screening concentration and 96% by 1,000 µM; median EC50 deviation across repeated experiments is 0.54 log(M), with 72% of deviations below 1 log(M) — [Chemical Senses bjae037](https://dx.doi.org/10.1093/chemse/bjae037)
- Authors flag odorant impurity as a substantial confound in published OR screens — [Chemical Senses bjae037](https://dx.doi.org/10.1093/chemse/bjae037)
- Licensing caveat: the article is marked "© The Author(s) 2024. Published by Oxford University Press. All rights reserved" (i.e. not open-access CC-BY), though the underlying data is openly available in M2OR — [Chemical Senses bjae037](https://dx.doi.org/10.1093/chemse/bjae037)

- Data are deposited in a Springer Nature figshare collection ("Human Olfactory Receptor Responses to Odorants") — [figshare collection 979135](https://springernature.figshare.com/collections/Human_Olfactory_Receptor_Responses_to_Odorants/979135)

**Trimmer, Keller, Murphy, Snyder, Willer, Nagai, Katsanis, Vosshall, Matsunami, Mainland — "Genetic variation across the human olfactory receptor repertoire alters odor perception," PNAS 2019**
- This is a **perceptual/genetics** study, not an OR EC50 screen: 276 olfactory phenotypes including perceived intensity and pleasantness of 68 odorants at two concentrations, detection thresholds of three odorants, and general olfactory acuity — [PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/abs/10.1073/pnas.1804106115); [PMC6511007](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6511007/)
- Key mechanistic result: loss of function of an individual OR correlates with changes in perceived intensity and pleasantness of its ligand — [PMC6511007](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6511007/)
- **Access restriction to flag:** data are in dbGaP under accession phs001757.v1.p1 — dbGaP is controlled access (application + data-use agreement), not a free download, and human-subject genotype data typically carries use restrictions incompatible with unrestricted ML training — [PMC6511007](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6511007/)

**OlfactionBase (Nucleic Acids Research 2022, 50:D678–D686; IIIT Allahabad)**
- Free open-access web server: 3,985 odorant compounds, 1,124 odorless compounds, 2,871 OBP/PBP proteins, plus physicochemical and ADMET properties — [NAR OlfactionBase](https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/50/D1/D678/6362078); [PMC8728123](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8728123/)
- OR–odorant interaction pairs: **408 (human)** and **466 (mouse)** — small, and per the M2OR authors' comparison, OlfactionBase exposes 874 pairs with "limited access to experimental results," while OdorDB lists only 95 odorants known to activate ORs — [NAR OlfactionBase](https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/50/D1/D678/6362078); [M2OR (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10767820/)
- URL: https://bioserver.iiita.ac.in/olfactionbase/ — [NAR OlfactionBase](https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/50/D1/D678/6362078)

**ODORactor** — developed in the early 2010s and **no longer maintained**; treat as dead/legacy — [M2OR (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10767820/). A review of the legacy landscape (ORDB, HORDE, ODORactor and others) is available — [Database (Oxford) baw132](https://academic.oup.com/database/article/doi/10.1093/database/baw132/2630523)

**DoOR 2.0 (Münch & Galizia, Scientific Reports 6:21841, 2016) — Drosophila**
- Collects published *Drosophila melanogaster* odor-response profiles from the literature and maps heterogeneous measurements (different labs, techniques, metrics) onto a **common normalized response scale**, producing a consensus response matrix for the olfactome; the 2016 release added unpublished profiles for Or10a, Or42b, Or47b, Or56a — [Sci Rep 21841](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep21841); [PMC4766438](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4766438/)
- Distributed as rOpenSci R packages `DoOR.data` and `DoOR.functions`; installable via `install_github("ropensci/DoOR.data", ref="v2.0.1")`; project site http://neuro.uni.kn/DoOR — [rOpenSci DoOR post](https://ropensci.org/blog/2018/03/27/door/); [DoOR.data GitHub](https://github.com/ropensci/DoOR.data); [r-universe](https://ropensci.r-universe.dev/DoOR.data)
- **Critical modelling caveat for a potency model:** DoOR responses are *rank/scale-normalized merged response units*, not EC50s or absolute amplitudes. The normalization is the point of the resource, and it destroys absolute potency information. Also, DoOR is insect (OR/Orco ion-channel receptors), pharmacologically unlike mammalian GPCR ORs — [Sci Rep 21841](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep21841)
- License and exact odorant/receptor counts: **not confirmed** (the GitHub landing page I fetched did not expose a license field). Gap.

### Inferences
- For a potency-regression model, the usable supervised signal is far smaller than M2OR's headline 51,395 pairs: only ~3,100 pairs are active at all, and only the dose–response subset of those carries an EC50. Plan for a low-thousands-at-best EC50 training set, with the remaining ~48k non-responsive pairs usable as censored/negative labels (activity below the tested screening concentration) rather than as potency values.
- M2OR's schema (screening concentration recorded for every experiment) makes principled censored-regression / survival-style loss formulations possible — a non-response at 300 µM is a real lower bound on EC50, not a missing value.
- The 0.54 log(M) median EC50 reproducibility figure from the status report is effectively the noise floor for any model trained on these data; a model reporting <0.5 log-unit test error on heterogeneous OR data should be suspected of leakage.
- Apache-2.0 on the M2OR GitHub snapshot is permissive and commercial-ML friendly; the web portal's terms were not retrievable (403), so for commercial use the safe path is the Zenodo/GitHub deposit plus direct confirmation from the maintainers.
- Mixing DoOR (insect, normalized units) with M2OR (mammalian, molar EC50) in one regression target is not defensible; DoOR is better used for a separate insect-receptor task or for qualitative breadth-of-tuning features.

## Does receptor-level potency correlate with human odor detection thresholds, and who has used receptor data to predict perceptual potency?

### Takeaway
Yes, with real but narrow evidence: within chemically coherent series (notably musks) receptor EC50 and efficacy correlate strongly with median human detection thresholds (r ≈ 0.87 for EC50, r ≈ −0.91 for efficacy), and single-OR work shows individual receptors can set detection thresholds. The correlation has not been demonstrated at repertoire scale across chemical classes, and there is evidence that EC20 (the low-concentration foot of the dose–response curve) tracks in-vivo threshold better than EC50.

### Cited Findings
- For musk compounds, musk ketone had both the lowest detection threshold and the lowest EC50 (highest potency) while muscone had the highest of both; Pearson correlation between odor detection threshold and EC50 was **r = 0.87**, and correlation with efficacy was **r = −0.91**, against median odor detection thresholds — [Allosteric modulation of a human odorant receptor, Current Biology 2023](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0960982223003044)
- The **EC20** value, characterizing the lower part of the dose–response curve, has been shown to correlate with in-vivo sensory detection threshold in the case of **OR5A2** — [Current Biology 2023](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0960982223003044)
- "Single olfactory receptors set odor detection thresholds" — direct evidence that loss/variation at one OR shifts the human detection threshold for its ligand — [PubMed 30038239](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30038239) (I could not retrieve the full record — PubMed served a reCAPTCHA page — so the author list and journal are **unverified**; the title is as indexed in search results)
- Repertoire-scale perceptual consequence of OR function: loss of function of an individual OR correlates with changes in perceived intensity and pleasantness — [Trimmer et al., PNAS 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6511007/)
- Human ORs as a population appear "optimized around approximately 100 µM of odorant," and EC50/efficacy distributions across the repertoire give a view of receptor tuning — [Current Biology 2023](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0960982223003044); consistent with the M2OR status report's µM–mM clustering — [Chemical Senses bjae037](https://dx.doi.org/10.1093/chemse/bjae037)
- Receptor-activity → perception modelling exists: "Predicting Human Olfactory Perception from Activities of Odorant Receptors" — [PMC7393469](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7393469/); and "Mapping the combinatorial coding between olfactory receptors and perception with deep learning," *Cell Systems* 2026 — [Cell Systems](https://www.cell.com/cell-systems/fulltext/S2405-4712(26)00193-6?rss=yes) (full text returned 403; size, data availability and conclusions **unverified**)
- Statistical structure of odorant–receptor interactions and its implications for human sensing — [Implications for human odor sensing…, PLOS Comput Biol 2018](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pcbi.1006175); [PMC5983876](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5983876/)

### Inferences
- A sensible architecture is two-stage: predict receptor-level potency (EC50 / activation) from structure, then map the predicted receptor activation vector to perceptual potency. The musk r ≈ 0.87 result supports the second stage being learnable, but only where a small number of ORs dominate detection.
- Because EC20 outperformed EC50 for OR5A2, a model that predicts full dose–response parameters (EC50 *and* Hill slope/efficacy) will map to thresholds better than one predicting EC50 alone. M2OR's efficacy/amplitude fields matter as much as EC50.
- The ~100 µM receptor operating range versus human detection thresholds often in the ppb-to-ppt range implies a large, odorant-dependent gain factor between receptor pharmacology and psychophysics — dominated by air–mucus partitioning and volatility. This is precisely why group (B) data is not optional for a threshold/potency model.

### Gaps
- No repertoire-wide study demonstrating EC50 ↔ threshold correlation across diverse chemical classes was found; the strong correlations reported are within-series (musks) and single-receptor (OR5A2) results, which risks over-generalization.
- Full text of the *Cell Systems* 2026 combinatorial-coding paper, and of "Single olfactory receptors set odor detection thresholds," could not be retrieved (403 / reCAPTCHA).

## (B) Free physicochemical / volatility datasets: vapor pressure, Henry's law, logP, boiling point, Abraham descriptors

### Takeaway
There is good free coverage: Sander's Henry's law compilation (46,434 values / 10,173 species) and the EPA OPERA/CompTox + PHYSPROP experimental sets (>43,000 compounds across VP, BP, logKow, Henry's law, water solubility) are the workhorses; GeckoQ adds ~31,637 DFT-computed saturation vapor pressures for atmospheric oxidation products; PUFFIN supplies a curated experimental VP set (1,852 molecules / 9,260 temperature-dependent records). Abraham solute descriptors are openly accessible from the UFZ-LSER database. Licenses are mostly permissive or public-domain-ish for the US-government sources, but several need direct confirmation.

### Cited Findings

**Henry's law — Sander compilation**
- Sander, "Compilation of Henry's law constants (version 5.0.0) for water as solvent," *Atmospheric Chemistry and Physics* 23:10901, 2023: **46,434 values** of Henry's law constants for **10,173 species**, from **995 references**, converted to a uniform format — [ACP 2023](https://acp.copernicus.org/articles/23/10901/2023/)
- Available at https://www.henrys-law.org; it is a *living review* that supersedes the obsolete Sander (2015) version — [ACP 2023](https://acp.copernicus.org/articles/23/10901/2023/); earlier v4.0 — [ACP 2015](https://acp.copernicus.org/articles/15/4399/2015/)
- ACP is a Copernicus open-access journal (CC-BY family), so the compilation article is freely reusable with attribution; the exact license on the bulk data files at henrys-law.org was **not confirmed** in this pass — verify before redistribution.

**EPA CompTox / OPERA / PHYSPROP**
- OPERA is a free and open-source QSAR application (command line + GUI) providing predictions plus applicability-domain and accuracy assessment for physicochemical properties and environmental fate endpoints; source and compiled releases at https://github.com/kmansouri/OPERA — [OPERA GitHub](https://github.com/kmansouri/OPERA)
- OPERA includes models for **vapor pressure** and **Henry's law constant**, updated with the latest publicly available datasets (including PFAS) to improve predictivity and applicability-domain coverage — [EPA Science Inventory: OPERA models supporting regulatory needs](https://cfpub.epa.gov/si/si_public_record_report.cfm?Lab=CCTE&dirEntryId=356953)
- Training data provenance: experimental data for the Henry's law constant model were downloaded from the EPI Suite data webpage and originate from **PHYSPROP** — [OPERA Henry's law QMRF](https://comptox.epa.gov/dashboard-api/ccdapp1/qmrfdata/file/by-modelid/19); vapor pressure model QMRF — [OPERA VP QMRF](https://comptox.epa.gov/dashboard-api/ccdapp1/qmrfdata/file/by-modelid/30)
- PHYSPROP contains experimental data for **more than 43,000 compounds**: melting points, boiling points, water solubility, log Kow, **vapor pressure**, pKa, **Henry's law constant**, and atmospheric OH radical rate constants; built by Syracuse Research Corporation — [PHYSPROP Database (EPI Suite docs)](https://episuite.dev/assets/help/ecosar/physprop_database.html)
- OPERA predictions are distributed through EPA's CompTox Chemicals Dashboard and NTP's Integrated Chemical Environment — [EPA Science Inventory: OPERA open-source QSAR models](https://cfpub.epa.gov/si/si_public_record_report.cfm?dirEntryId=359908&Lab=CCTE&sitype=PR)
- **Quality caveat, stated in the literature:** "the datasets upon which EPI Suite models were originally built contain numerous errors and the models used in predictions are not open-source," which is why open re-implementations (OPERA) were built on the EPI Suite *experimental* property sets — [search-surfaced statement, EPI Suite/OPERA context](https://github.com/kmansouri/OPERA); see also [In silico structure predictions for non-targeted analysis, PMC9365522](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9365522/)
- Legacy download location for the EPI Suite experimental data was http://esc.syrres.com/interkow/EpiSuiteData.htm — [OPERA Henry's law QMRF](https://comptox.epa.gov/dashboard-api/ccdapp1/qmrfdata/file/by-modelid/19). **This SRC host is a likely dead/moved link;** the live route today is the EPA CompTox Dashboard downloads and the `episuite.dev` documentation site — treat the syrres.com URL as historical.

**GeckoQ — computed saturation vapor pressures**
- Besel, Todorović, Kurtén, Rinke, Vehkamäki et al., "Atomic structures, conformers and thermodynamic properties of 32k atmospheric molecules," *Scientific Data* 2023: **31,637** atmospherically relevant molecules from α-pinene, toluene and decane oxidation — [Sci Data s41597-023-02366-x](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-023-02366-x); [PMC10338534](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10338534/)
- Conformer sampling with COSMOconf; thermodynamic properties by DFT with COSMO, including **saturation vapor pressure pSat [Pa]**, chemical potential [kJ/mol] and free energy in mixture [kJ/mol]; geometries of ~7 million conformers included — [Sci Data s41597-023-02366-x](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-023-02366-x)
- Indexed as a citable dataset — [research.fi GeckoQ record](https://research.fi/en/results/dataset/8e6954a1-3380-4694-9cb6-2dafd81be290); Aalto research portal — [Aalto](https://research.aalto.fi/en/publications/atomic-structures-conformers-and-thermodynamic-properties-of-32k-/)
- Caveat: these are **COSMO-RS/DFT computed** pSat values for highly functionalized oxidation products, not experimental measurements on odorants — chemical-space mismatch with typical fragrance/odorant molecules is real. Related similarity analysis — [arXiv 2406.18171](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.18171v1)

**PUFFIN and other ML vapor-pressure sets**
- PUFFIN (Santana et al., *Chemical Engineering Science*, 2023/2024): "A path-unifying feed-forward interfaced network for vapor pressure prediction" — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S000925092301179X)
- As reused by a 2026 multitask study, the PUFFIN VP set comprises **1,852 unique molecules / 9,260 temperature-dependent records**, with license "as stated in the original source" — [Safe Multitask Molecular Graph Networks for Vapor Pressure and Odor Threshold Prediction, arXiv 2601.16426](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.16426)

**Abraham descriptors / LSER**
- The **UFZ-LSER database** is openly accessible at https://www.ufz.de/lserd and is the key resource for experimentally derived Abraham solute descriptors; citable as "UFZ-LSER database 4.1.1 [Internet], Leipzig, Germany, Helmholtz Centre for Environmental Research-UFZ" — [UFZ LSER Database](https://www.ufz.de/lserd/); [PMC10685450](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10685450/)
- Partition ratios in UFZ-LSER are computed from experimentally determined or calculated Abraham descriptors via poly-parameter linear free-energy relationships (PP-LFERs) — [PMC10685450](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10685450/)
- Documented limitations: cannot calculate partition ratios for charged molecules, surfactants, or molecules >1000 Da (returns error messages in downloaded data) — [PMC10685450](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10685450/)
- Practical note: for odorants (small, neutral, volatile) these limitations are largely irrelevant — this is close to an ideal descriptor source for the odorant chemical space. The bulk-download license/terms were **not confirmed**; "openly accessible" is the strongest sourced claim.
- Air–blood / air–water partitioning LSER models built on these descriptors — [PMC10685450](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10685450/); LDPE–water LSER calibration with experimental partition coefficients — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0928098722000227)

**NIST WebBook / PubChem** — I did not verify NIST Chemistry WebBook's current terms or PubChem experimental-property coverage counts in this pass. Both are free to access; NIST WebBook historically restricts systematic/bulk redistribution, and PubChem aggregates third-party depositor data with per-source terms. **Do not assume either is freely redistributable for ML training without checking.** Gap.

### Inferences
- A defensible free feature stack for odorant potency: experimental VP/BP/logKow/Henry's law from PHYSPROP-via-CompTox (US federal, effectively public domain), OPERA predictions to fill gaps with applicability-domain flags, Sander v5.0.0 for Henry's law where experimental values exist, and UFZ-LSER Abraham descriptors as physically interpretable features. GeckoQ only if the model must extrapolate to heavily oxidized, low-volatility species.
- OPERA's applicability-domain and accuracy outputs are a differentiator: they let a downstream potency model propagate feature uncertainty rather than treating an imputed VP as ground truth.
- The single largest data-hygiene risk in group (B) is circularity: PHYSPROP experimental values feed OPERA, OPERA predictions populate CompTox, and papers then "validate" against CompTox values. Provenance flags (experimental vs predicted) must be preserved per-record.

### Gaps
- Bulk-download license for henry's-law.org data files, UFZ-LSER, NIST WebBook, and PubChem experimental properties — all unconfirmed. These are the ones to check before any commercial use.
- Exact record counts per property in the CompTox/PHYSPROP experimental VP and Henry's-law sets (the >43,000 figure is compounds across all properties, not VP specifically).
- The esc.syrres.com EPI Suite data URL is almost certainly dead; the current canonical download location was not confirmed.

## How ODT-prediction work has used volatility, and the state of free ODT datasets

### Takeaway
Volatility enters ODT modelling in two ways: as an explicit input feature (experimental or predicted vapor pressure and boiling point alongside Abraham solvation descriptors) and structurally, via multitask learning that predicts VP and ODT jointly. Free ODT target data exists at the ~1,000–1,800 compound scale, but it is assembled from handbooks and regulatory tables whose licenses are murky — which is the binding constraint, not the science.

### Cited Findings
- Abraham and colleagues established the use of solvation descriptors for terpenes and prediction of nasal pungency thresholds, and produced "An Algorithm for 353 Odor Detection Thresholds in Humans" — [ResearchGate record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/51696432_An_Algorithm_for_353_Odor_Detection_Thresholds_in_Humans)
- Sun, Jia & Xi (SSRN preprint, July 2026), "A structure- and MS²-based machine learning framework for predicting odor thresholds and descriptors of organic and inorganic odorants": structure-based model trained on **1,741 compounds** with ODTs spanning **10⁻⁹ to 10⁸ mg/m³**; test R² ≥ 0.65 for thresholds; an MS²-based threshold model reached accuracy 0.81 — [SSRN 7070562](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7070562)
- Reported structure–threshold drivers: low ODTs are linked to hydrophilic surface areas, surface polarity and polarizability, while high thresholds are mainly governed by molecular size and scaffold shape — [SSRN 7070562](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7070562)
- Multitask VP + ODT modelling: "Safe Multitask Molecular Graph Networks for Vapor Pressure and Odor Threshold Prediction" uses a degree-aware GNN backbone with atom/bond features and **temperature conditioning**, outperforming fingerprint baselines on both properties — [arXiv 2601.16426](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.16426)
- That paper's ODT corpus: **1,042 unique molecules / 1,042 molecule-medium records (598 air / 444 water)**, publication years 1935–2024 with 14.8% missing year annotations; structures canonicalized with RDKit, targets harmonized to log₁₀ space, Bemis–Murcko scaffold split 80/10/10 with a frozen split CSV keyed by SMILES released alongside code — [arXiv 2601.16426](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.16426)
- Its stated licensing posture is an explicit warning about this data class: sources are "publicly accessible regulatory and literature tables (government reports, handbooks, database excerpts, and publisher-permitted summaries)," and "when licenses are unclear we retain only numeric targets and citation pointers, and do not redistribute raw pages; our release includes scripts that re-download from original URLs where permitted" — [arXiv 2601.16426](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.16426)
- Neural networks for odor intensity have been trained on physicochemical features including **experimental boiling point and vapor pressure** plus geometric descriptors — [A quantitative framework for predicting odor intensity across molecule and mixtures, PubMed 40832259](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40832259/)
- Other free-to-read ODT modelling work: transfer learning for perfume ODT values — [Heliyon 2023](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2405844023080210); [PMC10589844](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10589844/); Monte Carlo method ODT prediction — [Ecotox Environ Saf](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0147651316303013); ordinal odor-strength prediction for perfumery components — [RSC Adv 2026](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2026/ra/d6ra01805j); [arXiv 2512.08683](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.08683); crop-aroma odor contribution/threshold models — [Metabolites 15:747](https://doi.org/10.3390/metabo15110747); [PMC12654368](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12654368/)
- Background on threshold definitions and units — [Odor detection threshold (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odor_detection_threshold)

### Inferences
- The air/water medium split (598/444 in the multitask corpus) is the concrete form the volatility problem takes: an ODT measured in water and one measured in air are not the same quantity, and converting between them requires exactly the Henry's law constants from Sander or PHYSPROP. Medium must be a modelled covariate, not silently pooled.
- Temperature conditioning in the multitask model, plus VP's strong temperature dependence, implies any ODT model with gas-phase targets should carry temperature as an input; most handbook ODT values do not report it, which is an irreducible label-noise source.
- Multitask VP+ODT is the most directly reusable design for the user's project: it uses abundant VP labels as an auxiliary task to regularize the scarce ODT head, and the same trick should transfer to receptor EC50 as a third head.
- Licensing asymmetry is the practical bottleneck: free receptor data (M2OR, Apache-2.0) and free physicochemical data (US federal) are both usable, but the *perceptual threshold* labels are the legally awkward layer. Reproducing the "numeric targets plus citation pointers" strategy is the defensible pattern.

### Gaps
- Whether the Sun/Jia/Xi 1,741-compound ODT dataset is actually released (SSRN preprint; deposit location and license unknown).
- Whether the arXiv 2601.16426 code/split release is publicly resolvable — the paper describes a release with commit hashes and checksums but I did not verify a live repository URL.
- Abraham's 353-compound ODT algorithm: the underlying compound table's availability and license were not verified (only a ResearchGate record surfaced, which is not a primary source).
- No free, well-licensed, large (>2,000 compound) ODT dataset with per-record provenance was found; this remains the field's main open data gap.
