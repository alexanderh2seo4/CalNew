# Freely accessible human odor detection/recognition threshold (ODT) datasets for ML (as of 2026-09-25)

Scope note: counts marked "(researcher count)" were computed on 2026-09-25 by downloading the cited file and counting rows. Everything else is quoted or paraphrased from the linked source. "Free" means free to access. It does not mean free to reuse; licence status is listed separately for each entry.

## Key Question 1: Which classic compilations exist, and which can actually be downloaded for free?

### Takeaway
Only a handful of threshold sources are both free and machine-readable: the Abraham 2012 set in pyrfume-data (268 CIDs with SMILES), the Nagata/Japan MoE triangle-odor-bag table (223 compounds, PDF only), PubChem's "Odor Threshold" heading (HSDB + Haz-Map, 511 CIDs, free text), the US EPA 1992 HAP guide (public, but a scanned PDF), and the Leffingwell water-threshold web page (268 compounds, no licence). The big authoritative compilations are all paywalled or commercial: Devos 1990, van Gemert 2003/2011, AIHA 2013, Amoore & Hautala 1983, Ruth 1986, ASTM DS 48A. So is Czerny 2008, which covers 84 compounds, not 1000+. The Leibniz-LSB@TUM database is free to view, but its terms forbid systematic downloading without permission.

### Cited Findings

#### A. pyrfume-data `abraham_2012` (best ready-to-use free ML file)
- The repo contains folder `abraham_2012` with files `ThresholdsAbraham2011.xls` (raw, "Data scraped from in-line tables"), `behavior.csv`, `molecules.csv`, `stimuli.csv`, `main.py` and benchmarks. The source is Abraham, Sánchez-Moreno, Cometto-Muñiz & Cain, "An Algorithm for 353 Odor Detection Thresholds in Humans", DOI 10.1093/chemse/bjr094. — [pyrfume manifest](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pyrfume/pyrfume-data/main/abraham_2012/manifest.toml); [folder](https://github.com/pyrfume/pyrfume-data/tree/main/abraham_2012)
- The pyrfume-data repository is MIT-licensed. Only three of its ~60 dataset folders carry a "threshold" tag: `abraham_2012`, `jones_2019` (mouse receptor minimum activating concentrations, so not human ODT) and `keller_2012`. — [pyrfume-data README](https://github.com/pyrfume/pyrfume-data); [jones_2019 manifest](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pyrfume/pyrfume-data/main/jones_2019/manifest.toml)
- (Researcher count) `behavior.csv` has 268 rows, one per unique PubChem CID. Target column: `Log (1/ODT)`, range −2.78 to 6.11. It also holds the Abraham solvation descriptors E, S, A, B, L, V and indicator variables (AL, AC, ES, C1, HS, ...) plus a `Train` flag. `molecules.csv` carries CID, IsomericSMILES and IUPAC name. — [behavior.csv](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pyrfume/pyrfume-data/main/abraham_2012/behavior.csv); [molecules.csv](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pyrfume/pyrfume-data/main/abraham_2012/molecules.csv)
- (Researcher count) `main.py` averages duplicate entries per CID and records how many were merged: "There are some duplicate entries so average over duplicates". The `Duplicates` column shows 211 CIDs with one value, 37 with 2, 15 with 3 and 5 with 4, which is about 350 raw rows collapsed to 268 CIDs. The 268-sample figure is also what the NOSE paper (2026) reports for this set. — [main.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pyrfume/pyrfume-data/main/abraham_2012/main.py); [NOSE arXiv 2604.10452](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.10452v2)
- About the paper: 193 Nagata triangle-bag ODTs were fit as log(1/ODT) with R² = 0.748, SD 0.830 log units. ODTs from Cometto-Muñiz & Cain, Cometto-Muñiz & Abraham, and Hellman & Small "could be put on the same scale" as Nagata's, giving 353 ODTs with R² = 0.759 and SD = 0.819. Indicator variables cover aldehydes, acids, unsaturated esters and mercaptans. Published in Chem Senses 37(3), March 2012. — [OUP abstract](https://academic.oup.com/chemse/article-abstract/37/3/207/325917); [eScholarship record](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/9b72q2v9)
- The paper's table of Nagata values is titled "Nagata values of odor detection thresholds (ppm)", so the unit is ppm (v/v) in air. — [ResearchGate table](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/Nagata-values-of-odor-detection-thresholds-ppm-used-to-obtain-eq-5_tbl2_51696432)

#### B. Nagata 2003, Japan Ministry of the Environment (triangle odor bag method)
- PDF: "Measurement of Odor Threshold by Triangle Odor Bag Method", Yoshio Nagata, Japan Environmental Sanitation Center, pp. 118–127 of the MoE Odor Measurement Review. — [env.go.jp PDF](https://www.env.go.jp/en/air/odor/measure/02_3_2.pdf) (live 2026-09-25)
- 223 substances were measured between 1976 and 1988. Values run from 0.77 ppt (isoamyl mercaptan) to 1500 ppm (propane). Units are ppm v/v, detection thresholds. — [same PDF](https://www.env.go.jp/en/air/odor/measure/02_3_2.pdf)
- Method: triangle odor bag, a panel of 6 screened and trained panelists (T&T olfactometer screening, ages 20–50). Threshold = primary-sample concentration / odor concentration. Primary samples were verified by GC-FID/FPD/FTD, the indophenol method, or GC-MS. Amines, fatty acids, skatole and indole were corrected for bag recovery. For odorants measured more than once, "the geometric mean of each observed value was taken as the threshold". Max/min ratio across repeat measurements was "about 5 at the maximum". — [same PDF](https://www.env.go.jp/en/air/odor/measure/02_3_2.pdf)
- Identifiers: Table 2 gives substance names only; no CAS or SMILES were seen. The PDF's text layer splits names and values into separate runs, so extraction needs manual alignment (observed while extracting text from the PDF). — [same PDF](https://www.env.go.jp/en/air/odor/measure/02_3_2.pdf)
- Nagata values are the de facto "Asian/triangle bag" reference. A 2025 review found that picking Nagata thresholds versus other sources changed which building-material odorants were ranked key. — [Building & Environment 2025, ADS abstract](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025BuEnv.28013122R/abstract)

#### C. PubChem "Odor Threshold" heading (HSDB + Haz-Map)
- (Researcher count via PUG-View `annotations/heading/JSON?heading=Odor%20Threshold`) The heading has 819 annotation records (433 from HSDB, 386 from Haz-Map) linked to 511 unique CIDs, with 1,193 data items. — [PUG-View endpoint](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/rest/pug_view/annotations/heading/JSON?heading=Odor%20Threshold)
- Values are free-text strings, for example "Odor low= 9.00 mg/cu m; Odor high= 9.00 mg/cu m; Irritating Concn= 2.0 mg/cu m" for iodine. They mix air and water, detection and recognition, and low and high values. Unit strings: ppm (684 items), mg/cu m (242), mg/L (97), mg/m3 (38), ppb (41), µg/L (23). — [PUG-View endpoint](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/rest/pug_view/annotations/heading/JSON?heading=Odor%20Threshold)
- Most-cited primary sources inside the HSDB records: Fazzalari (ed.), ASTM "Compilation of Odor and Taste Threshold Values Data" (152), Ruth 1986 Am Ind Hyg Assoc J 47:A142–151 (145), Verschueren handbook (~92), USCG CHRIS (48), Patty's (~50+), Amoore & Hautala 1983 (~36 incl. PMID 6376602), Fenaroli/Burdock (~16). — [PUG-View endpoint](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/rest/pug_view/annotations/heading/JSON?heading=Odor%20Threshold)
- Licence: HSDB records point to the NLM web policies page. Haz-Map records carry no LicenseURL in PubChem. — [NLM web policies](https://www.nlm.nih.gov/web_policies.html); [Haz-Map About](https://haz-map.com/About)
- Haz-Map (Jay A. Brown, MD) draws on textbooks, journal articles, ACGIH TLV Documentation and NLM databases. — [Haz-Map About](https://haz-map.com/About); [NLM Tech Bull 2003](https://www.nlm.nih.gov/pubs/techbull/jf03/jf03_hazmap.html)

#### D. US EPA 1992, "Reference Guide to Odor Thresholds for Hazardous Air Pollutants Listed in the Clean Air Act Amendments of 1990"
- EPA/600/R-92/047, March 1992. Prepared by TRC Environmental Consultants for EPA's Air Risk Information Support Center. NTIS PB-92-239516. — [EPA Science Inventory](https://cfpub.epa.gov/si/si_public_record_report.cfm?Lab=NCEA&dirEntryId=40610); [OSTI record](https://www.osti.gov/biblio/7008820)
- Free on NEPIS (Dockey 2000BHG5). The PDF (about 3.2 MB) is a scan with no text layer; PDFKit extraction returned nothing in the researcher's test, so it needs OCR. — [NEPIS](https://nepis.epa.gov/Exe/ZyPURL.cgi?Dockey=2000BHG5.TXT)
- Scope and method: the 189 CAAA hazardous air pollutants. Published values were screened against 13 quality criteria: panel of at least 6, sensitivity screening, calibration, forced choice, ascending presentation, concentration steps no larger than 3-fold, analytical measurement, detection vs recognition, and others. The guide reports "the geometric mean value, based on all accepted values, or recommended best estimate" to 2 significant figures, in mg/m³ and ppm. Table 2-1 lists CAS, name, formula, MW, citations, individual values, threshold type, geometric mean and odor character. — [NEPIS text via WebFetch](https://nepis.epa.gov/Exe/ZyPURL.cgi?Dockey=2000BHG5.TXT); [HERO record](https://hero.epa.gov/reference/12120/)

#### E. Leffingwell & Associates, "Odor & Flavor Detection Thresholds in Water (In Parts per Billion)"
- URL: `http://www.leffingwell.com/odorthre.htm`, a frameset whose data sit in `odor.htm`. HTTPS fails TLS verification ("unable to verify the first certificate"); plain HTTP works. The HTML was generated 4/26/1999. — [odorthre.htm](http://www.leffingwell.com/odorthre.htm); [odor.htm](http://www.leffingwell.com/odor.htm)
- (Researcher count) 286 FEMA-numbered rows: 268 with an odor threshold and 71 with a flavor threshold, in ppb in water. Some values are ranges, e.g. acetaldehyde 15–120 and benzaldehyde 350–3500. Identifiers are the FEMA number and names/synonyms; there is no CAS or SMILES. — [odor.htm](http://www.leffingwell.com/odor.htm)
- The reference list includes Buttery/Guadagni/Teranishi (USDA) papers. Reference 125 reads "Threshhold detection values abstracted from a more extensive list included in FlavorBase 98", a commercial Leffingwell product. The page carries no explicit reuse licence. — [odor.htm](http://www.leffingwell.com/odor.htm)
- Not the same thing: pyrfume's `leffingwell` dataset is odor descriptor labels, not thresholds, and ships a LICENSE with "use restrictions according to the terms of John Leffingwell and Google". — [pyrfume leffingwell manifest](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pyrfume/pyrfume-data/main/leffingwell/manifest.toml)

#### F. Leibniz-LSB@TUM Odorant Database (Kreissl, Mall, Steinhaus P., Steinhaus M.)
- Version 1.2 (2022): "more than 1700 odorants extracted from more than 700 publications". It includes "odor threshold values in different matrices", retention indices and odor qualities. Free online since 2019. — [DB start page](https://www.leibniz-lsb.de/en/databases/leibniz-lsbtum-odorant-database/start/); [Preface](https://www.leibniz-lsb.de/en/databases/leibniz-lsbtum-odorant-database/preface)
- Thresholds were "determined according to Eur. Food Res. Technol. 2008, 228, 265", i.e. the Czerny et al. protocol. The identifier is CAS RN; no SMILES or InChI is mentioned. The interface shows at most 20 search results and describes no export function. — [User guide](https://www.leibniz-lsb.de/en/databases/leibniz-lsbtum-odorant-database/user-guide)
- Terms: "Copyright © 2019-2025". Scientific dissemination with acknowledgement is allowed. Systematic downloading of the database contents, incorporating the data into similar products, or reproducing the database (or parts of it) in any format requires prior written permission. — [Copyright and Citation](https://www.leibniz-lsb.de/en/databases/leibniz-lsbtum-odorant-database/copyright-and-citation)

#### G. Czerny et al. 2008 (Eur Food Res Technol 228:265–273)
- 84 key food odorants were re-evaluated, 10 of them for the first time. Recognition thresholds in water range from 0.00076 µg/L (bis(2-methyl-3-furyl) disulfide) to 2,000,000 µg/L (ethanol). The paper also defines an aroma language. So it covers **84 compounds, not 1000+**. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00217-008-0931-x); [ResearchGate table "Odour thresholds of key food odorant (µg/L of water)"](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/Odour-thresholds-of-key-food-odorant-lg-L-of-water_tbl1_225447154)
- Access: Springer paywall. Table images are visible on ResearchGate and a copy is on academia.edu (legal status unclear). — [academia.edu](https://www.academia.edu/59050717/Re_investigation_on_odour_thresholds_of_key_food_aroma_compounds_and_development_of_an_aroma_language_based_on_odour_qualities_of_defined_aqueous_odorant_solutions)

#### H. Dunkel et al. 2014 (Angew Chem Int Ed 53:7124–7143)
- A meta-analysis defining 226 key food odorants (OAV ≥ 1 in at least one of 227 food samples), out of roughly 10,000 food volatiles. Paywalled at Wiley. — [PubMed 24939725](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24939725/); [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/anie.201309508)

#### I. Devos, Patte, Rouault, Laffort & van Gemert 1990, "Standardized Human Olfactory Thresholds" (IRL Press/OUP)
- Described as "the first homogeneous inventory of weighted and averaged human olfactory thresholds for over 500 chemicals". INRS records 529 substances drawn from about 120 references. — [OUP product page](https://global.oup.com/academic/product/standardized-human-olfactory-thresholds-9780199631469); [INRS record](https://portaildocumentaire.inrs.fr/Default/doc/SYRACUSE/74533/standardized-human-olfactory-thresholds-seuils-olfactifs-normalises-chez-l-homme?_lg=fr-FR)
- Not free: a book, now on Oxford Academic behind a paywall (DOI 10.1093/oso/9780199631469.001.0001). #### J. van Gemert, "Odour Thresholds: Compilations of Odour Threshold Values in Air, Water and Other Media"
- Second enlarged and revised edition, Oliemans Punter & Partners BV, Zeist, 2011. A separate 2011 BACIS title, "Compilation of Odour Threshold Values in Air and Water", is also cited. - Authors S.S. Murnane, A.H. Lehocky and P.D. Owens. AIHA, Falls Church VA, 182 pp. ISBNs 9781935082613 and 9781621987987. Sold as an eBook and through Knovel. — [WorldCat](https://search.worldcat.org/title/odor-thresholds-for-chemicals-with-established-health-standards/oclc/860899539)

#### M. Other classic or regulatory compilations
- Ruth 1986, "Odor thresholds and irritation levels of several chemical substances: a review", Am Ind Hyg Assoc J 47:A142–A151. It tabulates about 450 substances with odor descriptions, thresholds and irritant concentrations. Paywalled, but it is the second most-cited source inside PubChem/HSDB. — [INRS record](https://portaildocumentaire.inrs.fr/Default/doc/SYRACUSE/59016/odor-thresholds-and-irritation-levels-of-several-chemical-substances-a-review-seuils-olfactifs-et-co?_lg=fr-FR); [PUG-View](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/rest/pug_view/annotations/heading/JSON?heading=Odor%20Threshold)
- INRS ND 2221 (Falcy & Malard, 2005), "Comparaison des seuils olfactifs de substances chimiques avec des indicateurs de sécurité utilisés en milieu professionnel". Covers more than 293 substances, free PDF, © INRS. — [INRS](https://www.inrs.fr/media.html?refINRS=ND+2221)
- ASTM DS 48A (Fazzalari, ed.), "Compilation of Odor and Taste Threshold Values Data". A commercial ASTM publication and the most-cited source in HSDB odor-threshold records. — [PUG-View](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/rest/pug_view/annotations/heading/JSON?heading=Odor%20Threshold)
- Keller et al. 2012 (BMC Neurosci): pyrfume `keller_2012` includes "Detection thresholds for 3 odorants" measured across a diverse metropolitan population. This is useful for inter-individual variance, not for structure-activity modelling. — [manifest](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pyrfume/pyrfume-data/main/keller_2012/manifest.toml)
- Wakayama et al. 2019 (Ind Eng Chem Res, Kao): dose-response curve parameters (I_max, C, D) for perfumery raw materials, "scraped from supplemental pdf". (Researcher count: 313 molecules.) These are suprathreshold intensity data that can serve as a potency proxy. — [pyrfume wakayama_2019 manifest](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pyrfume/pyrfume-data/main/wakayama_2019/manifest.toml)
- Cometto-Muñiz & Abraham psychophysical series, e.g. concentration–response functions for homologous n-alcohols. Full text is free on UC eScholarship. These are the high-quality VDD/forced-choice values that Abraham 2012 rescaled onto the Nagata scale. — [eScholarship PDF](https://escholarship.org/content/qt8dj9f4b2/qt8dj9f4b2_noSplash_3539a1759a1358516f817fc5140a5d8a.pdf); [OUP abstract](https://academic.oup.com/chemse/article-abstract/37/3/207/325917)
- Flavornet: pyrfume's deposition is tagged only "human;odorCharacter". No threshold data was found in it. — [pyrfume-data](https://github.com/pyrfume/pyrfume-data)
- China: GB/T 14675 is the national triangle odor bag *method* standard, not a threshold dataset. — [MEE standard page](https://english.mee.gov.cn/Resources/standards/Air_Environment/air_method/200809/t20080924_129341.shtml)
- ECHA/IUCLID: registration data are published "as provided" by industry and ECHA does not verify their accuracy. The bulk "REACH Study Results" set is distributed through the IUCLID site, but the terms page returned 403 to the fetcher. — [ECHA Legal Notice](https://echa.europa.eu/en-US/legal-notice); [Get IUCLID data](https://iuclid6.echa.europa.eu/get-iuclid-data)

### Inferences
- The only sources that are free, reasonably clean, and legally low-risk for commercial ML are pyrfume `abraham_2012`, the Li et al. 2025 Zenodo set (CC BY 4.0; see KQ2), EPA 1992 (a US government work, so presumably public domain, but it needs OCR), PubChem/HSDB (NLM public-domain policy, but free text needing parsing), and possibly Nagata/MoE (Japanese government terms not verified).
- pyrfume's MIT licence covers pyrfume's own code and packaging. It probably cannot relicense numbers taken from an OUP-copyrighted paper. Raw facts are generally not copyrightable in the US, but EU sui generis database rights could apply to substantial extraction from compilations such as Leibniz-LSB or AIHA. Treat this as a risk flag, not legal advice.
- The AIHA 2nd edition reports min–max ranges per chemical rather than a geometric mean. Using it as a regression target means choosing an aggregation (geometric midpoint or lower bound), and Oliveira 2023 chose the lowest value (see KQ2).
- Leffingwell, Czerny and Leibniz-LSB report thresholds in water (ppb or µg/L, food orthonasal). Nagata, EPA, AIHA, Abraham and most of HSDB report thresholds in air (ppm v/v or mg/m³). Mixing them needs an air–water partition (Henry's law) conversion or separate tasks.

### Gaps
- Exact number of the 189 HAPs with accepted EPA 1992 values: not verified, because the NEPIS PDF is image-only and the OSTI abstract has no count.
- Terms of use for the Japan MoE Nagata PDF (env.go.jp): not checked.
- Whether Dunkel 2014 supplementary material contains a machine-readable threshold table: not verified (paywalled).
- Whether ECHA IUCLID has a structured "odour threshold" endpoint, and the REACH Study Results licence: not verified (403). Odor thresholds often appear only in SDS section 9, which is not in the dossiers.
- The "Rumeau" compilation named in the brief could not be identified. Searches returned only the INRS/Devos/Ruth French records.
- No free Chinese or Korean national ODT dataset was found. Only method standards (GB/T 14675) turned up.
- Burdock/Fenaroli, VCF (Volatile Compounds in Food) and FlavorBase are commercial. Their threshold coverage and licence were not examined.
- AIHA 1st edition (1989) coverage was not verified.

## Key Question 2: Which recent (2018–2026) ML/QSAR odor-threshold papers released curated datasets?

### Takeaway
Only one recent ODT regression dataset was found released under an open, commercial-friendly licence: Li et al. 2025 (Metabolites), with 716 compounds, SMILES, a −log10 mg/L target and CC BY 4.0 on Zenodo. The largest curated sets are Wu/Wang/Yu 2026 on arXiv (1,042 molecules; 598 air and 444 water records) and Toropov et al. 2016/2018 (906 and 1,259 compounds). Neither has a verified public data file. Most other "odor ML" papers of 2023–2026 predict descriptor labels or odorous/odorless, not thresholds.

### Cited Findings
- **Li et al. 2025, Metabolites 15(11):747**, "Predictive Models of Odor Contribution and Thresholds for Volatiles in Identification of Novel Crop Aroma Compounds". 716 volatile compounds with thresholds, aggregated from several databases (refs 29–32), harmonized to mg/L and modelled as −log10[mg/L]. Where several values existed, "we used the median value". ECFP4–GBDT reached R² = 0.94 and RMSE = 0.44. There are also 31,459 aroma-contribution records. Released on Zenodo 17559514 and GitHub under CC BY 4.0. — [PMC12654368](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12654368/); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/metabo15110747)
- Zenodo record 17559514 holds 14 files (10.8 MB), including `threshold_data_med.csv` (23.8 KB), trained `best_model_thres.joblib`, and training scripts, under CC BY 4.0. Author: Honglun Yuan, Hainan University. — [Zenodo 17559514](https://zenodo.org/records/17559514)
- The GitHub repo expects `data/threshold_data.csv` with columns `SMILES` and `threshold` (mg/L) and trains on −log10(threshold). Weights and artifacts are also on Zenodo 17605258. — [GitHub yuanhonglun/odor_prediction_models](https://github.com/yuanhonglun/odor_prediction_models)
- **Wu, Wang & Yu 2026, arXiv 2601.16426**, "Safe Multitask Molecular Graph Networks for Vapor Pressure and Odor Threshold Prediction". 1,042 unique molecules with 1,042 records: 598 air and 444 water. Data were drawn from "publicly accessible regulatory and literature tables (government reports, handbooks, database excerpts, and publisher-permitted summaries), collected via targeted scraping and manual verification". Units (ppb, ppm, mg/m³, µg/L, ng/L) were converted to a common basis and log10. Duplicates were aggregated by log-space median, 2–2.5% tails winsorized, and IQR plus replicate count kept as uncertainty. Values span 1935–2024, and 14% lack a year. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.16426v1)
- Wu et al. on release: "when licenses are unclear we retain only numeric targets and citation pointers, and do not redistribute raw pages; our release includes scripts that re-download". The arXiv abstract page gives no code or data link, and the paper licence is CC BY-NC-ND 4.0. Single-task ODT validation MSE was about 0.60–0.61 in normalized space. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.16426v1); [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.16426)
- **Oliveira et al. 2023, Heliyon**, "A framework for predicting odor threshold values of perfumes by scientific machine learning and transfer learning". ODTs came from AIHA 2nd ed. (295 molecules), cross-referenced with a perfume database, leaving 45 compounds (ppm, air). When several values existed the authors took "the lowest odor detection threshold". The 45-compound table is in the SI. The method transfers GCN odor-descriptor embeddings into an FFNN threshold regressor. — [PMC10589844](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10589844/); [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2405844023080210)
- **Toropov et al. 2016, Ecotoxicol Environ Saf**, "Odor threshold prediction by means of the Monte Carlo method" (CORAL, SMILES-based). n = 906, units mg/m³. Train r² 0.647 (RMSE 1.18), calibration 0.610, validation 0.686 (RMSE 1.06). — [PubMed 27500544](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27500544/)
- **Toropov et al. 2018, Mol Divers**, "QSPR analysis of threshold of odor for the large number of heterogenic chemicals". 1,259 compounds with "arithmetic odor thresholds". r² was about 0.62 ± 0.02 for training and 0.62 ± 0.04 for validation over 3 splits. — [PubMed 29209954](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29209954/); [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11030-017-9800-5)
- **OpenSmell/Smellability** (GitHub) predicts ODT from structure using "Abraham et al. (2012), accessed via Pyrfume". It reports R² ≈ 0.575 and itself points to Yuan's repo as more accurate. — [GitHub OpenSmell/Smellability](https://github.com/OpenSmell/Smellability)
- **NOSE, arXiv 2604.10452 (2026)**, a tri-modal olfactory embedding model. It uses Abraham 2012 (268 samples, log(1/ODT)) as a downstream potency task. Code and data are at github.com/Xianyusyy/NOSE, and the paper tabulates licences of reused resources (pyrfume MIT; Pred-O3 CC BY-NC 4.0). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.10452v2)
- **Fichtelmann et al. (2025/26)**, "Machine learning for smell: Ordinal odor strength prediction of molecular perfumery components". Over 2,000 compounds with ordinal odor strength, built from Good Scents and PubChem via a scraping script. Code is MIT-licensed on GitHub, with external validation on Keller 2016. This is a potency proxy, not ODT. — [GitHub peter-fichtelmann/odor-strength](https://github.com/peter-fichtelmann/odor-strength); [arXiv 2512.08683](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.08683); [PubMed 42158754](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42158754/)
- **Swine-slurry study, Sci Total Environ 2024.** Screened QSAR ODT models (ridge regression and random-forest regression) to assign thresholds to 27 emitted compounds, including thiirane, DMTS and DMQS, "without odor threshold records". — [PubMed 38286275](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38286275); [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0048969724005643)
- **Wang et al. 2025, Curr Res Food Sci.** Measured S-curve (3-AFC, P = 0.5) thresholds for only 10 aroma molecules in ethanol (µg/mL). "Data will be made available on request." — [PMC12152661](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12152661/)
- **Negative checks (no ODT data):**
  - Odor-Sight (JCIM 2026) is binary odorant vs odorless on 4,201 OlfactionBase compounds (CC BY 4.0, GitHub LabMolUFG/OdorSight). — [PMC13508998](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13508998/)
  - Mayhew et al. 2022 PNAS is odorous vs odorless. — [pyrfume mayhew_2022 manifest](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pyrfume/pyrfume-data/main/mayhew_2022/manifest.toml)
  - Chacko et al. 2020 Sci Rep models perceptual ratings (sweet, musky) from 55 subjects. — [PubMed 33051564](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33051564/)
  - The GNN-vs-QSAR comparison (Molecules 2025) is multi-label descriptors on 3,304 Good Scents molecules. — [PMC12693603](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12693603/)

### Inferences
- Li et al.'s R² = 0.94 is far above the roughly 0.6–0.76 reported by Abraham 2012, Toropov 2016/2018 and Smellability on similar targets. Possible causes: random splits leaking near-duplicates or homologs, or a narrower, more homogeneous (water/food) source mix. Re-validate with scaffold or group splits before relying on it.
- The ~0.8 log-unit SD of Abraham's model and the ~1.0–1.2 RMSE of Toropov's are comparable to inter-study noise for the same compound (KQ4). That suggests a practical error floor unless targets come from one harmonized protocol.
- Wu et al. 2026 is the closest published analogue to "scrape everything public and harmonize". Its 1,042-molecule figure is a useful empirical estimate of what public tables yield. Its no-redistribution stance reflects the licensing problems noted in KQ1.

### Gaps
- Where Toropov et al. got their 906 and 1,259 values (possibly van Gemert or HSDB), and whether the data are in SI: not verified (paywalled).
- Whether Wu et al. 2026 has actually published its scraping scripts or a repository: no link found.
- The exact composition of Li et al.'s 716-compound set (which databases refs 29–32 are; air vs water): not verified from the full text. The Zenodo CSV row count was not downloaded or checked.
- Older QSARs (e.g., Hau & Connell, "QSARs for Odor Thresholds of VOCs") and Zarzo 2012 (Sensors, aliphatic ODT vs carbon length) were not examined for data release.
- No Kaggle or Hugging Face dataset specifically of odor thresholds was found. Searches returned only descriptor datasets such as "Multi-Labelled SMILES Odors". — [Kaggle](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/aryanamitbarsainyan/multi-labelled-smiles-odors-dataset)

## Key Question 3: How big is the union of free threshold data, and which sources already have SMILES?

### Takeaway
The free, structure-resolvable union of human ODT data is probably about 1,000–1,500 unique molecules. Air and water thresholds together are likely under ~2,000. Without paywalled books (van Gemert, Devos) or permission from Leibniz-LSB, a clean free set is closer to ~800–1,200 molecules. Only pyrfume Abraham (268) and Li et al. 2025 (716) come with SMILES. PubChem (511 CIDs) resolves to SMILES trivially. The rest need name/CAS-to-structure mapping.

### Cited Findings
- Sources with SMILES or CIDs:
  - pyrfume `abraham_2012`: 268 CIDs with IsomericSMILES. — [molecules.csv](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pyrfume/pyrfume-data/main/abraham_2012/molecules.csv)
  - Li et al. 2025: CSV with a SMILES column, 716 compounds. — [GitHub](https://github.com/yuanhonglun/odor_prediction_models); [PMC12654368](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12654368/)
  - PubChem Odor Threshold: 511 CIDs (researcher count). — [PUG-View](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/rest/pug_view/annotations/heading/JSON?heading=Odor%20Threshold)
- Sources with CAS only:
  - EPA 1992: CAS in Table 2-1. — [NEPIS](https://nepis.epa.gov/Exe/ZyPURL.cgi?Dockey=2000BHG5.TXT)
  - AIHA 2013: CAS, 295 rows. — [SWESIAQ PDF](https://swesiaq.se/onewebmedia/Dokument/ODOR%20THRESHOLDS.pdf)
  - Leibniz-LSB: CAS RN. — [User guide](https://www.leibniz-lsb.de/en/databases/leibniz-lsbtum-odorant-database/user-guide)
- Sources with names only: Nagata, 223 names. — [env.go.jp PDF](https://www.env.go.jp/en/air/odor/measure/02_3_2.pdf)
- Sources with FEMA number plus names: Leffingwell, 268 odor thresholds. — [odor.htm](http://www.leffingwell.com/odor.htm)
- Scale reference points:
  - Wu et al. 2026 found 1,042 unique molecules across air and water in public tables. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.16426v1)
  - Toropov 2018 used 1,259 compounds. — [PubMed 29209954](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29209954/)
  - Devos 1990: 529 substances. — [INRS record](https://portaildocumentaire.inrs.fr/Default/doc/SYRACUSE/74533/standardized-human-olfactory-thresholds-seuils-olfactifs-normalises-chez-l-homme?_lg=fr-FR)
  - Leibniz-LSB: more than 1,700 odorants, though not all necessarily with thresholds. — [DB start](https://www.leibniz-lsb.de/en/databases/leibniz-lsbtum-odorant-database/start/)
  - Dunkel 2014: 226 key food odorants. — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24939725/)
- Overlap evidence: 193 of Abraham's ODTs are Nagata values, so Nagata ⊂ Abraham to a large degree. — [OUP abstract](https://academic.oup.com/chemse/article-abstract/37/3/207/325917)
- Overlap evidence: HSDB's odor threshold records largely re-cite the same few compilations (ASTM DS 48A, Ruth 1986, Amoore & Hautala 1983, Verschueren). — [PUG-View](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/rest/pug_view/annotations/heading/JSON?heading=Odor%20Threshold)

### Inferences
- The industrial-hygiene sources (EPA, AIHA, HSDB, Amoore, Ruth) are heavily redundant with each other: small solvents, sulfur compounds and amines. The food and flavor sources (Leffingwell, Czerny, Leibniz-LSB, Li 2025) add structurally different, low-threshold odorants: pyrazines, thiols, lactones, furanones. The two clusters are complementary.
- A realistic plan:
  1. Merge Abraham (pyrfume), Li 2025 (Zenodo), PubChem/HSDB (parsed), EPA 1992 (OCR), Nagata (PDF) and Leffingwell (HTML).
  2. Map names and CAS to PubChem CIDs.
  3. Deduplicate by InChIKey.
  4. Keep medium (air vs water) and type (detection vs recognition) as explicit columns.

  This probably yields about 1,000–1,300 unique structures, a similar order to Wu 2026's 1,042. It is an estimate, not a measured count.
- Odorless compounds (Mayhew 2022; Odor-Sight's 1,124 odorless) could serve as censored or "above-maximum" labels to extend the dynamic range. This is a modelling choice, not threshold data.

### Gaps
- No one has published a deduplicated union count across all of these free sources. The ~1,000–1,500 figure is an estimate.
- The overlap between Li et al.'s 716 and Abraham's 268 was not computed; the CSV was not downloaded in this session.
- How many Leibniz-LSB entries carry a threshold, and in which matrices, is unknown. The interface is limited to 20 results and bulk extraction needs permission.

## Key Question 4: How much do thresholds vary between studies, and how has prior work harmonized them?

### Takeaway
For the same compound, literature ODTs routinely differ by 2–4 orders of magnitude, and in extreme cases more than 4.6 orders (46,316-fold for butanal). Within-lab repeatability under a fixed protocol (e.g., Nagata) is about 5-fold. The method used (squeeze bottle vs olfactometer vs triangle bag vs GC-O), how the stimulus was verified analytically, and the panel population all bias results systematically. Prior work harmonizes by one of five approaches: quality screening plus geometric mean (EPA, Devos), rescaling datasets onto a reference scale (Abraham), median aggregation (Li 2025; Wu 2026 plus winsorization), taking the lowest value (Oliveira 2023), or re-measuring under one protocol (Nagata, Czerny/Leibniz-LSB).

### Cited Findings
- ODT values in compilations or published before 2003 "rarely have an accuracy of less than three orders of magnitude". Main variance sources: stimulus preparation including analytical verification, stimulus presentation, and panel selection and training. Standardized validated methods give variability of "one or two orders of magnitude", with values lower than traditionally reported. — [PubMed 37393967 (Indoor air quality – overview of methods for measuring ODTs, 2023)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37393967/)
- A 2025 review of 63 indoor VOCs found method ranking squeeze bottle > dynamic olfactometry > triangle odor bag > GC-O, with "a variability of up to 46,316 times for butanal". Western populations showed ODTs 2.15 orders higher than Asian populations. ODT choice changed which building-material odorants were ranked key. — [ADS abstract, Building & Environment 2025](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025BuEnv.28013122R/abstract)
- AIHA 2013 ranges illustrate the spread: acetaldehyde 0.0015–1,000 ppm (about 5.8 log units), acetone 0.40–11,745 ppm (about 4.5 log units), acetic acid 0.0004–204 ppm. — [SWESIAQ-hosted AIHA PDF](https://swesiaq.se/onewebmedia/Dokument/ODOR%20THRESHOLDS.pdf)
- Nagata's single-lab triangle-bag repeat measurements varied at most about 5× (max/min), and repeats were combined by geometric mean. Nagata wrote the method was adopted because thresholds "may vary considerably in the difference of measuring method". — [env.go.jp PDF](https://www.env.go.jp/en/air/odor/measure/02_3_2.pdf)
- EPA 1992 applied 13 acceptance criteria (panel ≥6, forced choice, ascending series, ≤3-fold steps, analytical concentration measurement, etc.) and reported the geometric mean of accepted values or a best estimate. — [NEPIS](https://nepis.epa.gov/Exe/ZyPURL.cgi?Dockey=2000BHG5.TXT)
- Devos et al. 1990 produced "weighted and averaged" standardized thresholds. The INRS record notes thresholds "vary from one author to another". — [OUP](https://global.oup.com/academic/product/standardized-human-olfactory-thresholds-9780199631469); [INRS](https://portaildocumentaire.inrs.fr/Default/doc/SYRACUSE/74533/standardized-human-olfactory-thresholds-seuils-olfactifs-normalises-chez-l-homme?_lg=fr-FR)
- Abraham et al. 2012 rescaled the Cometto-Muñiz/Cain, Cometto-Muñiz/Abraham and Hellman & Small datasets onto the Nagata scale before fitting one equation (353 ODTs, SD 0.819 log units). — [OUP abstract](https://academic.oup.com/chemse/article-abstract/37/3/207/325917)
- Wu et al. 2026 used a log-space median per molecule-medium pair, winsorized the 2–2.5% tails per medium, and kept IQR and replicate counts. They note values for one analyte "can span orders of magnitude" and mix detection and recognition tasks. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.16426v1)
- Li et al. 2025 used the median of multiple measurements, in mg/L. — [PMC12654368](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12654368/)
- Oliveira et al. 2023 used the lowest AIHA value, "the lowest known measurement", and acknowledged that several values per compound each come from different research. — [PMC10589844](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10589844/)
- Czerny et al. 2008 re-measured 84 key food odorants under one protocol because literature data "may differ significantly". Leibniz-LSB thresholds follow that protocol. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00217-008-0931-x); [Leibniz-LSB user guide](https://www.leibniz-lsb.de/en/databases/leibniz-lsbtum-odorant-database/user-guide)
- The threshold-calculation method alone shifts values. Reading's study of 26 aroma compounds in alcohol-free beer examined the "effect of threshold calculation method" on orthonasal and retronasal thresholds. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0963996919302662); [CentAUR PDF](https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/83299/5/08%20Apr%20Manuscript%20Reviewed%20for%20Centaur.pdf)
- Triangle odor bag and dynamic olfactometry give systematically different odor concentrations. — [ResearchGate: "Difference in the odor concentrations measured by the triangle odor bag method and dynamic olfactometry"](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/24311851_Difference_in_the_odor_concentrations_measured_by_the_triangle_odor_bag_method_and_dynamic_olfactometry)

### Inferences
- For ML, the target should be log10 of the threshold in a single molar or v/v basis per medium, with metadata columns: source, method family (triangle bag / olfactometer / squeeze bottle / GC-O / 3-AFC in water), detection vs recognition, and number of values aggregated. Either train with a source- or method-offset term, like Abraham's rescaling, or restrict the test set to one protocol family, e.g. Nagata-style air or Czerny-style water.
- Given 1–3 log units of irreducible between-study noise, evaluation should use per-compound replicate spread as an error floor. Reported R² values above ~0.8 on mixed-source data deserve scrutiny for leakage.
- Using the geometric mean or median in log space is the most common and defensible aggregation. The "lowest value" rule (Oliveira) biases toward sensitive panels and GC-O-like methods.

### Gaps
- No free, systematic, compound-level analysis of inter-study variance across all compounds (e.g., distribution of per-compound log-SD) was found. The 2025 Building & Environment review covers 63 VOCs but its data availability was not verified (ScienceDirect 403).
- The authors and data availability of the 2023 methods overview (PubMed 37393967) were not confirmed; only the abstract was seen.
