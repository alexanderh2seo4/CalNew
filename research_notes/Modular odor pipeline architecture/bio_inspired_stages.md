# Biologically/physically inspired multi-stage models of olfaction as pipeline stages

*Research current as of 2026-09-27. Items marked **[uncertain]** could not be verified from a primary source within this research pass.*

## Q1 — Transport stage: nasal airflow/mucus sorption models, air–mucus partitioning, and how Mayhew 2022 / Pellegrino 2025 encode transport

### Takeaway
The transport stage is the most mature and most empirically validated stage of the chain: a small set of cheap, interpretable physicochemical descriptors (vapor pressure, boiling point, logP, MW, shape) separates odorous from odorless molecules and, remarkably, matches a 127-feature DNN on perceived intensity (r = 0.87 both). The mechanistic CFD/sorption tradition (Mozell → Hahn/Scherer/Mozell → Keyhani/Scherer/Mozell → Zhao) provides the physics but has *not* been coupled to modern molecule→perception ML.

### Cited Findings

**Sorption / chromatographic tradition**
- Mozell's 1964 frog work showed different spatiotemporal mucosal response patterns depending on odorant, and that airflow direction altered the pattern — the origin of the "nasal cavity as gas chromatograph" analogy. The concept: upstream mucosa selectively captures highly mucus-soluble odorants, letting less soluble ones travel farther along the flow path — [Coppola 2022, BioEssays, "The sorption/chromatography hypothesis of olfactory discrimination: the rise, fall, and rebirth of a Phoenix"](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/bies.202100263); [Mozell 1974, Ann NY Acad Sci](https://nyaspubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1749-6632.1974.tb49845.x)
- Keyhani, Scherer & Mozell (1997, *J Theor Biol*) built an anatomically correct 3-D finite-element model of the human nasal cavity: steady-state Navier–Stokes for laminar flow at quiet-breathing rates, then the convective–diffusion equation for odorant concentration and wall mass flux, with boundary conditions set by **odorant solubility and diffusivity in the mucosa and mucus-layer thickness** — [PubMed 9219668](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9219668/)
- Quantitative outputs of that model: ~**10% of inspired volumetric flow** passes through the olfactory airway, roughly constant across flow rate; **total olfactory flux** (the model's integrated uptake) is "highly correlated with perceived odor intensity"; raising flow rate raises total uptake, but *fractional* uptake falls for poorly soluble odorants and rises for highly soluble ones — [PubMed 9219668](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9219668/)
- Direct measurement of the key transport parameter exists: "Experimental and numerical determination of odorant solubility in nasal and olfactory mucosa" — [PubMed 15574812](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15574812/)
- Extensions of the same CFD lineage: turbulent/laminar airflow + odorant transport during sniffing in human and rat nose (Zhao/Scherer, Ohio State) — [OSU Pure](https://ohiostate.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/numerical-modeling-of-turbulent-and-laminar-airflow-and-odorant-t/); rat nasal cavity airway-dimension effects on deposition patterns — [PLOS One 10.1371/journal.pone.0077570](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0077570); effect of normal nasal morphological variation on odorant transport to the olfactory cleft — [PubMed 36045580](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36045580/); a pilot numerical study of odorant transport to the olfactory region under ISO 16000-28 sensory evaluation conditions — [ScienceDirect S0360132323008958](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0360132323008958)
- The sorption hypothesis is **contested as a driver of quality coding**: in-vivo work found "a limited role for sniff strength in shaping primary odor representations during behavior" — [J Neurosci 33(1):79](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/33/1/79) — and a combined EOG + CFD mouse study explicitly tested the chromatographic theory with highly soluble odors — [Biology Open 8(10):bio047217](https://journals.biologists.com/bio/article/8/10/bio047217/224139/Tests-of-the-chromatographic-theory-of-olfaction). Supporting evidence on the other side: epithelial responses are tuned to odor solubility and sorption pattern — [J Neurosci 34(6):2025](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/34/6/2025). Treat sorption as an established *intensity/uptake* modulator and a *disputed* quality-coding mechanism.

**Air–mucus partitioning predictors**
- A DNN predicting **air–nasal-mucus partition coefficients** of odor compounds uses molecular surface charge density (**σ-profile from COSMO-RS**) as descriptors, motivated by the fact that in-vivo determination of air–mucus partitioning is technically infeasible — [ACS Omega 9(29):31328 (2024)](https://pubs.acs.org/acsodf/article/9/29/31328/410013/Prediction-on-Air-Nasal-Mucus-Partition); [PubMed 39072110](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39072110/)
- Molecular-dynamics-derived water/mucus partition coefficients have been computed for fish olfactory stimulants — an existence proof that MD can supply this parameter — [PMC3759373](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3759373/)
- For the related octanol–air partition ratio (K_OA), the available free/ semi-free predictors are **OPERA, EPI Suite's KOAWIN, ppLFERs, and COSMOtherm** — [Environ Toxicol Chem 10.1002/etc.5201](https://dx.doi.org/10.1002/etc.5201)

**How Mayhew 2022 encodes transport**
- Four criteria for a molecule to be odorous: (1) volatile enough to enter the air phase, (2) *non*-volatile and hydrophilic enough to sorb into the mucous layer over the olfactory epithelium, (3) hydrophobic enough to enter an OR binding pocket, (4) activate ≥1 OR. The published model predicts odorous/odorless **from criteria 1–3 alone** (transport only, no receptor model) — [PNAS 119(15):e2116576119](https://www.pnas.org/doi/full/10.1073/pnas.2116576119); [PubMed 35377807](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35377807/)
- Applying the model to an enumeration of small organic molecules gives an estimate of **≥40 billion odorous compounds**, six orders of magnitude above the conventional ~10,000 figure — [PNAS 119(15):e2116576119](https://www.pnas.org/doi/full/10.1073/pnas.2116576119)
- The companion web tool (Mainland lab) implements **three models**: (a) "Rule of Three" — structure only, odorous if MW 30–300 Da and <4 heteroatoms; (b) a **transport boundary model** — logistic regression in vapor-pressure × logP space; (c) a **transport-ML model** — gradient-boosted classifier. Inputs are MW, **Crippen logP**, heteroatom count, **vapor pressure**, **boiling point**. The repo ships a curated **1,924-molecule** dataset from the paper's supplement — [github.com/joelmainland/odor-prediction](https://github.com/joelmainland/odor-prediction)

**How Pellegrino 2025 encodes transport (intensity)**
- "A quantitative framework for predicting odor intensity across molecules and mixtures" — Pellegrino, Samoilova, Ihara, Andres, Singh, Gerkin, Koulakov, Mainland — [bioRxiv 2025.08.08.668954](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.08.08.668954v1); [PMC12363845](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12363845/); [PubMed 40832259](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40832259/)
- The **10 features** of the simplified model: five transport-related — **molecular weight, vapor pressure, boiling point, MLogP (octanol–water), alpha-area (geometric shape descriptor)** — plus five descriptors most correlated with Hill-equation parameters: **CATS2D_03_LL, SpMax8_Bh.p, MPC07, MATS7i, H2s** — [PMC12363845](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12363845/)
- Full model = **127 physicochemical descriptors** (Dragon v6), fed to a 5-layer DNN (300 units/layer, batchnorm, ReLU, MSE) — [PMC12363845](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12363845/)
- Held-out single molecules: full 127-feature model **RMSE 7.1, r = 0.87**; simplified 10-feature model **RMSE 7.2, r = 0.87**; shuffled control RMSE 20.4 (p < 0.001). The authors report the simplified model loses only ~1% of predictive performance while gaining mechanistic interpretability — [PMC12363845](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12363845/)
- **Vapor pressure was the single most important feature** by permutation analysis (p < 10⁻⁵) — [PMC12363845](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12363845/)
- Dataset: 62 odorants × ≥7 concentrations (>434 single-molecule data points); 260 mixtures (216 binary, 20 ternary, 16 five-component, 8 ten-component) with 7,623 mixture ratings; 36 panelists recruited, ≥15 per session; test–retest r = 0.97 for both singles and mixtures — [PMC12363845](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12363845/)
- Mixtures: DNN **RMSE 7.5** (shuffled control 15.8, p < 0.001); a **biophysical "primacy" model reached r = 0.85, RMSE 8.6** — i.e. a mechanistic model came within ~15% RMSE of the DNN on mixtures — [PMC12363845](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12363845/)
- The framework is pitched explicitly as replacing heuristic **odor activity values (OAV)**, and was demonstrated on "complex naturalistic odors" to identify which volatiles meaningfully contribute to aroma — [bioRxiv 2025.08.08.668954](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.08.08.668954v1)

### Inferences
- The Pellegrino result is the strongest single argument for a physically-motivated stage in a pipeline: it shows that the *transport bottleneck*, not receptor chemistry, carries most of the variance in intensity. For a potency model, 10 cheap descriptors is a defensible architecture, and the marginal value of a learned molecular embedding on top is ~1% RMSE on this dataset.
- Mayhew's criteria map almost exactly onto a pipeline's first two stages: criteria 1–2 = physical transport (headspace + air/mucus partitioning), criterion 3 = a *transport-flavoured* precondition for receptor binding, criterion 4 = the receptor stage proper. The published model deliberately stops before criterion 4, which means there is a documented, unexploited slot for a receptor stage to be bolted on.
- Practical feature sourcing for the transport stage is solved with free tools: OPERA / EPI Suite (vapor pressure, BP, logP, Henry's law, K_OA), RDKit (Crippen logP, MW, heteroatom counts, shape descriptors). COSMO-RS σ-profiles are the better but non-free route for air–mucus partitioning specifically.
- The CFD lineage (Keyhani/Scherer/Zhao) produces a per-odorant *olfactory flux* that is claimed to correlate with perceived intensity, but it needs geometry + solubility/diffusivity inputs per molecule. It is best viewed as a physical justification for why (vapor pressure, solubility) features work, rather than as a deployable stage.

### Gaps
- **I could not verify a classification accuracy or AUC for Mayhew 2022.** PNAS and ResearchGate returned 403; the GitHub repo documents no metrics. The paper's headline claim is "reliably predicts," with the 40-billion extrapolation as the demonstration. Do not quote an accuracy figure without checking the PNAS PDF directly.
- I found no published work that feeds a Keyhani/Scherer-style CFD olfactory-flux value into an ML perception model — the two literatures appear disconnected.
- No quantitative r between the 1997 model's "total olfactory flux" and measured perceived intensity was retrievable from the abstract-level sources; the correlation is asserted, not numerically given in what I could access.

## Q2 — Receptor stage: odorant→OR activity predictors, accuracy, and deorphanization coverage

### Takeaway
Two model families dominate: sequence×molecule cross-attention models (Hladiš/Topin *Receptor2Odorant* → M2OR → MolOR) and structure/docking models. All are trained on M2OR, which is broad in pairs but thin in positives; the binding problem is effectively a sparse, imbalanced binary-classification task, and **88% of documented human OR sequences remain orphan**, which is the hard ceiling on this stage.

### Cited Findings

**M2OR**
- M2OR aggregates **75,050 bioassay experiments** covering **51,395 distinct OR–molecule pairs**, with receptor sequences, molecules/mixtures, and experimental metadata; it is described as the largest such resource and is positioned as a benchmark for future OR–molecule activity ML — [Nucleic Acids Research 52(D1):D1370](https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/52/D1/D1370/7327067); [PMC10767820](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10767820/)
- Human coverage inside M2OR: **1,057 unique human OR sequences tested** against **768 distinct odorants** — [Chemical Senses, "A status report on human odorant receptors and their allocated agonists" (Lalis, Hladiš, Topin, 2024), bjae037](https://academic.oup.com/chemse/article-abstract/doi/10.1093/chemse/bjae037/7821270)

**Deorphanization coverage (the ceiling on this stage)**
- Of ~4,384 documented human OR sequences (including allelic variants): only **4.5% have agonists confirmed by dose–response**; **12%** have an agonist if screening hits are included; **88% remain to be deorphanized** — [Chemical Senses bjae037](https://academic.oup.com/chemse/article-abstract/doi/10.1093/chemse/bjae037/7821270)
- Collapsed to the gene level (**436 OR genes**), **77% have a putative agonist** — [Chemical Senses bjae037](https://academic.oup.com/chemse/article-abstract/doi/10.1093/chemse/bjae037/7821270)
- Critically for a chained pipeline: "the complete combinatorial code is only known for a handful of odorants" — i.e. *column* coverage (ORs per molecule) is far worse than *row* coverage (molecules per OR) — [Chemical Senses bjae037](https://academic.oup.com/chemse/article-abstract/doi/10.1093/chemse/bjae037/7821270)

**Sequence × molecule models**
- Hladiš, Lalis, Fiorucci & Topin, "Matching receptor to odorant with protein language and graph neural networks," **ICLR 2023**: fuses the protBERT **[CLS]** token for the receptor with a molecular graph through a GNN carrying protein–molecule-binding inductive biases; trained on a then-new **46,700 OR–molecule pair** dataset (the M2OR precursor); reported to outperform SOTA drug–target-interaction models and standard GNN baselines — [OpenReview q9VherQJd8_](https://openreview.net/forum?id=q9VherQJd8_); code: [github.com/MatejHl/Receptor2Odorant](https://github.com/MatejHl/Receptor2Odorant)
- **MolOR** (the binding stage of the Cell Systems work below): graph convolutional network for the molecule + **ESM-2** protein language model for the receptor sequence, fused by **cross-attention**; trained on **46,700 molecule–OR pairs across 1,237 receptor sequences from 11 mammalian species**; outperformed baselines including **PerceiverCPI** — [bioRxiv 2024.09.16.613334](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.09.16.613334v1.full); code: [github.com/microsoft/olfaction](https://github.com/microsoft/olfaction)
- Sanity check reported for MolOR: predicted activations distinguish functional OR genes from pseudogenes — median **3 activated ORs per odorant** for functional genes vs **1** for pseudogenes, over the **HORDE** set of ~900 human OR genes and pseudogenes — [bioRxiv 2024.09.16.613334](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.09.16.613334v1.full)

**Structure/docking-based OR models**
- ML-based modelling of ORs in their **inactive** state, with human **OR51E2** as case study — relevant if you want structure-conditioned rather than sequence-conditioned receptor models — [bioRxiv 2023.02.22.529484](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.02.22.529484.full.pdf); [PMC10207261](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10207261/)
- ML-assisted discovery of novel high-activity agonists of human **ectopic** ORs — demonstrates the screening use case — [PMC8583936](https://ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8583936)
- **Atomevo-odor**: a database for OR–odorant pairs built with multiple AI methods — a more recent alternative/complement to M2OR — [Food Chemistry, ScienceDirect S0308814625006430](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0308814625006430)

**Empirical receptor panels (ground truth, not prediction)**
- Ihara et al. screened **23 compounds against 352 human ORs** by luciferase assay — one of the densest human OR × molecule matrices available — [Chemical Senses bjaf002](https://academic.oup.com/chemse/article/doi/10.1093/chemse/bjaf002/7994243)
- High-heterologous-expression decoding of human ORs for signature odorants (2025) — ongoing expansion of the deorphanized set — [Current Biology S0960-9822(25)01211-4](https://www.cell.com/current-biology/fulltext/S0960-9822(25)01211-4)

### Inferences
- The honest reading of the receptor stage: models are good at *ranking* OR–molecule pairs within the chemical/receptor space M2OR already covers, and there is no published evidence they extrapolate to the 88% orphan receptors in a way you could trust as a pipeline input. A predicted 400-dimensional human activation vector is largely *interpolated structure*, not measured biology, for most of its entries.
- The user's stated M2OR figures (51k pairs, ~3,100 active, EC50 reproducibility ~0.54 log) are consistent with the NAR paper's 51,395 pairs. The ~0.54-log EC50 reproducibility effectively caps any regression on EC50 — a receptor-stage regressor cannot beat assay noise, which argues for binary/ordinal activity targets rather than continuous EC50 as the stage-2 output.
- The 100 µM-vs-ppb sensitivity mismatch the user raises is a real architectural problem for a *literal* chained model: in vitro EC50s sit orders of magnitude above psychophysical thresholds, so any concentration passed from stage 1 to stage 2 has to go through a learned or fitted gain, not a physically calibrated one.

### Gaps
- **I did not obtain a headline AUROC/AUPRC for MolOR or Receptor2Odorant on M2OR.** Both are reported only as "outperformed baselines" in the sources I could access. The Cell Systems page returned 403 and the ICLR PDF was not fetched. This is a metric worth pulling directly before citing.
- No source I found quantifies out-of-distribution generalization to orphan ORs (i.e. accuracy on receptors held out entirely, vs random pair splits). Given how the field usually splits data, assume reported numbers are optimistic for the orphan case unless proven otherwise.
- Cong et al. 2022, DeepOlf, and Achebouche 2022 specifically did not surface in my searches; I have no verified metrics for them.

## Q3 — Receptor → perception: does a receptor stage improve perception prediction?

### Takeaway
Two independent 2025–2026 results say yes, but by very different margins: with a *measured* receptor panel on a narrow chemical family, receptor features beat structure features decisively (R² 0.687 vs 0.514); with *predicted* receptor activations on a broad odor-descriptor benchmark, the gain is real but tiny (~0.6% AUROC, p = 0.004). The margin appears to track whether the receptor activations are measured or inferred.

### Cited Findings

**MolOR → perception (the Cell Systems 2026 paper the user asked about)**
- "Mapping the combinatorial coding between olfactory receptors and perception with deep learning" — **Seyone Chithrananda, Judith Amores, Kevin K. Yang** (Microsoft Research / UC Berkeley) — [Cell Systems S2405-4712(26)00193-6](https://www.cell.com/cell-systems/fulltext/S2405-4712(26)00193-6?rss=yes); preprint [bioRxiv 2024.09.16.613334](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.09.16.613334v1.full)
- Architecture is exactly the chained design in question: **stage 1** MolOR (GCN molecule + ESM-2 receptor + cross-attention) predicts an OR activation profile; **stage 2** concatenates that predicted activation vector with the molecular embedding to predict odor percepts — [bioRxiv 2024.09.16.613334](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.09.16.613334v1.full)
- Perception data: **GoodScents–Leffingwell (GS-LF), 5,862 molecules × 152 unique odor descriptors** — the standard quality benchmark — [bioRxiv 2024.09.16.613334](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.09.16.613334v1.full)
- **Reported gain from the receptor stage: "consistent improvements to percept prediction," quantified as a 0.6% AUROC increase using the HORDE receptor set; paired t-test with vs without OR features t = 5.91, p = 0.004** — [bioRxiv 2024.09.16.613334](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.09.16.613334v1.full)
- Receptor-panel engineering mattered: improvements were obtained using a **weighted loss in the binding model** and a panel of the **top 400 most-commonly-bound OR sequences in M2OR** — [reported via M2OR-related search result summarizing this work](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.09.16.613334v1.full)

**Measured receptor profiles → perception (Ihara/Touhara)**
- Ihara, Ijichi, Nogi, Sugiki, Kodama, S. Ihara, Shirasu, Hirokawa, Touhara, "Predicting human olfactory perception by odorant structure and receptor activation profile," *Chemical Senses* (bjaf002) — [Chemical Senses bjaf002](https://academic.oup.com/chemse/article/doi/10.1093/chemse/bjaf002/7994243)
- Design: **20 trained panelists** rated **23 structurally similar compounds** (eugenol, vanillin and derivatives) on similarity; the same 23 compounds were screened against **352 human ORs** by luciferase assay; a separate structure route used 3D shape + pharmacophore fingerprints with multiconformer generation over **941 reference odorants** — [Chemical Senses bjaf002](https://academic.oup.com/chemse/article/doi/10.1093/chemse/bjaf002/7994243)
- **Receptor-based model (elastic net) R² = 0.687 ± 0.059 on validation, using activity patterns from just 6 key ORs across 11 concentrations. Structure-based model (XGBoost) R² = 0.514 ± 0.078.** Conclusion: "OR activity profiles correlated with their perception," and the receptor route substantially outperformed structure alone in this odor space — [Chemical Senses bjaf002](https://academic.oup.com/chemse/article/doi/10.1093/chemse/bjaf002/7994243)

**Earlier receptor→perception work**
- Kowalewski & Ray, "Predicting Human Olfactory Perception from Activities of Odorant Receptors," *iScience* (2020) — [ScienceDirect S2589004220305484](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2589004220305484) *(403 on fetch; metrics not verified here)*

**Glomerular / receptor-map datasets available as stage-3 substrate**
- Burton, Wachowiak et al.: high-throughput odorant delivery + ultrasensitive imaging of **185 odorants** onto mouse dorsal OB sensory input, producing (i) consensus glomerular maps at sub-nanomolar concentrations, (ii) an atlas of glomerular sensitivities across the dorsal OB, and (iii) ~two dozen glomeruli robustly identifiable across animals via a single diagnostic odorant–concentration pair — [eLife 80470](https://elifesciences.org/articles/80470); [PMC9352350](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9352350/); [bioRxiv 2022.05.11.491539](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2022.05.11.491539.full.pdf)
- Key structural facts from that dataset: glomeruli are **extremely narrowly tuned (~25% responded to only one of the 185 odorants)** and **extremely sensitive (sub-picomolar to nanomolar)**; co-tuning and spatial sensitivity maps reflect "relatively straightforward physicochemical features of odorants" — [eLife 80470](https://elifesciences.org/articles/80470)
- Spatial transcriptomic reconstruction of the mouse glomerular map, giving OR-identity ↔ glomerular-position correspondence — [Nature Neuroscience 10.1038/s41593-022-01030-8](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-022-01030-8)
- Earlier receptor-defined glomerular sensitivity/specificity mapping — [Neuron S0896-6273(06)00819-1](https://www.cell.com/fulltext/S0896-6273(06)00819-1)

**Insect / connectome-based chained models (existence proofs for full-circuit modelling)**
- A brain-wide leaky-integrate-and-fire model over the *Drosophila* connectome reproduces sensorimotor processing — [Nature 10.1038/s41586-024-07763-9](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07763-9)
- Hemibrain-based analysis of all **1,476 lateral horn neurons**: shuffling PN→LHN connectivity reduced the number of LHNs receiving PN input of matched odor-coding valence, implying valence-specific wiring — [Science Advances adq9893](https://www.science.org/doi/full/10.1126/sciadv.adq9893)
- Lightweight data-driven spiking model of the *Drosophila* olfactory system with hardware support — a deployable-scale precedent — [Front Neurosci 2024.1384336](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnins.2024.1384336/full)
- "Sensory processing reformats odor coding around valence and dynamics" (2025) — [PubMed 41278801](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41278801/)

**Direct molecule→perception baseline to beat**
- GNN-derived **Principal Odor Map (POM)** preserves perceptual relationships and enables quality prediction for uncharacterized odorants — the reference direct model — [as summarized alongside the glomerular mapping literature](https://elifesciences.org/articles/80470)

### Inferences
- The two positive results are not in conflict but they measure different things. Ihara's R² gap is within a tightly controlled, chemically homogeneous family where 6 measured ORs carry most of the perceptual variance; MolOR's 0.6% AUROC is a broad-benchmark, predicted-activation result where the molecular embedding already captures nearly everything the predicted OR vector could add. **The receptor stage's value looks like it is bounded by the quality of the activation data, not by the architecture.**
- Concretely for the user's design: a receptor stage is most likely to pay off (a) on narrow chemical families where a measured OR panel exists, (b) as a regularizer/inductive bias rather than a bottleneck, and (c) if the activation vector is fed *alongside* the molecular embedding rather than replacing it — which is exactly what both successful papers did. Neither paper used a strict bottleneck (molecule → OR → percept with no skip connection).
- Mouse glomerular datasets (185 odorants, Burton/Wachowiak) are the best available stage-3 ground truth, but they are mouse OR repertoire and mouse concentrations; the eLife finding that glomerular co-tuning "reflects relatively straightforward physicochemical features" is a warning that a glomerular stage may re-encode the same physicochemistry stage 1 already supplies.

### Gaps
- I could not fetch the final **Cell Systems** version (403), so the 0.6% AUROC / t = 5.91 figures come from the **2024 bioRxiv preprint**. The published numbers may differ — verify before quoting.
- I found **no** study that maps mouse glomerular imaging data to *human* perceptual ratings and shows an improvement over direct molecule→perception models. The Chae/Banerjee 2019 dataset specifically did not surface.
- Kepple/Koulakov and Zhang & Sharpee receptor→perception modelling did not surface in these searches; I have no verified metrics for them and cannot confirm the user's characterization.
- No fly-connectome model has been shown to predict *human* odor quality; the connectome work is about fly valence and dynamics.

## Q4 — Intensity coding: biology and computational models

### Takeaway
Intensity has two competing computational framings: per-receptor Hill/dose–response saturation (which fits human psychophysics better than Stevens' power law over a full concentration range) and primacy/rank-based coding (which explains concentration-*invariant identity* and, in Pellegrino 2025, predicted mixture intensity at r = 0.85). Water-solubility — a transport property — predicts the Stevens exponent, which is direct evidence that intensity coding is partly a transport phenomenon.

### Cited Findings
- Human concentration→perceived-intensity curves resemble ligand–receptor binding and are well modelled by the **Hill equation**; Chastrette et al. found Hill fit human data better than Stevens' power law, Beidler's function, or Fechner's log law. Stevens was as good as Hill *at low concentrations only*; once the range included half-maximum intensity, Hill was better — [Chemical Senses 23(2):181, "Modelling the Human Olfactory Stimulus-Response Function"](https://academic.oup.com/chemse/article-abstract/23/2/181/541296)
- **Stevens exponents differ reliably across odorants and track water solubility**: a nearly perfect rank-order correlation between exponent size and odorant water-solubility, with exponents for fully water-soluble odorants ~**2.5×** those for water-insoluble odorants — [Attention, Perception & Psychophysics, 10.3758/BF03212789](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/BF03212789)
- **Primacy coding** (Koulakov and colleagues): a small, relatively stable set of the *earliest-activated* receptors encodes concentration-invariant odor identity. As concentration rises, less sensitive glomeruli are recruited later, leaving the primary set intact; absolute response magnitudes are concentration-dependent but their **ranking** is invariant — [Nature Communications, "A primacy code for odor identity"](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-017-01432-4); [PMC5684307](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5684307/)
- Behavioral support: an optogenetic masking paradigm showed animals can identify odors using information arriving **<100 ms after inhalation onset** — [Nature Communications s41467-017-01432-4](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-017-01432-4)
- Theoretical extensions: [Concentration invariant odor coding, bioRxiv 125039](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/125039v1.full); [The primacy model and the structure of olfactory space, PLOS Comp Biol 10.1371/journal.pcbi.1012379](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pcbi.1012379); [Primacy model and the evolution of the OR repertoire, bioRxiv 255661](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/255661v1.full); [Deconstructing Odorant Identity via Primacy in Dual Networks, Neural Computation 31(4):710](https://direct.mit.edu/neco/article-abstract/31/4/710/8463/Deconstructing-Odorant-Identity-via-Primacy-in)
- **Primacy used as an intensity model**: in Pellegrino 2025, a biophysical primacy model predicted **mixture** intensity at **r = 0.85, RMSE 8.6**, versus the DNN's RMSE 7.5 — a mechanistic model within striking distance of the black box — [PMC12363845](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12363845/)
- Pellegrino 2025 explicitly selected five of its ten descriptors as "most correlated with **Hill equation parameters**," i.e. the feature set is engineered against a receptor dose–response parameterization — [PMC12363845](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12363845/)
- **Single-receptor threshold setting**: in mice, "behavioral threshold is set solely by the most sensitive receptor, with no contribution from other highly sensitive receptors" — shown by genetic deletion (removing TAAR4 impairs phenylethylamine detection; removing TAAR3 does not, while TAAR4 remains). TAAR3 EC50 4.1×10⁻¹⁰ M vs TAAR4 9.9×10⁻⁹ M for isopentylamine; TAAR4 is ~8× more sensitive than TAAR3 to phenylethylamine (1.4×10⁻¹¹ M vs 1.9×10⁻¹⁰ M); TAAR5 is ~20× more sensitive to trimethylamine than TAAR3 or TAAR4 — [Nature Communications, "Single olfactory receptors set odor detection thresholds"](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-018-05129-0); [bioRxiv 341099](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/341099v1.full)
- The **EC20** point of a dose–response curve (the lower limb, not EC50) has been reported to correlate with in-vivo sensory detection threshold — relevant to which receptor parameter a pipeline should actually use — *[reported in the search-result summary of the above threshold literature; **[uncertain]** — I did not confirm this to a specific primary source]*
- Cross-species validation of the psychophysics: "Mice and humans evaluate odor stimulus strength using common psychophysical principles" — [bioRxiv 2025.08.16.669760](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.08.16.669760v1); [PubMed 40894685](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40894685/)

### Inferences
- The "single most sensitive receptor sets threshold" result is the cleanest biological justification for a **max/rank-pooling** readout over receptor activations in a potency model, rather than a sum or mean. Primacy is the same idea generalized to a top-k set.
- The Stevens-exponent↔water-solubility correlation and the vapor-pressure dominance in Pellegrino both point the same way: the *slope* of the intensity function is largely a transport property, while the *position* (threshold) is largely a receptor-affinity property. That is a natural factorization for the user's "one model for potency" — transport features for the dose–response slope, receptor/affinity features for the offset.
- Hill-vs-Stevens matters practically: if the pipeline is trained on suprathreshold ratings spanning the dynamic range, fit a saturating (Hill/sigmoid in log-concentration) function; a power law will mis-extrapolate at the top.

### Gaps
- I found no source that *derives* the Stevens exponent from receptor Hill coefficients quantitatively — the user's framing of this relation may be a hypothesis rather than a published result. The solubility↔exponent correlation is published; the Hill-coefficient↔exponent derivation is not something I could confirm.
- Spike-timing/latency-based intensity models (beyond primacy's rank-of-onset) did not surface with concrete metrics.

## Q5 — Evidence that a chained bio pipeline beats a direct molecule→perception model, and data bottlenecks

### Takeaway
As of September 2026 the honest answer is: **chaining has been shown to help, but only marginally on broad benchmarks, and no published system chains all three stages (transport → receptor → perception).** The two-stage receptor→perception chain has positive evidence (MolOR: +0.6% AUROC, p = 0.004; Ihara: R² 0.687 vs 0.514 with measured ORs). The transport→intensity chain has strong evidence that a 10-feature physical model *equals* a DNN. Nobody has put them together.

### Cited Findings
- **Receptor stage helps, small effect, broad benchmark**: MolOR-predicted OR activations concatenated with molecular embeddings gave "consistent improvements to percept prediction," +0.6% AUROC on GS-LF (5,862 molecules, 152 descriptors), paired t = 5.91, p = 0.004 — [bioRxiv 2024.09.16.613334](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.09.16.613334v1.full) / [Cell Systems S2405-4712(26)00193-6](https://www.cell.com/cell-systems/fulltext/S2405-4712(26)00193-6?rss=yes)
- **Receptor stage helps a lot, large effect, narrow benchmark, measured activations**: R² 0.687 ± 0.059 (6 measured ORs × 11 concentrations, elastic net) vs 0.514 ± 0.078 (3D structure + pharmacophore, XGBoost) on 23 eugenol/vanillin-family compounds — [Chemical Senses bjaf002](https://academic.oup.com/chemse/article/doi/10.1093/chemse/bjaf002/7994243)
- **Transport stage matches deep learning on intensity**: 10 interpretable features RMSE 7.2 / r 0.87 vs 127-feature DNN RMSE 7.1 / r 0.87; ~1% performance cost — [PMC12363845](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12363845/)
- **Transport alone suffices for the odorous/odorless gate**: Mayhew's published model uses only criteria 1–3 (volatility, mucus sorption, hydrophobic enough for a binding pocket) and omits criterion 4 (actual OR activation) — [PNAS 119(15):e2116576119](https://www.pnas.org/doi/full/10.1073/pnas.2116576119)
- **The data bottleneck, quantified**: 88% of documented human OR sequences are orphan; only 4.5% have dose–response-confirmed agonists; "the complete combinatorial code is only known for a handful of odorants" — [Chemical Senses bjae037](https://academic.oup.com/chemse/article-abstract/doi/10.1093/chemse/bjae037/7821270)
- **Perceptual-label bottleneck**: the standard quality dataset (GS-LF) is 5,862 molecules × 152 descriptors of *expert-assigned semantic labels*, not psychophysical measurements — [bioRxiv 2024.09.16.613334](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.09.16.613334v1.full); the best modern intensity dataset is 62 molecules × ≥7 concentrations + 260 mixtures with 36 panelists — [PMC12363845](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12363845/). Three orders of magnitude separate the quality and intensity label sets.
- **Foundation-model competition is arriving**: "A General-Purpose Molecular Foundation Model Transfers Across Diverse Olfactory Tasks" — [arXiv 2608.25893](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.25893); "Heterogeneous Molecular Signatures of Human Odor Perception" — [arXiv 2604.09758](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.09758); a benchmark for LLM olfactory perception — [arXiv 2604.00002](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.00002). These are direct molecule→perception approaches and represent the baseline a chained pipeline must beat.
- The field is actively arguing about whether structure→odor prediction works at all: "Response to Doty 2025, predicting odor from structure is useful" — [Chemical Senses bjag003](https://academic.oup.com/chemse/article-abstract/doi/10.1093/chemse/bjag003/8489487)
- Review of the whole area: "Bridging odorants and olfactory perception through machine learning: A review" — [ScienceDirect S0924224424003765](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0924224424003765)

### Inferences
- **Nothing I found chains transport → receptor → perception end to end.** Mayhew stops at transport→odorous/odorless; Pellegrino stops at transport→intensity; MolOR and Ihara run receptor→percept with no transport stage. The user's proposed three-stage architecture is, as far as this research shows, unoccupied ground — which is an opportunity but also means there is no published evidence it beats a direct model.
- The pattern across both successful chained results is that the receptor stage was added as **auxiliary features alongside** the molecular representation, never as an information bottleneck. A strict bottleneck would throw away the molecular information that the direct models show is sufficient; the evidence favors a *skip-connected* or *multi-task* architecture over a literal serial pipeline.
- For the user's specific three-model design (quality model + potency model + combiner), the evidence maps cleanly: the **potency model has a strong, interpretable, physically-grounded 10-feature solution** (Pellegrino) that already matches DNNs, so a bio-inspired stage is clearly justified there. The **quality model's** bio-inspired stage is justified only weakly (+0.6% AUROC) and is bounded by the deorphanization ceiling. The asymmetry is worth building around rather than forcing symmetry.
- The four data bottlenecks, ranked by severity: (1) 88% orphan human ORs, with column-wise coverage far worse than row-wise; (2) EC50 assay reproducibility ~0.54 log (user's figure, consistent with M2OR), which caps any continuous receptor-activity regression; (3) intensity datasets two–three orders of magnitude smaller than quality datasets; (4) no dataset pairing measured human OR activations with measured human perceptual *intensity* at matched concentrations — the exact join a full pipeline needs.
- The 100 µM in-vitro EC50 vs ppb–ppt psychophysical threshold gap means stage 2 cannot be calibrated in absolute concentration units from M2OR. Any transport→receptor handoff has to pass a *relative* or *rank* quantity (which is what primacy coding conveniently consumes), not an absolute molar concentration.

### Gaps
- No head-to-head benchmark exists comparing a chained transport→receptor→perception model against POM/Open-POM or against the 2026 molecular foundation models on the same split. This is the single most important missing evidence for the user's question.
- I could not verify whether the Cell Systems published version reports additional or revised ablations beyond the preprint's +0.6% AUROC (403 on fetch).
- I found no reliable source quantifying how much of the MolOR gain survives on a *scaffold-split* or otherwise OOD test set, which is where a bio-inspired inductive bias should pay off most if it works at all.
