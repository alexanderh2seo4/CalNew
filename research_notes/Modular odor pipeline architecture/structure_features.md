## Q1. Abraham solvation (LSER) descriptors: the ODT / nasal-pungency equations, which terms dominate, and how to compute E, S, A, B, V, L for new molecules

### Takeaway
The Abraham LSER framework is the single best-documented *explicit* structure→potency model for odor: it decomposes odor detection threshold into dispersion (E), dipolarity/polarizability (S), H-bond acidity/donor (A), H-bond basicity/acceptor (B), and a volatility/size term (L or V), plus chemical-class indicator variables for the classes that are more potent than bulk physicochemistry predicts (mercaptans, aldehydes, acids, unsaturated esters). The published 353-compound model reaches R² = 0.759, SD = 0.819 log units — close to the ceiling set by inter-lab ODT scatter.

### Cited Findings
- The six Abraham solute descriptors are defined as: **E** = excess molar refractivity (London dispersion), **S** = dipole–dipole / dipole-induced-dipole (dipolarity/polarizability), **A** = H-bond **donor** (acidity), **B** = H-bond **acceptor** (basicity), **V** = McGowan molecular volume, **L** = log of the hexadecane/air partition ratio — [UFZ-LSER / Abraham descriptor overview, via search summary](https://pubs.rsc.org/ew/article/11/2/481/850181/A-machine-learning-framework-to-predict-PPCP)
- In the Abraham 2012 ODT paper the descriptors are stated precisely as: E = "solute excess molar refractivity in units of (dm³ mol⁻¹)/10"; S = "solute dipolarity/polarizability"; A = "overall or summation hydrogen bond acidity"; B = "overall or summation hydrogen bond basicity"; L = "logarithm of the gas to hexadecane partition coefficient at 25 °C" — [Abraham, Sánchez-Moreno, Cometto-Muñiz & Cain, *Chem Senses* 37:207 (2012), PMC3278675](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3278675)
- The main model (their eq. 6, in the form log(1/ODT) = c + eE + sS + aA + bB + lL + indicator terms) was fitted on **N = 353** compounds: **R² = 0.759, SD = 0.819 log units, predictive SD = 0.869 log units** — [Abraham et al. 2012, PMC3278675](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3278675)
- Four **indicator variables** carry the "extra potency" of specific classes: **M = 1 for mercaptans, AL = 1 for aldehydes, AC = 1 for carboxylic acids, UE = 1 for unsaturated esters**. i.e. bulk solvation descriptors alone systematically *under-predict* the potency of these four classes — [Abraham et al. 2012, PMC3278675](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3278675); consistent with the abstract summary "Aldehydes, acids, unsaturated esters, and mercaptans were included in the equation through indicator variables that took into account the higher potency of these compounds" — [Chem Senses abstract](https://academic.oup.com/chemse/article-abstract/37/3/207/325917)
- An **alternative equation (their eq. 8) substitutes McGowan volume V for L**, for use when gas–hexadecane partition data are unavailable: N = 353, **R² = 0.735, SD = 0.980** log units. This is the practically important variant, because V is computable from structure by simple atom/bond counting — [Abraham et al. 2012, PMC3278675](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3278675)
- The 353-compound dataset was assembled by putting ODTs from Cometto-Muñiz & Cain, Cometto-Muñiz & Abraham, and Hellman & Small onto the **same scale as the Nagata** values — i.e. cross-lab scale harmonisation was a prerequisite for fitting — [Chem Senses abstract](https://academic.oup.com/chemse/article-abstract/37/3/207/325917)
- A pyrfume-data issue tracks ingesting this exact dataset, so the 353 ODTs are plausibly available in machine-readable form — [pyrfume-data issue #37 "Abraham et al, 2012"](https://github.com/pyrfume/pyrfume-data/issues/37)
- The same solvation framework was applied to **nasal pungency** (trigeminal irritation) thresholds measured in **anosmics** — subjects with no olfaction, so the response is pure trigeminal. The descriptor set used there is written in the older notation: π₂ᴴ (dipolarity/polarizability), Σα₂ᴴ and Σβ₂ᴴ (overall H-bond acidity and basicity), and log L¹⁶ — [Abraham et al., nasal irritation solvation equation, *Exp Brain Res* / Toxicol In Vitro line of work](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0272059096900779)
- Abraham, Gola, Cometto-Muñiz & Cain published a combined treatment of **VOC thresholds for nasal pungency, eye irritation and odour** in one paper, i.e. the three chemosensory endpoints share a descriptor basis — [*Indoor and Built Environment* 10:252 (2001)](https://dx.doi.org/10.1177/1420326X0101000320)
- Abraham et al. also determined solvation descriptors **for terpenes specifically** and used them to predict nasal pungency thresholds — relevant because terpenes are a large fraction of any fragrance dataset and often lack experimental descriptors — [*J Chem Soc Perkin Trans 2* (1998)](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlelanding/1998/p2/a805665j)
- Reported goodness of the pungency prediction: "y = 1.013x − 0.025, r = 0.954" for the all-chemicals equation (predicted vs observed) — [search summary of the Abraham nasal-pungency QSAR line of work](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/238131916_The_determination_of_solvation_descriptors_for_terpenes_and_the_prediction_of_nasal_pungency_thresholds) *(flagged: taken from a search-engine summary, not a parsed primary text)*
- **Where to get descriptors for new molecules:** the **UFZ-LSER database** provides experimental Abraham descriptors for **>7000 small molecules**, queryable by name, CAS-RN or **SMILES**, and additionally returns *calculated* descriptors from SMILES alone when no experimental values exist — [UFZ-LSER database description](https://pubs.rsc.org/ew/article/11/2/481/850181/A-machine-learning-framework-to-predict-PPCP)
- **Group-contribution and ML estimation of Abraham descriptors from canonical SMILES** has been developed and benchmarked; the ML route gave better test-set prediction than group contribution, with MAE = 3.89 kJ/mol for ΔH_solv and 4.184 kJ/mol for ΔG_solv — [search summary of Abraham-descriptor ML estimation work](https://doi.org/10.3390/liquids2030007) *(flagged: the MAE numbers are for downstream solvation energies, not for the descriptors themselves)*
- L descriptors for long/branched alkanes have been back-computed from **GC retention data**, extending L coverage to C11–C42 methylated alkanes — a template for filling L for long-chain fragrance materials — [*Liquids* 2(3):7](https://doi.org/10.3390/liquids2030007)

### Inferences
- **Pipeline recommendation:** implement the LSER block as a 5–6-vector (E, S, A, B, V, and L when available) with a lookup-then-predict cascade: (1) UFZ-LSER experimental value by InChIKey/SMILES; (2) UFZ-LSER SMILES-calculated value; (3) an in-house ML/group-contribution predictor trained on the UFZ-LSER table. Store a `descriptor_source` flag per molecule so downstream models (and SHAP plots) can be audited for how much signal comes from predicted vs measured descriptors.
- The V-based equation (R² 0.735 vs 0.759) loses only ~0.02 R² relative to the L-based one. For a general pipeline over arbitrary SMILES, the **V form is the right default** — V is deterministic from structure, L is not. Keep L as an optional enrichment feature.
- The four indicator variables are exactly the "osmophore" idea in regression form: they are a statement that `A`/`B`/`S` do not capture what a thiol or an aldehyde does at the receptor. In an ML pipeline this argues for keeping **SMARTS-based functional-group flags alongside** continuous physicochemical descriptors rather than assuming a tree model will reconstruct them from bulk properties.
- The Abraham A and B descriptors are *continuous, thermodynamically calibrated* versions of RDKit's integer `NumHDonors`/`NumHAcceptors`. Including both is cheap and likely complementary: the counts encode topology/valence, A and B encode strength (a phenol OH and an alcohol OH are both `NumHDonors = 1` but differ strongly in A).

### Gaps
- **The exact numeric coefficients (c, e, s, a, b, l, and the M/AL/AC/UE offsets) of Abraham 2012 eq. 6 and eq. 8 could not be retrieved.** The PMC full text renders the equations as images; the eScholarship PDF and the ResearchGate copy would not parse (binary / HTTP 403). *Do not fabricate them.* To obtain: open [PMC3278675](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3278675) and read the equation figures directly, or the OUP version at [Chem Senses 37(3):207](https://academic.oup.com/chemse/article-abstract/37/3/207/325917). I cannot state which individual terms dominate numerically without them.
- Likewise the specific nasal-pungency equation coefficients (Abraham/Cometto-Muñiz) were not retrieved.
- No source found that benchmarks *predicted* Abraham descriptors (from SMILES) inside an ODT model — i.e. how much the R² 0.759 degrades when E/S/A/B/L are estimated rather than measured. This is a real risk for a pipeline over novel molecules and should be measured locally.

## Q2. Homologous series: odor threshold vs carbon chain length, the cut-off effect, and its mechanistic explanation

### Takeaway
Across every homologous series studied, sensory potency increases (threshold falls) roughly log-linearly with carbon number until it plateaus and then reverses or disappears entirely — the "cut-off". The plateau tends to arrive around C8 (octyl acetate is the canonical example). Roughly three-quarters of the chain-length effect is attributable to selective transport (volatility + partitioning), with a residual specific size effect and residual class effects for aldehydes and acids.

### Cited Findings
- **General law:** "All sensory thresholds decreased systematically with carbon chain length in homologous series of volatile organic compounds. Odor and pungency thresholds declined (i.e., sensory potency increased) with increasing carbon chain length" — [Cometto-Muñiz & Cain, *Indoor Air* 4:140 (1994)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1600-0668.1994.t01-1-00002.x)
- **Cut-off:** "chemesthetic detection thresholds decrease with increasing carbon chain length, but this trend reached a break point in each series where the homolog failed to produce chemesthesis, even at vapor saturation. All ensuing larger homologs failed too." — [Cometto-Muñiz et al., cut-off in ocular chemesthesis for alkylbenzenes and 2-ketones](https://www.osti.gov/etdeweb/biblio/21140906)
- **Where the plateau sits (acetates):** "Odor detection thresholds declined to a plateau for **octyl acetate** and beyond, whereas decreasing thresholds often end in a plateau or an increase at about the level of octyl acetate across acetates in other mammalian species, including non-human primates and rats" — [Cometto-Muñiz & Abraham, concentration-detection functions for homologous n-acetate esters, PMC2639399](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2639399/)
- **Mechanistic split:** "Selective transport accounts for some **77%** of the total effect, with the remainder due to a specific size effect that might involve **odour-binding proteins**, and a specific effect for **aldehydes and carboxylic acids**" — [Abraham et al., "A model for odour thresholds"](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/11520625_A_model_for_odour_thresholds) *(flagged: retrieved via search summary; the ResearchGate PDF returned HTTP 403. The 77% figure should be verified against the primary Chem Senses text before quoting in a report.)*
- **Cross-class quantitative model:** an analysis of **114 aliphatic compounds across nine functional-group classes** fitted a non-linear saturating function of molecular size with **R² = 0.90**: `pOL = [1 − exp(−k·n^1.4)] · pOL_max`, where **n = number of non-hydrogen atoms** and `pOL_max` is the asymptotic (best attainable) potency for that homologous series — [Zarzo, "Effect of functional group and carbon chain length on the odor detection threshold of aliphatic compounds", PMC3355402](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3355402/)
- That paper's class ordering of intrinsic potency (most→least potent, i.e. lowest→highest threshold): **thiols ≪ amines < aldehydes ≈ esters < alcohols ≈ ketones < hydrocarbons**; hydrocarbons are least potent "due to lower solubility and diffusivity in the mucus layer"; amine thresholds depend on **pKa** — [PMC3355402](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3355402/)
- Threshold values **asymptote** rather than continuing monotonically — "they increase initially then plateau rather than continuing indefinitely… maximum thresholds vary by functional group but stabilize for longer chains" — [PMC3355402](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3355402/)
- The authors explicitly decline a single mechanism, citing **mucus diffusion, odorant-binding proteins, enzymatic biotransformation, and the ~350 human olfactory receptors** as jointly responsible — [PMC3355402](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3355402/)
- **Thiols have their own chain-length optimum:** "a minimum [threshold] observed for thiols with **five to seven carbon atoms**, whereas increasing the chain length led to an exponential increase in odor threshold"; odor thresholds were "strongly influenced by steric effects" — [Structure–odor correlations in homologous series of alkanethiols, 3D-QSAR, *J Agric Food Chem* (2015)](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/jf506135c)
- Dedicated homologous-series ODT studies exist per class and are the right source for curve fitting: **n-alcohols** — [Cometto-Muñiz & Abraham, human olfactory detection of homologous n-alcohols](https://escholarship.org/content/qt8dj9f4b2/qt8dj9f4b2_noSplash_3539a1759a1358516f817fc5140a5d8a.pdf); **aldehydes and carboxylic acids** — [Cometto-Muñiz, Cain & Abraham, *Exp Brain Res* 118:180 (1998)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s002210050270); **n-acetate esters** — [PMC2639399](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2639399/); **alkylbenzenes and 2-ketones** — [ocular chemesthesis cut-off study](https://www.osti.gov/etdeweb/biblio/21140906)

### Inferences
- The Zarzo functional form is directly usable as an **explicit feature transform or as a prior**: compute `n` (heavy-atom count) and a one-hot class label, and either (a) feed `1 − exp(−k·n^1.4)` as an engineered feature, or (b) fit `pOL_max` per class as a learned per-class asymptote (a simple mixed-effects / per-class-intercept model). This is a natural fit for a **GAM/EBM** shape function on heavy-atom count, or for a **monotonic-then-saturating constraint**.
- **A monotonic constraint on chain length is wrong.** The relationship is non-monotonic in log-threshold terms (falling, then plateauing, then rising after cut-off). A monotone GBM constraint would be actively harmful past C8–C10. Prefer a *saturating* parameterisation (the exp form above), or a spline/EBM with enough flexibility to bend.
- The 77% "selective transport" share implies the Rodríguez-2011-style trio (vapor pressure, water solubility, logKow, r² ≈ 0.77 per the brief) and the Abraham L/V terms are largely *the same signal* as chain length. Expect heavy collinearity among `MolWt`, `heavy atom count`, `logP`, `L`, `V`, and `longest aliphatic chain`. Use correlated-feature-aware attribution (e.g. grouped SHAP, or TreeSHAP with a feature-clustering step) or the interpretation will be unstable.
- The residual ~23% — specific size effect and the aldehyde/acid class effect — is exactly the part that **generic fingerprints and physicochemical descriptors miss and explicit osmophore flags recover**. This is the strongest argument in the literature for the user's "model functional groups explicitly" instinct.
- Because the cut-off is series-specific, a `longest_aliphatic_chain × functional_class` **interaction** term is likely more informative than either alone. Tree models can find this; linear/LSER models cannot without explicit cross terms.

### Gaps
- Exact per-carbon ODT tables (log ppm by carbon number for each series) could not be extracted: the eScholarship PDFs for the alcohol and aldehyde/acid papers returned unparseable binary. Numbers are in those PDFs — retrieve them locally, or pull the corresponding Pyrfume datasets.
- I found no confirmation of a specific "minimum threshold at C8–C10 for aldehydes" claim; the confirmed plateau statement is for **acetates at octyl acetate**. Do not generalise the C8 figure to aldehydes without checking the 1998 *Exp Brain Res* paper.
- No source quantified how much of the cut-off is peripheral (vapor pressure ceiling — long homologs cannot reach threshold concentration in air even at saturation) vs receptor-level. The chemesthesis cut-off wording ("even at vapor saturation") strongly suggests a saturation-vapor-pressure limit, but this is an inference, not a cited attribution.

## Q3. Functional-group effects on potency (osmophores), branching, unsaturation, stereochemistry; quantitative SAR for ODT

### Takeaway
Functional group identity moves ODT by orders of magnitude at constant carbon skeleton — sulfur (thiols) and alkylpyrazines are the extreme low-threshold classes, and single substituent changes (methyl→ethyl on a pyrazine; SH→OH; SH→SMe) can shift thresholds by 3–4 log units. Stereochemistry matters at similar magnitude (up to ~800-fold between enantiomers), which means any potency model that ignores chirality has a hard error floor.

### Cited Findings
- **Sulfur:** thiols show "the lowest detection thresholds; compounds detected at remarkably lower concentrations than structurally similar alcohols" — [PMC3355402](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3355402/)
- **The SH group itself is the osmophore, not just the lipophilicity:** "Replacement of the SH group by an OH group as well as S-methylation of the thiols significantly increased the odor thresholds" — [*J Agric Food Chem* alkanethiol 3D-QSAR](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/jf506135c)
- **Branching matters, and in the non-obvious direction:** "tertiary alkanethiols revealed clearly lower odor thresholds than found for primary or secondary thiols" — [*J Agric Food Chem* alkanethiol 3D-QSAR](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/jf506135c)
- **Alkylpyrazines — huge substituent sensitivity:** "Trimethylpyrazine had the lowest threshold (50 ng/l air), and substitution of the methyl group in position 2 by an ethyl group yielded **2-ethyl-3,5-dimethylpyrazine** showing a **4500-fold lower** odour threshold" — [Structure–odour-activity relationships of alkylpyrazines, *Eur Food Res Technol*](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s002170050422) *(flagged: the wording in the retrieved summary is internally inconsistent — if trimethylpyrazine already "had the lowest threshold", the ethyl analogue cannot be 4500-fold lower. Verify the direction and the reference compound in the primary text before quoting. The qualitative point — that a single methyl→ethyl swap shifts the threshold by ~3.5 log units — is well established.)*
- **A dedicated pyrazine SOR study** using 3D-QSPR, HQSPR, Monte Carlo, docking, ADME-Tox and MD exists for pyrazines and derivatives — [ResearchGate record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/373535194_Structure-odor_relationship_in_pyrazines_and_derivatives_a_physicochemical_study_using_3D-QSPR_HQSPR_Monte_Carlo_Molecular_Docking_ADME-Tox_and_Molecular_Dynamics)
- **Amines:** threshold values are "influenced by the acid dissociation constant (pKa)" — i.e. a protonation-state feature, not just an H-bond count — [PMC3355402](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3355402/)
- **Enantiomers — carvone:** "(S)-carvone reminds one of caraway, whereas (R)-carvone has a minty odor"; thresholds differ too, with one enantiomer reported perceptible at **600 ppb** and the other at **43 ppb** (~14×) — [ScienceDirect carvone overview](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/agricultural-and-biological-sciences/carvone) *(flagged: the d-/l- ↔ R/S mapping in the retrieved summary was not stated unambiguously; check the enantiomer labels before use.)*
- **Enantiomers — nootkatone:** the two enantiomers differ by roughly **800-fold** in odor threshold, with reported values of **0.6–1.0 µg/L vs 400–800 µg/L in water**; one is grapefruit, the other woody/spicy — [Odor characteristics of novel non-canonical terpenes, PMC9230113](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9230113/) *(flagged: the retrieved summary assigned the high threshold to (+)-nootkatone, which conflicts with the well-known grapefruit character of (+)-nootkatone. Treat the 800-fold magnitude as reliable and the enantiomer assignment as unverified.)*
- **Rotundone:** aroma detection threshold in water of **8 ng/L**, "amongst the lowest for any natural product yet discovered" — [Bioscience, Biotechnology, and Biochemistry, rotundone synthesis paper](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/09168451.2019.1625264)
- **Enantiomeric odor *quality* differences correlate with molecular flexibility** — a structural, computable predictor of when chirality will matter — [Odour character differences for enantiomers correlate with molecular flexibility, PMC2610320](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2610320/)
- **Oak lactone:** odor detection thresholds have been determined for **all four stereoisomers** in both a white and a red wine — a clean four-point stereochemistry benchmark, and also a demonstration that ODT is **matrix-dependent** — [*Aust J Grape Wine Res*, oak lactone stereoisomer ODT](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/230144727_An_odour_detection_threshold_determination_of_all_four_possible_stereoisomers_of_oak_lactone_in_a_white_and_red_wine)
- The classic demonstration that enantiomers can smell different at all: [Friedman & Miller, "Odor Differences between Enantiomeric Isomers", *Science* 172:1044 (1971)](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.172.3987.1043); and linalool enantiomers differ in both perception and forehead surface-potential response — [*Chem Senses* 25:77 (2000)](https://academic.oup.com/chemse/article/25/1/77/345718)
- A recent review consolidates sulfur-compound SOR: "Decoding Sulfur-Containing Aroma Compounds in Foods: From Key Odorant Mapping to Structure–Odor Mechanisms and Flavor Design", noting Schieberle-school work "systematically revealing the precise influence of structural parameters on odor thresholds" — [PMC13564995](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13564995)
- There are QSAR models specifically for **odor thresholds of VOCs** as a class — [QSARs for Odor Thresholds of VOCs](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/229745845_Quantitative_Structure-Activity_Relationships_QSARs_for_Odor_Thresholds_of_Volatile_Organic_Compounds_VOCs) — and a QSPR study aimed explicitly at "the essential molecular functionalities of potent odorants" — [ResearchGate record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/259538217_A_quantitative_structure-property_relationship_approach_to_determine_the_essential_molecular_functionalities_of_potent_odorants)
- Predictive models of **odor contribution and thresholds** aimed at discovering novel crop aroma compounds — a recent applied ODT-prediction pipeline worth mining for its feature set — [PMC12654368](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12654368/)

### Inferences
- The tertiary-thiol result is the cleanest single argument for **explicit branching descriptors** (degree of the carbon bearing the osmophore, Kier–Hall kappa shape indices, number of quaternary/tertiary carbons) rather than relying on heavy-atom count. A SMARTS like `[CX4]([CX4])([CX4])([CX4])S` is a one-line feature that a Morgan fingerprint would only capture at radius ≥2 and in an entangled way.
- Because chirality shifts ODT by up to ~3 log units while leaving *all* 2D descriptors and standard Morgan fingerprints identical, a 2D-only potency model has an irreducible error contribution from every chiral pair in the training set. Practical options: (a) use **chiral-aware fingerprints** (RDKit `useChirality=True` in Morgan/atom-pair), (b) add a `has_unspecified_stereocenters` flag and either drop or down-weight those rows, (c) report a per-molecule "stereo-ambiguity" uncertainty. The molecular-flexibility correlation ([PMC2610320](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2610320/)) suggests `NumRotatableBonds` can act as a *gate* on how much chirality is expected to matter.
- The Abraham indicator variables (M/AL/AC/UE) and the Zarzo class ranking agree on the same short list of high-potency osmophores. A defensible minimal osmophore feature block: `thiol/SH`, `sulfide/disulfide`, `aldehyde`, `carboxylic acid`, `unsaturated ester`, `pyrazine`, `alkoxypyrazine`, `lactone`, `ketone`, `primary/secondary/tertiary alcohol`, `primary/secondary/tertiary amine + pKa proxy`, `thiazole/thiophene`, `terpene skeleton`.
- ODT is **matrix-dependent** (oak lactone in white vs red wine). Any training table pooling air-phase ODTs, water ODTs and wine ODTs needs a **matrix covariate**, or the model learns matrix noise. This is the same scale-harmonisation problem Abraham solved by putting four labs onto the Nagata scale.

### Gaps
- No single published **group-contribution table for ODT** (fragment → Δlog ODT) was located; the closest is the Zarzo per-class `pOL_max` asymptote and the Abraham indicator variables. If one is needed, it would have to be fitted locally.
- Could not retrieve quantitative ODTs for methoxypyrazines specifically (IBMP, IPMP), lactones as a class, or (E,Z)-2,6-nonadienal — the unsaturation example in the brief. The general principle that conjugation/unsaturation lowers thresholds in aldehydes is widely asserted but I did not find a citable quantitative source in this pass.
- No source found that isolates the marginal value of a chirality-aware representation for *potency* specifically (as opposed to quality/character).

## Q4. Osmophore / pharmacophore theories of odor quality (Beets, Rossiter, Kraft, Ohloff) and quality SOR for musks, ambergris, sandalwood

### Takeaway
The classical structure–odor-relationship literature is organised around "osmophores" — a polar anchoring group (usually C=O or OH, sometimes ether or a heteroatom analogue) held at a defined distance and orientation from a bulky lipophilic scaffold. The best-developed quantitative cases are sandalwood, ambergris and musk; the field's standard review is Rossiter 1996.

### Cited Findings
- **Osmophore definition, classical:** "The osmophore is associated with a functional group such as **carbonyl, hydroxyl, occasionally an ether or a limited variety of heteroatomic homologues**" — [Chemical Classification and Structure–Odour Relationships, Springer chapter](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/978-94-011-3826-0_9.pdf)
- **The canonical SOR-rich odor classes:** "the most investigated classes [are] **fatty aldehydes, degraded carotenoids, sandalwood odorants, ambergris and musk compounds**" — [Chemical Classification and Structure–Odour Relationships](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-94-011-3826-0_9)
- **Where QSAR worked best — rigid scaffolds:** "QSAR equations and molecular descriptors have been used to derive structure-odor relationships for the most rigid molecules such as **α-campholenic derivatives (sandalwood odor), trans-decalin (ambergris odor), tetralin, and indans (musk odor)**" — [Chemical Classification and Structure–Odour Relationships](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-94-011-3826-0_9)
- **Sandalwood osmophore is two-part and both parts are required:** structural modification of β-santalol "affected the osmophoric alcohol group or its transformation into an ester or aldehyde, with substitution of the **exocyclic double bond — which serves as another important osmophoric group** — by a keto- or methoxy-group leading to a **complete loss** of the typical sandalwood odor" — [SAR of sandalwood odorants: cyclopropano-β-santalol](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0223523407003856)
- Further sandalwood SOR series: [tricyclo β-santalol synthesis and odor](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0223523404001977); [Structure–odor relationships of α-santalol derivatives with modified side chains, PMC6268821](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6268821/)
- **Rossiter 1996 is the standard review:** "Structure-odor relationships have been extensively reviewed, most recently by **Rossiter (1996)**" — [Chemical Classification and Structure–Odour Relationships](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-94-011-3826-0_9). (Rossiter, K.J., *Chem. Rev.* 96:3201–3240, 1996, "Structure–Odor Relationships".)
- Other review entry points: [Weyerstahl, "Odor and Structure", *J. Prakt. Chem.* 336:95 (1994)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/prac.19943360202); ["Structure-odor relations: A modern perspective"](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/241197841_Structure-odor_relations_A_modern_perspective); [German-language "Beziehungen zwischen Struktur und Geruch"](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-662-09768-7_2)

### Inferences
- The osmophore concept maps almost exactly onto a **3D pharmacophore fingerprint**: one H-bond acceptor (or donor) feature, a hydrophobic/aromatic feature, and a distance bin between them. That is precisely what Gobbi 2D pharmacophore fingerprints and RDKit's `Gobbi_Pharm2D` / `SigFactory` produce. For the *quality* head of a modular pipeline (musk / sandalwood / ambergris / fruity), a pharmacophore-pair fingerprint is the theory-matched featurization, more so than Morgan.
- Sandalwood's "two osmophores, both required" result is a **conjunction**, which linear models and count-based fingerprints handle badly but which a tree ensemble, a pharmacophore-*triplet* fingerprint, or a motif-level GNN can represent natively.
- The classical literature's emphasis on **rigid** scaffolds is a warning: 3D pharmacophore features are only well-defined when a single low-energy conformer dominates. For flexible acyclic odorants (most food volatiles), either use 2D pharmacophore fingerprints (conformer-free) or a multi-conformer ensemble. The flexibility/enantiomer-discrimination result in Q3 supports gating 3D features on `NumRotatableBonds`.

- I did not retrieve Kraft, Bajgrowicz, Denis & Fráter, "Odds and Trends: Recent Developments in the Chemistry of Odorants", *Angew. Chem. Int. Ed.* 39:2980 (2000), nor any of Ohloff's or Beets' primary formulations. These are the canonical statements of the "odor rules" and should be consulted directly for the report; I have no citable quotes from them.
- No quantitative (numeric) pharmacophore geometry — e.g. the specific donor–hydrophobe distance range claimed for the sandalwood or ambergris osmophore (the "triaxial rule" for ambergris is the relevant concept) — was retrieved in this pass.

## Q5. Featurization approaches that encode these features explicitly (RDKit, SMARTS, pharmacophore FPs, GNN variants, interpretable models)

### Takeaway
There is a clean ladder from cheap explicit counts (RDKit Lipinski/fragment descriptors) through SMARTS functional-group vectors and pharmacophore fingerprints to motif/fragment-aware GNNs. Recent olfaction-specific work shows the explicit-chemistry blocks are **complementary** to learned representations rather than obsolete, and the learned representations do not reliably beat them.

### Cited Findings
- **RDKit provides the explicit counts directly.** `Lipinski.NumHDonors` / `NumHAcceptors` (and the `rdMolDescriptors.CalcNumHBD/CalcNumHBA` and `NumHDonors`/`NHOHCount`/`NOCount` variants), plus ~85 `Descriptors.fr_*` fragment counters (e.g. `fr_aldehyde`, `fr_ketone`, `fr_ester`, `fr_COO`, `fr_SH`, `fr_sulfide`, `fr_ether`, `fr_lactone`, `fr_Ar_N`, `fr_NH2`), and shape/branching indices (`Kappa1-3`, `Chi*`, `NumRotatableBonds`, `TPSA`, `MolLogP`, `LabuteASA`) — [RDKit descriptor documentation](https://www.rdkit.org/docs/GettingStartedInPython.html#list-of-available-descriptors)
- RDKit ships **2D pharmacophore fingerprints** including the **Gobbi** feature definitions (`rdkit.Chem.Pharm2D.Gobbi_Pharm2D`, used with `Generate.Gen2DFingerprint` and a `SigFactory`), plus a `ChemicalFeatures` factory (`BaseFeatures.fdef`) exposing Donor / Acceptor / Hydrophobe / Aromatic / PosIonizable / NegIonizable features — [RDKit 2D pharmacophore fingerprint docs](https://www.rdkit.org/docs/GettingStartedInPython.html#d-pharmacophore-fingerprints)
- **PharmaGNN (2025/26, olfaction-specific) — the key ablation.** Pharmacophore regions extracted from **3D** structures via RDKit's feature factory; each region encoded as a **10-dimensional vector**: averaged Gasteiger charge (electrostatics), total molar refractivity + averaged logP (hydrophobicity), **local topological polar surface area (hydrogen bonding)**, plus centre coordinates and three surface distances (shape). Multichannel pooling (max/mean/sum) → 2-layer MLP → 128-d pharmacophore embedding, concatenated with a 128-d GAT graph embedding → 256-d → 6-layer MLP head — [PharmaGNN, PMC13543736](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13543736/)
- **PharmaGNN ablation numbers:** graph-only AUROC **0.8695** (Dataset 1: 4,983 molecules / 138 odor labels) and **0.9660** (Dataset 2: 13,088 molecules / 85 labels); pharmacophore-only **0.8420** and **0.9544**; **combined 0.8811 and 0.9702** — i.e. explicit pharmacophore features add **+1.2 AUROC points** over the graph alone on the small dataset and +0.4 on the large one, and pharmacophore-only is nearly competitive with graph-only. Generalised to a third dataset of 13,203 molecules / 232 labels "with minimal degradation" — [PharmaGNN, PMC13543736](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13543736/)
- **Explicit descriptors vs fingerprints, head-to-head on 8,681 odorants** (curated from ten expert sources incl. Arctander, FlavorDB, IFRA glossary), three tree models (RF, XGBoost, LightGBM), three feature blocks:
  - **ST** (Morgan fingerprints from 3D-optimised MolBlock): AUROC **0.828**, AUPRC 0.237, accuracy 97.8%, specificity 99.5% — best overall
  - **MD** (molecular descriptors: MolWt, **H-donors/acceptors**, TPSA, LogP, rotatable bonds, heavy-atom count, ring count): AUROC **0.802**, AUPRC 0.200
  - **FG** (SMARTS functional-group fingerprint): AUROC **0.753**
  Conclusion stated: "Descriptors complemented rather than replaced fingerprints" — [Comparative study of ML models on molecular fingerprints for odor decoding, *Communications Chemistry* (2025), PMC12462479](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12462479/)
- **Which descriptors carried the signal in that study:** `MolLogP` (importance ≈ **0.206**), `MolWt` (≈ **0.172**), `TPSA` (≈ **0.170**) were the principal determinants — interpreted as lipophilicity→receptor binding, molecular weight→volatility and size, TPSA→"balances mucosal diffusion and polarity" — [PMC12462479](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12462479/)
- **Per-functional-group interpretability from the FG block:** acetate patterns dominated *fruity* (importance 0.26); aldehydes peaked at 0.21 for *citrus*; sulfide motifs 0.31 for *meaty* — a demonstration that SMARTS features buy interpretability that fingerprints do not — [PMC12462479](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12462479/)
- **Learned embeddings do not beat explicit chemistry in olfaction.** A reliability audit compared **MoLFormer and ChemBERTa against RDKit descriptors and Morgan fingerprints** on the Keller–Vosshall and Bierling single-molecule rating datasets plus the Ma binary-mixture dataset: "Learned embeddings do not consistently outperform conventional representations in global alignment, and **MoLFormer provides no clear incremental predictive value beyond a combined RDKit–Morgan baseline** in either single-molecule dataset." Human rating geometry was reproducible across participant splits (median RSA 0.743 and 0.855) while model–human alignment was RSA 0.019–0.158 — [Huang & Sun, "Beyond Predictive Accuracy: A Reliability-Aware Audit of Molecular Representations for Human Olfaction", arXiv:2607.24848](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24848)
- The combined **RDKit-descriptors + Morgan** block is explicitly described as "the primary stringent baseline for incremental prediction" in current olfaction ML — [search summary of the same audit / GraphNOSE line of work](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.05694)
- **Functional-group-aware GNNs exist and are benchmarked:** a **Dual-Branch GNN for predicting molecular odors and discovering the relationship between functional groups and odors** — [PubMed 40008798](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40008798/); **ScentGraphX** on 4,967 molecules for multi-label odor descriptors, with ablations "confirming the indispensability of each module"; **multi-feature graph attention networks** for odor — [Molecular Odor Prediction Based on Multi-Feature Graph Attention Networks, arXiv:2502.01430](https://pith.science/paper/2502.01430)
- **GraphNOSE** (graph transformer for olfaction) outperformed "linear models, molecular language model embeddings, molecular fingerprints, and baseline GNNs by an average AUROC margin of **4.52%**" using **6× fewer parameters** than a standard GNN baseline — [GraphNOSE, arXiv:2609.05694](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.05694)
- **Motif/fragment hierarchy outside olfaction (transferable machinery):** **FG-BERT**, a self-supervised functional-group-based molecular representation framework — [*Briefings in Bioinformatics* 24(6):bbad398](https://academic.oup.com/bib/article/24/6/bbad398/7337693); **HiMol / HimGNN**, hierarchical molecular graph self-supervised learning capturing motif structures and multi-level representations; **FARM**, functional-group-aware representations for small molecules — [ResearchGate record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/384631066_FARM_Functional_Group-Aware_Representations_for_Small_Molecules); **BRICS-fragment-enhanced GNNs** with a two-stage self-supervised pretraining scheme — [ACM AICI 2025](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3730436.3730461); **fingerprint-enhanced hierarchical molecular GNNs** — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2095177925000590); **Add-GNN** dual-representation fusion with additive attention — [*Symmetry* 17(6):873](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-8994/17/6/873); **MulAFNet**, integrating multiple molecular representations — [*ACS Omega* 10(12):12043](https://pubs.acs.org/acsodf/article/10/12/12043/3653053/MulAFNet-Integrating-Multiple-Molecular)
- **FGBench** provides a dataset/benchmark for molecular property reasoning **at functional-group level** — useful as an external sanity check that a pipeline's FG block is behaving — [arXiv:2508.01055](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.01055)
- **Mol-PECO** is a deep model predicting human olfactory perception from molecular structure, using Coulomb-matrix/electrostatic-style input rather than graphs — the closest published "electrostatic feature" precedent in olfaction — [arXiv:2305.12424](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.12424)
- Interpretable multitask deep models for odor perception based on molecular structure — [PubMed 41140575](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41140575/)
- One ablation caveat worth recording: "Feature-ablation results suggest fingerprint features may **trade AUROC for F1 in imbalanced settings**" — [search summary of the olfaction representation-comparison literature](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12462479/)

### Inferences
- **Concrete recommended feature blocks for the modular pipeline**, ordered by evidence strength:
  1. **Physicochemical/transport block** (strongest, ~77% of the chain-length effect): `MolLogP`, `MolWt`, heavy-atom count, `TPSA`, `NumRotatableBonds`, ring count, `LabuteASA`, plus estimated vapor pressure / water solubility / logKow if available. This reproduces both the Rodríguez r²≈0.77 result and the top-3 importances in [PMC12462479](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12462479/).
  2. **H-bonding block:** RDKit `NumHDonors`, `NumHAcceptors`, `NHOHCount`, `NOCount` (integer/topological) **plus** Abraham **A** and **B** (continuous/energetic) **plus** the `PEOE_VSA*` family (charge-weighted surface area — note `PEOE_VSA1` was already a top SHAP feature in the Hou 2026 work cited in the brief). These three encodings of "H-bonding" are not redundant: count, strength, and charge-weighted surface.
  3. **Osmophore block:** a fixed, hand-curated SMARTS vector (thiol, sulfide, disulfide, thioester, aldehyde, ketone, carboxylic acid, ester, lactone, α,β-unsaturated carbonyl, pyrazine, alkoxypyrazine, furan, thiazole, pyrrole, phenol, primary/secondary/tertiary alcohol, amine by degree) — this directly instantiates the Abraham M/AL/AC/UE indicators and the Zarzo class effects. RDKit `fr_*` covers much of this for free; add the odor-specific missing ones (pyrazine, methoxypyrazine, lactone subtypes) by hand.
  4. **Chain/shape block:** longest aliphatic carbon chain (computable in RDKit by taking the largest matched path over `[CX4,CX3;!R]` atoms, or via `GetSubstructMatches` on increasing-length chain SMARTS / `rdmolops.GetDistanceMatrix` restricted to acyclic carbons — RDKit has no single built-in "longest chain" descriptor), count of tertiary/quaternary carbons, Kier `Kappa1-3` shape indices, `Chi` connectivity indices, `FractionCSP3`, number of branch points. Mordred adds further chain/path descriptors.
  5. **Pharmacophore block:** Gobbi 2D pharmacophore fingerprint (conformer-free, safe for flexible molecules) and/or ErG; optionally the PharmaGNN-style 10-d 3D pharmacophore vectors for rigid molecules only.
  6. **Generic fingerprint block:** Morgan/ECFP (with `useChirality=True`) and MACCS as the "catch-all" that the literature says still outperforms MD alone.
- **The evidence says: combine, don't choose.** Two independent recent olfaction results point the same way — PharmaGNN's combined block beats either branch alone, and the reliability audit finds learned encoders add nothing beyond RDKit+Morgan. For a small-data potency task this is a strong prior in favour of descriptor+tree with explicit osmophore features, matching the Fichtelmann & Westermayr and Hou findings already in hand.
- **Interpretable-model fit:** an **EBM/GAM** is well matched to this problem because the known structure is additive-with-a-few-interactions: a saturating shape function on size, per-class offsets (the Abraham indicators are literally additive offsets), and a small number of genuine interactions (`chain length × class`, `flexibility × chirality`). Symbolic regression is worth trying against the Zarzo form as a sanity target, since a known closed form (`1 − exp(−k·n^1.4)`) already fits at R² = 0.90 — if symbolic regression cannot rediscover something close, the search is misconfigured.
- **Do not impose a monotonic constraint on chain length** (see Q2 inference). If monotonic constraints are wanted, apply them to `MolLogP` or vapor pressure within a fixed class, not to carbon count globally.
- **Attribution caution for the report:** the existing SHAP results (size/shape/polarity dominating; `PEOE_VSA1`, `NHOHCount`, BCUT) are consistent with the Abraham decomposition, but SHAP over the heavily collinear transport block will arbitrarily split credit among `MolWt`/`logP`/`L`/`V`/chain length. Any claim of the form "size matters more than H-bonding" needs grouped attribution or a leave-one-block-out ablation to be defensible.
