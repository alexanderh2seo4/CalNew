# Fragrance & flavor industry: can outside researchers get odor potency data? (company by company)

Scope note: this is web research current to late September 2026. Every claim marked with a source comes from that source. Items marked **[unverified]** or listed under Gaps come from background knowledge or weak sources and need checking before anyone relies on them. Search coverage was good for Givaudan, dsm-firmenich, Symrise, Kao, Osmo and Monell. It was thin for IFF, Takasago, Mane and Robertet, and almost empty for Huabao, T. Hasegawa, Ogawa, BASF Aroma, Privi, Vigon and Treatt.

## Q0. Company by company: what data each is known or likely to hold, AI initiatives, partners and contact points

### Takeaway
Each major house says in public that it holds large proprietary datasets: formula libraries, "odour value" maps of its ingredients, receptor-assay data, and consumer or sensory panels. Each also describes this data as a competitive asset. None of them runs a public program that gives outsiders access to raw potency or threshold data. What exists is bilateral collaboration (academia, Monell, startups) under contract and in-house AI tools.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- Each house holds at least three internal data types relevant to potency: (a) per-ingredient odour-value or threshold maps (explicit for Givaudan's Odour Value Map; Kao's 314-PRM dose–response DB), (b) internal performance or substantivity testing, and (c) formula libraries of millions of formulas. The data exists. Symrise's encryption stance shows the barrier is policy, not technology.
- The most realistic routes for an LMU researcher are:
  1. Collaboration through **Leibniz-LSB@TUM** (Freising). It is geographically close, has a public odorant database with thresholds, and is Symrise's top academic partner.
  2. A sponsored project or industrial PhD with Symrise, Givaudan or dsm-firmenich under NDA. Any data would likely come with publication restrictions, anonymised identifiers (as with IBM) or model-only outputs.
  3. Working through Monell, whose industry members include dsm-firmenich, IFF, Symrise and Takasago.
- Japanese houses (Kao, and possibly T. Hasegawa and Takasago) seem more willing to publish dose–response or intensity methodology papers with in-house panel data than the Swiss houses, which mainly publish thresholds for individual new molecules. Treat this as a hypothesis.

### Gaps
- No public, current (2024–2026) olfaction- or AI-specific call for proposals, grant or doctoral fellowship was found for Givaudan, dsm-firmenich, IFF or Symrise. Firm contact points for scientific collaboration (as opposed to general press/contact forms) were not found except the Monell and Symrise generic contacts.
- IFF: no documented academic data-sharing, grant program or AI-research partner was found in this pass. IFF's older collaborations (e.g., with the Vosshall/Keller lab at Rockefeller) are **[unverified]**.
- Huabao, T. Hasegawa, Ogawa, Privi, Vigon, Treatt, Mane: essentially no findings. A targeted search on J-STAGE/Japanese-language sources for T. Hasegawa and Ogawa threshold papers is recommended.
- Could not verify whether Givaudan has a formal university chair, PhD fellowship or Zurich-area (ETH/UZH) olfaction program. Search found nothing.
- The user's premise mentions "Givaudan open innovation". No dedicated Givaudan open-innovation submission portal was found for fragrance. Givaudan has in the past run open-innovation portals for flavour and taste, but this is **[unverified]** for 2024–2026.

## Q1. Which companies have ever released proprietary sensory or potency data to the public or to academics?

### Takeaway
Only **Kao** has released a large in-house potency (dose–response intensity) dataset: 314 PRMs, 18 expert raters, published in IECR 2019. It came out as a normal peer-reviewed paper, not as a data-sharing program. The other large public olfaction datasets (Keller 2016, Lee/POM 2023, DREAM) were built by academic labs, Google or Osmo using academic panels and public catalogs, with no fragrance-house data.

### Cited Findings
- Kao: 314-PRM dose–response database published by Wakayama et al. (IECR 2019). The press release describes internal use only and names no external collaborators. — [Kao release](https://www.kao.com/global/en/newsroom/news/release/2020/20200108-001/); [IECR](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acs.iecr.9b01225)
- POM (Lee et al. 2023): training labels came from GoodScents and Leffingwell (public or commercial catalog descriptors), and the panel ran at Monell. No fragrance-house data or authors. — [PMC11898014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11898014/)
- DREAM Olfactory Mixtures Prediction Challenge (2024/2025, Sage Bionetworks/Synapse): used publicly available data from 3 studies (>700 mixtures, ~600 discriminability measurements) plus 46 unpublished test mixture pairs. The sources found name no industry sponsor or industry data. 26 teams took part. — [Synapse 2025 challenge](https://www.synapse.org/DREAM_Olfactory_Mixtures_Prediction_Challenge2025); [Sage GitHub](https://github.com/Sage-Bionetworks-Challenges/olfactory-mixtures-prediction); [bioRxiv results preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2025.12.13.694160.full.pdf)
- Mayhew et al. 2022 (PNAS), odorous vs odorless: >1,900 molecules from literature and web scraping (1,796) plus human discrimination. Dataset openly available ("no restrictions"). Not industry-sourced. — [PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2116576119); [GitHub joelmainland/odor-prediction](https://github.com/joelmainland/odor-prediction)
- Pyrfume aggregates >40 olfactory datasets covering >20,000 odorants and is the main public hub. Industry-origin data in it is limited to what was published. — [Pyrfume, Sci Data 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-024-04051-z)
- Fichtelmann & Westermayr (arXiv 2512.08683) built an ordinal odor-strength dataset (odorless/low/medium/high) of >2,000 molecules by combining two public sources. This fits the "1–2k noisy molecules" ceiling the user describes. — [arXiv 2512.08683](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08683)

### Inferences
- The industry-to-academia data flow happens through publications (single-molecule thresholds, method papers, Kao's DB) and personnel moves, not through dataset releases. No evidence was found of any major house contributing data to a public challenge.
- Kao is the most promising company to approach for a potency dataset. It has already published one, it is a consumer-goods company rather than a pure fragrance house (so fragrance IP is less central to its revenue), and its lab has an ML-oriented intensity-prediction agenda.

### Gaps
- Not verified whether Wakayama 2019's Supporting Information includes the raw dose–response parameters for all 314 PRMs, or whether Kao published follow-up papers (e.g., mixtures, or a larger n).
- Not verified whether any Givaudan or Firmenich scientist co-authored Keller/Vosshall or Monell psychophysics datasets.

## Q2. Which companies publish papers with in-house threshold data?

### Takeaway
Givaudan (the Kraft group) is the most prolific. It regularly reports GC-olfactometry odor thresholds in ng/L air for new and known odorants in Chemistry & Biodiversity and Helvetica Chimica Acta. Firmenich publishes olfactometry methodology. Kao published dose–response intensity data. Leibniz-LSB@TUM (Symrise's main academic partner) maintains a public odorant database with thresholds.

### Cited Findings
- Givaudan: Kraft et al., Chem. Biodivers. 2014 ("From Cassyrane to Cashmeran – The Molecular Parameters of Odorants"), with threshold 0.036 ng/L air for the most potent ionone-family compound. Kraft 2008 ("Woody Pretzels"), threshold 0.067 ng/L. — [2014](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/cbdv.201400071); [2008](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/cbdv.200890108)
- Givaudan patents (e.g., cyclopropanated sandalwood compounds) are another source of in-house potency comparisons. — [USPTO 12552732](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12552732)
- Firmenich: Chaintreau (REACH-driven analysis and physicochemistry, FFJ 2010). Cicchetti et al. 2018 compared olfactometric methods for impact odorants. — [FFJ 2010](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ffj.1963); [FFJ 2018](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/ffj.3426)
- Kao: IECR 2019 dose–response DB (see Q1). — [IECR](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acs.iecr.9b01225)
- Leibniz-LSB@TUM Odorant Database (Freising): identifies food odorants and gives odor threshold values in different matrices, plus retention indices and odor qualities, with references per substance. There is an open Pyrfume issue to ingest it. — [LSB Odorant DB](https://www.leibniz-lsb.de/en/databases/leibniz-lsbtum-odorant-database/start/); [Preface](https://www.leibniz-lsb.de/en/databases/leibniz-lsbtum-odorant-database/preface); [Pyrfume issue #50](https://github.com/pyrfume/pyrfume-data/issues/50)
- Food-odorant threshold compilations from the Grosch/Schieberle tradition (e.g., "Re-investigation on odour thresholds of key food aroma compounds") come from academic groups, not fragrance houses. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/225447154_Re-investigation_on_odour_thresholds_of_key_food_aroma_compounds_and_development_of_an_aroma_language_based_on_odour_qualities_of_defined_aqueous_odorant_solutions)

### Inferences
- A systematic text-mining pass over Givaudan (Kraft), Firmenich and Symrise papers in Chem. Biodivers., Helv. Chim. Acta, Eur. J. Org. Chem. and FFJ, plus patents, could plausibly yield several hundred industry-measured thresholds that share a house protocol (GC-O, ng/L air). This is feasible without anyone's permission.
- The LSB database uses the Grosch/Schieberle protocols and so offers internally consistent thresholds. It is geographically close to LMU and an obvious first partner.

### Gaps
- Exact count of Kraft/Givaudan threshold values in the literature is unknown.
- Did not verify whether the LSB database allows bulk download or reuse for ML. Licensing is unknown.

## Q3. Monell Chemical Senses Center industry sponsors: who, and does it give data access?

### Takeaway

Members get early access to science, symposia, visiting-scientist training, collaboration opportunities and "special access to intellectual property". This is the flow of Monell's data **to** industry. Nothing indicates it opens industry data to academics.

### Cited Findings

## Q5. What companies say publicly about data as a competitive asset

### Takeaway
The houses openly describe their formula and sensory data as core capital. Symrise is the most explicit ("our capital"). Givaudan lists proprietary data among its unique strengths, and dsm-firmenich touts its "data-science engine". Osmo's pitch rests on owning "the world's largest interconnected scent dataset". This strongly suggests raw potency data will not be released freely.

### Cited Findings
- Symrise: "The mixtures are our capital and we don't want to just give it away". Data was sent to IBM encrypted and anonymised. — [Symrise](https://www.symrise.com/our-stories/fragrances-from-big-data/)
- Givaudan: proprietary data among its unique strengths, and data and AI "across our value chain". — [Givaudan 2030 strategy](https://integratedreport.givaudan.com/2025/our-strategy/our-2030-strategy)
- dsm-firmenich: "our state-of-the-art data-science engine … empowers the development of unique, precision solutions". — [IAR 2025](https://annualreport.dsm-firmenich.com/2025/our-company/science-research/our-approach.html)
- Kao: DB presented as internal technology for intensity control and sustainability. — [Kao](https://www.kao.com/global/en/newsroom/news/release/2020/20200108-001/)
- Osmo: "world's largest interconnected scent dataset", and Google transferred all IP, including data, to Osmo. Part of the validation structures is withheld. — [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260204293785/en/Osmo-Digital-Scent-Design-Company-Announces-$70M-in-Series-B-Funding-and-Executive-Leadership-Team-to-Scale-AI-Powered-Olfaction-Technology); [PMC11898014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11898014/)
- Context: Firmenich, Givaudan, IFF and Symrise were under antitrust investigation (2023) in several jurisdictions. This may make them more cautious about any multi-house data pooling. — [Cosmetics Business](https://cosmeticsbusiness.com/news/article_page/Firmenich_Givaudan_IFF_and_Symrise_under_investigation_by_several_antitrust_agencies/207372)

### Inferences
- Any access will probably be limited to (a) anonymised structures or descriptors, (b) model-to-data setups (the company runs the model and returns metrics), or (c) publication of aggregate results. The antitrust context argues against a pre-competitive multi-house data consortium unless it runs through a neutral body (e.g., RIFM or IFRA), and this is speculative.

### Gaps
- Did not read full 2025 annual reports of IFF, Symrise or Takasago for explicit data-policy text.
- No company was found with a stated policy that permits sharing sensory data with academics.
