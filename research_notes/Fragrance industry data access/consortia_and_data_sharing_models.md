# Consortia, industry bodies and privacy-preserving data-sharing routes to fragrance-industry odor potency data

Scope: collective/institutional routes (not bilateral company deals) through which an outside academic (LMU Munich) could access or train on proprietary odor threshold/intensity/performance data. Research date: 26 Sep 2026. Items tagged **[unverified]** come from background knowledge and could not be confirmed with a live source in this session.

## 1. Industry bodies (RIFM, IFRA, FEMA, EFFA/IOFI, BACIS/Leffingwell, DVRH): what they hold and whether outsiders can get in

### Takeaway
None of the fragrance/flavor trade bodies runs a known odor-potency database that academics can access. RIFM's database is a toxicology/safety resource behind a membership or subscription wall. RIFM is running new human odor-threshold studies, but it has not said whether or when it will publish them. IFRA and FEMA hold usage and safety data, not potency data. The best realistic "institutional" asset is RIFM's forthcoming threshold work plus its physicochemical data (e.g., vapor pressure), both obtained through a negotiated research collaboration, not a standard access program.

### Cited Findings
**RIFM (Research Institute for Fragrance Materials)**
- The RIFM Database is described as the most comprehensive worldwide source of toxicology data, literature and general information on fragrance and flavor raw materials: >7,000 materials, >80,000 references and nearly 200,000 human health and environmental studies. It covers toxicology, clinical, regulatory and environmental data — [RIFM Database page](https://rifm.org/rifm-database/); summarized via [search result](https://rifm.org/rifm-database/)
- Non-logged-in users see "Access denied. You are not a member". Certain RIFM assessment tools are "available exclusively to RIFM members and database subscribers", which means a non-member subscription tier exists. The page gives no pricing and no academic tier — [RIFM Database](https://rifm.org/rifm-database/)
- A FEMA-hosted description says the database is supported by RIFM and IOFI and includes "basic material information such as CAS numbers, synonyms, structures, molecular formulas and weights, and physical properties" (older figures: >6,000 materials, >135,000 studies) — [FEMA/IOFI page](https://www.femaflavor.org/topic/iofi). Because physical properties are included, vapor pressure data are likely present, but I could not confirm this directly.
- A Perfumer & Flavorist news item titled "RIFM Database Open to F&F Industry" shows the database was opened beyond members to the flavor and fragrance industry, but the page returned 403, so the terms and date were not verified — [Perfumer & Flavorist](https://www.perfumerflavorist.com/fragrance/regulatory-research/news/21870984/rifm-database-open-to-ff-industry)
- **Odor thresholds at RIFM:** "RIFM is now researching odor thresholds, updating earlier studies that looked at the mechanism in humans… Current science has replaced the techniques and study designs used back then with more sophisticated, refined versions." RIFM gives no publication timeline and no list of materials — [RIFM "If I can smell it, is it safe?"](https://rifm.org/question/if-i-can-smell-it-is-it-safe/)
- At RIFM INFOX 2025 (Paris, Oct 2025), RIFM Principal Scientist Nikaeta Sadekar presented "respiratory safety and odor threshold research". The same event covered NAMs and chemical-clustering strategies (Holger Moustakas), which shows in-house computational capacity — [RIFM INFOX 2025](https://rifm.org/advancing-fragrance-safety-through-innovation-science-and-collaboration-rifm-infox-2025-in-paris/)
- RIFM publishes its safety assessments openly via the Elsevier-hosted Fragrance Material Safety Resource — [Fragrance Material Safety Resource](https://fragrancematerialsafetyresource.elsevier.com/content/rifm-home). **[unverified]** From background knowledge, the per-material safety assessments published in *Food and Chemical Toxicology* list physicochemical properties (vapor pressure, log Kow, water solubility, MW) but not odor thresholds. That makes them a free, scrapeable source of volatility descriptors for ~1,000+ fragrance materials, but not a source of potency labels.
- RIFM posts public calls for industry data, e.g. "Request for Concentration Data on 110 Fragrance Ingredients", which shows it can collect data from member companies — [RIFM](https://rifm.org/request-for-concentration-data-on-110-fragrance-ingredients/)

**IFRA (International Fragrance Association)**
- The IFRA Transparency List is built from IFRA members' anonymous, confidential reports in the Volume of Use (VoU) survey, which runs about every 4–5 years. Results are "aggregated anonymously and treated under full confidentiality in line with anti-trust obligations", and they feed RIFM's Safety Assessment Program — [IFRA About the Transparency List](https://ifrafragrance.org/transparency-list/about-the-ifra-transparency-list); [IFRA 2024 VoU survey launch](https://ifrafragrance.org/latest-updates/press-releases/ifra-announces-launch-of-the-2024-volume-of-use-survey)
- The 2025 Transparency List has 3,691 ingredients: 3,312 fragrance ingredients used for odor or malodor cover, 379 functional ingredients, and 1,021 natural complex substances — [IFRA press release](https://ifrafragrance.org/latest-updates/press-releases/ifra-launches-updated-transparency-list-a-comprehensive-overview-of-ingredients-used-in-fragrance-creation-worldwide)
- Precedent value: the VoU survey is a working, antitrust-vetted mechanism by which competing fragrance houses already send confidential per-ingredient data to a neutral body, which publishes only aggregates. The same machinery could in principle collect aggregated potency data such as median threshold per ingredient (inference; see Inferences).

**FEMA / IOFI / EFFA (flavor side)**
- The FEMA Flavor Ingredient Library is open to members and non-members. It gives identifiers, GRAS status and links to GRAS publications and safety assessments. Nothing indicates it holds odor or flavor thresholds — [FEMA Flavor Ingredient Library](https://www.femaflavor.org/fema-flavor-ingredient-library)
- FEMA has a member guidance document on sensory testing for flavorings with modifying properties ("Member Distribution"). It shows that FEMA members do sensory testing under FEMA protocols, but the data are for regulatory submissions, not a shared database — [FEMA Sensory Guidance PDF](https://www.femaflavor.org/sites/default/files/2024-06/FEMA%20Sensory%20Guidance%20January%202022%20Member%20Distribution%20020322%20final.pdf)
- EFFA: no specific source found in this session on EFFA data holdings (see Gaps).

**DVRH (Deutscher Verband der Riechstoff-Hersteller, German fragrance manufacturers' association)**
- DVRH is based in Berlin and has 18 member companies (14 regular, 4 supporting). It covers about 90% of fragrance companies in Germany, including global players (Symrise is German) and SMEs. Its statutes include "coordinat[ing] and promot[ing] the joint study of scientific and technical questions concerning fragrances among member companies". It is an IFRA member — [DVRH portrait / search summary](https://duftstoffverband.de/home/dvrh-verbandsportraet/); [DVRH members](https://duftstoffverband.de/home/mitglieder-dvrh/); [EFEO listing](https://efeo.eu/about-us/members/germany-dvrh-german-asso-of-the-fragrance-manufacturers/)

**BACIS / Leffingwell (commercial compilers)**
- **[unverified]** BACIS (Boelens Aroma Chemical Information Service, NL) sells the *Compilations of Odour Threshold Values in Air, Water and Other Media* (L.J. van Gemert; 2003 and 2011 editions) and the VCF (Volatile Compounds in Food) database. These are the largest literature threshold compilations. They are commercial licenses, not consortia. Their data come from published literature, not company-internal measurements.
- **[unverified]** Leffingwell & Associates licensed an odor-descriptor dataset that was later combined with Firmenich data (see Section 2) and used in Google/Osmo work. It is a precedent for commercial compilers licensing data for ML.

**Public-sector German threshold resource close to the user**
- The Leibniz-LSB@TUM Odorant Database (Freising, near Munich) lists prevalent food odorants with retention indices, odor qualities, and "odor threshold values in different matrices", plus the foods in which they occur in odor-active amounts — [Leibniz-LSB@TUM Odorant Database](https://www.leibniz-lsb.de/en/databases/leibniz-lsbtum-odorant-database/start/); [user guide](https://www.leibniz-lsb.de/en/databases/leibniz-lsbtum-odorant-database/user-guide). It covers food odorants, not the fragrance palette, and its size and license terms were not checked here.

### Inferences
- The only trade-body asset that directly targets odor potency is RIFM's ongoing human odor-threshold program. It is likely small (tens of materials?) but measured with modern, consistent methods. That makes it valuable as a high-quality calibration and test set, not as bulk training data. The route in would be a collaboration proposal to RIFM's science staff, framed around a use RIFM cares about, e.g. a threshold model for respiratory/sensory safety, read-across, or NAMs.
- IFRA's VoU survey is the strongest proof that the fragrance industry already has antitrust-cleared, confidential, aggregated data pooling. A proposal could reuse that governance template, e.g. companies submitting per-ingredient threshold/intensity values to a neutral custodian such as RIFM or a university trustee, with only aggregates or a trained model released.
- DVRH is the natural German door-opener (Berlin; Symrise plus SMEs). It is also the kind of association that could act as the industry side of an IGF-style project (Section 4). Note that DVRH is not an IGF "Forschungsvereinigung" itself as far as found; FEI or DECHEMA would be.

### Gaps
- RIFM database subscription tiers, prices, and whether an academic or student tier exists: not found (the page shows only a login wall). Contact RIFM directly.
- Whether the RIFM Database contains odor thresholds or odor-intensity fields at all: not confirmed.
- Scope, size and publication plans of RIFM's new odor threshold study: not public.
- EFFA and IOFI data holdings relevant to potency: no sources found.
- BACIS/van Gemert pricing and licensing for ML use: not verified in this session.

## 2. Precompetitive and federated consortia in other industries (and any olfaction precedents) that could be copied

### Takeaway
Cross-company federated learning on proprietary chemistry data is proven at scale in pharma. MELLODDY (10 pharma, IMI-funded, 2019–2022) ran QSAR models on 2.6B data points; AISB/OpenFold3 (5 pharma, results Sept 2026) and the Apheris ADMET Network (launched Feb 2026) are current examples. No equivalent fragrance/flavor consortium for odor prediction was found. The closest fragrance precedents are one-off data releases for public challenges: Firmenich's "Learning to Smell" (2020–21) and the DREAM Olfaction Challenge, which used academic data.

### Cited Findings
**MELLODDY (Machine Learning Ledger Orchestration for Drug Discovery)**
- 10 pharma partners (Amgen, Astellas, AstraZeneca, Bayer, Boehringer Ingelheim, GSK, Janssen, Merck KGaA, Novartis, Servier) plus academic partners BME Budapest and KU Leuven — [MELLODDY FAQ](https://www.melloddy.eu/faqs)
- Budget €18.4M: EFPIA companies €10M in kind, public partners up to €8M EU grant (IMI2 JU grant No 831472), plus an NVIDIA contribution — [search summary of Owkin/MELLODDY pages](https://www.melloddy.eu/blog/melloddy-a-co-opetitive-platform-for-machine-learning-across-companies-powered-by-owkin-technology)
- Protection model: "The data never leaves the owner's infrastructure and only non-sensitive models are exchanged". A private blockchain ledger across partners meant there was "no central authority". A central dispatcher required unanimous partner approval for operations, with full activity logging, designed to "prevent the leaking of proprietary information from one data set to another" — [MELLODDY FAQ](https://www.melloddy.eu/faqs)
- Platform: Owkin's Substra, since open-sourced and hosted by the Linux Foundation (LF AI & Data) — [Owkin](https://www.owkin.com/newsfeed/owkin-launches-open-science-push-by-open-sourcing-ai-software-substra-and-releasing-two-open-source-ai-innovations-at-neurips)
- Results: every one of the 10 companies improved its own classification/regression models. The dataset covered 2.6+ billion confidential activity data points, 21+ million molecules and 40,000+ assays. Gains kept growing with more data but with "saturating return", and were "markedly higher" for PK and safety-panel tasks. Interim median conformal-efficiency gain was 5.5% (up to 9.7%). Published in J Chem Inf Model 2024, 64(7), 2331–2344 — [JCIM](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acs.jcim.3c00799); [ChemRxiv preprint](https://chemrxiv.org/doi/10.26434/chemrxiv-2022-ntd3r); platform paper [arXiv 2210.08871](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.08871)
- **[unverified, from the MELLODDY paper as recalled]** Architecture: a shared multitask "trunk" trained federatedly, with private per-company "heads". Assay and task identities stayed private. Molecules were featurized locally with an agreed hashed fingerprint (via the open-source MELLODDY-TUNER and SparseChem) so no structures were exchanged.

**AI Structural Biology (AISB) Network / federated OpenFold3 (Apheris)**
- Members: AbbVie, J&J (founders), Astex, BMS, Takeda. The academic partner is the AlQuraishi Lab (Columbia), which develops OpenFold3 under an Apache license — [Apheris AISB results](https://www.apheris.com/resources/federated-training-dramatically-improves-the-accuracy-of-protein-ligand-co-folding-on-private-pharma-structures); [STAT](https://www.statnews.com/2025/03/27/abbvie-johnson-and-johnson-pharma-data-sharing-openfold3-ai-drug-discovery/)
- Results (published Sept 2026): trained on 20,167 private structures with 1,056 held out. High-quality interface predictions rose from 35.6% to 52.1%, and correct ligand poses from 28.9% to 46.8%. The model beat both public models and any single-partner fine-tune. Fine-tuning took under ten weeks — [Apheris](https://www.apheris.com/resources/federated-training-dramatically-improves-the-accuracy-of-protein-ligand-co-folding-on-private-pharma-structures); [Bio-IT World, 22 Sep 2026 (headline: "four big pharma are betting bigger")](https://www.bio-itworld.com/news/2026/09/22/apheris-announced-the-results-of-their-federated-learning-co-folding-initiative-now-four-big-pharma-are-betting-bigger)
- Protection: "Data stays inside each partner's environment, enforced by the Apheris Gateway". Only parameters were aggregated. Membership-inference and data-reconstruction attack tests found "no meaningful privacy risk". AWS provided infrastructure and NVIDIA compute. The federated weights appear to be private to participants — [Apheris](https://www.apheris.com/resources/federated-training-dramatically-improves-the-accuracy-of-protein-ligand-co-folding-on-private-pharma-structures)
- Key structural feature to copy: an **academic lab owns an open base model**, and industry fine-tunes it federatedly on private data.

**Apheris ADMET Network (2026)**
- Launched Feb 2026 with founding members Lundbeck, Orion Pharma, Recursion, Servier and one undisclosed member. Each committed ~80% of its relevant data to a shared ADMET foundation model. It covers >100 endpoints. Members fine-tune locally and run inference locally — [Apheris ADMET launch](https://www.apheris.com/resources/blog/apheris-launches-admet-network); [GEN](https://www.genengnews.com/topics/artificial-intelligence/admet-predictions-get-ai-boost-federated-data-network-unites-pharma/)
- Relevance: ADMET endpoints (solubility, volatility-adjacent physchem, PK) are assay-heterogeneous and noisy, much like odor thresholds. The finding that federation pays off on noisy, cross-lab assay data carries over by analogy.

**Fragrance/olfaction precedents**
- Firmenich's "Learning to Smell" challenge on AIcrowd (Round 1: 8 Sep–27 Oct 2020; Round 3 ended 15 Feb 2021; linked to AMLD EPFL 2021) released SMILES with multi-label odor descriptors (109 odor words). It covered descriptors, not potency — [AIcrowd](https://www.aicrowd.com/challenges/learning-to-smell); [AMLD](https://appliedmldays.org/events/amld-epfl-2021/challenges/learning-to-smell-challenge)
- Firmenich's challenge data were later merged with the Leffingwell set into an "Integrated dataset" of 7,374 molecules and 109 odor classes, used in academic work — [PLOS One 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12118925/)
- The DREAM Olfaction Prediction Challenge used Keller & Vosshall's academic dataset (49 subjects, 476 molecules, with intensity and pleasantness ratings), not industry data. Models predicted intensity and pleasantness plus 8 of 19 descriptors — [Science 2017](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aal2014); [DREAM site](https://dream-olfaction.github.io/)
- Firmenich–EPFL partnership (2018) on a digital lab for AI in fragrance development — [Glossy/search summary](https://www.glossy.co/beauty/artificial-intelligence-is-quietly-disrupting-the-fragrance-development-process/)
- Company AI tools are strictly in-house: Givaudan "Carto", Symrise/IBM "Philyra" — [Perfumer & Flavorist](https://www.perfumerflavorist.com/events/event-coverage/news/21873544/givaudan-artificial-intelligence-the-future-of-fragrance); [stellarix](https://stellarix.com/insights/articles/ai-fragrance-creativity-compliance/)
- EU projects found on olfaction ML are about sensing and diagnostics, not odor potency, and have no fragrance-house partners. SMELLODI (EIC Pathfinder, GA 101046369) has TU Dresden, Uni Jena, Hebrew Univ., Tampere and SmartNanotubes — [CORDIS](https://cordis.europa.eu/project/id/101046369). POTION (H2020, GA 824153) studies social body-odor chemosignals — [CORDIS](https://cordis.europa.eu/project/id/824153). "ROBORACE" is not an olfaction project, and no "SmartSmell" EU project was found.
- A search for a 2025–2026 precompetitive fragrance-industry consortium on odor prediction returned only academic ML papers, e.g. a 2026 RSC Advances ordinal odor-strength dataset of >2,300 molecules built from two public sources — [RSC Advances 2026](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlelanding/2026/ra/d6ra01805j); [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13182554/)

### Inferences
- No fragrance equivalent of MELLODDY or AISB exists (as of Sept 2026, to the extent searchable). An outsider would be proposing something new, not joining something that exists.
- The AISB template fits a university best. It has an academic group with an open base model (here: an LMU odor-potency model trained on public data), 3–5 companies fine-tuning federatedly on private threshold/intensity data, and a neutral platform provider. Companies keep the federated weights, and the academic partner publishes the method and aggregate benchmarks.
- MELLODDY shows the business case needs several sizable data holders to see gains, and gains saturate. The fragrance market is highly concentrated: Givaudan, dsm-firmenich, IFF and Symrise are the big four, with Symrise in Germany. A 3–4-company federation is therefore structurally feasible, but each company has far fewer measured thresholds than pharma has assay points.
- The main obstacle is less technical than competitive. Potency and performance data are close to the core IP of perfumery (substantivity, "bloom", cost-in-use), unlike pharma's many precompetitive assays. Safety-framed potency, i.e. thresholds for sensory/respiratory safety via RIFM, is the most precompetitive framing available.

### Gaps
- MELLODDY post-project fate (model reuse, continued network) not documented in the sources fetched.
- Pistoia Alliance: no fragrance/olfaction initiative found. I did not fetch Pistoia-specific federated learning material.
- Open Reaction Database: not researched in detail (it is a open-contribution repository, not federated). Not verified in this session.
- Apheris and Owkin commercial pricing: not public.

## 3. Round-robin / interlaboratory odor threshold studies and publicly funded threshold measurements

### Takeaway
Interlaboratory work shows odor threshold values for the same substance differ by 2–3 orders of magnitude across labs, and olfactometer sensitivity varies by ≥10× in interlab comparisons. Recent compilations (848 flavor compounds, 2026; 63 VOCs, 2025) publish data in supplements but involve no industry data. German public contracts (UBA/Olfasense) produce small, high-quality, public EN 13725 datasets.

### Cited Findings
- Yang et al., *Comprehensive Reviews in Food Science and Food Safety* 2026, compiled odor thresholds for 848 flavor compounds across 11 matrices from 146 studies (1952–2024). Authors are academic (Beijing Technology and Business Univ., South China Univ. of Technology) and no industry data sources are named. Data are in supplementary tables A.1–A.7, with no separate open repository stated. They note differences "up to two to three orders of magnitude" between laboratories, and that inconsistent data undermine "the feasibility of developing reliable predictive models" — [PMC13420774](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13420774/); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42528307/)
- A 2025 *Building and Environment* review analyzed ODTs of 63 VOCs from 35 studies over five decades. It fit predictive exponential models for aldehydes, alkanes and aromatics, and called for consensus on panelist training, fixed dilution step factors and presentation mode — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0360132325006031)
- Classic finding: compiled ODT values for the same substance "rarely exhibit accuracy better than three orders of magnitude". Dynamic olfactometers varied in sensitivity to reference odorants "by a factor of 10 or more in interlaboratory comparison tests" — [Sensors 2012 (Cometto-Muñiz/Abraham-type review; MDPI)](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/12/4/4105); [overview of ODT methods, 2023](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1569904823000964)
- UBA (German Environment Agency) project FKZ 3717 61 250 0, "Bestimmung von Geruchswahrnehmungsschwellen für Innenraumschadstoffe", was carried out by Olfasense GmbH, Kiel (Brosig & Maxeiner), finished Feb 2020 and published July 2023. It measured thresholds for 20 indoor pollutants per DIN EN 13725, intensities and Weber-Fechner coefficients per VDI 3882-1, and hedonics per VDI 3940-4, with analytical concentration checks (FID, GC-MS, GC-IMS). The full 203-page report is free — [UBA Umwelt & Gesundheit 04/2023](https://www.umweltbundesamt.de/system/files/medien/11850/publikationen/04_2023_ug_bestimmung_von_geruchswahrnehmungsschwellen.pdf)
- AIHA *Odor Thresholds for Chemicals* (4th ed.) is a commercial critically-evaluated compilation — [AIHA preview](https://aiha-assets.sfo2.digitaloceanspaces.com/AIHA/aihaconnect/Educational-Books-Preview/Odor-Thresholds-for-Chemicals-43-PREVIEW.pdf)

### Inferences
- The UBA/Olfasense study shows a model: a government contract to an accredited olfactometry lab (EN 13725, n-butanol-calibrated panel) produces a small, consistent, public dataset. A comparable commissioned set of ~50–200 fragrance materials is plausible as a calibration anchor, and cost is its main limit.
- With 2–3 log-units of inter-lab spread, even federated access to company data needs per-source calibration. Useful ML-level answers: per-lab offsets, shared reference odorants such as n-butanol, and a method-type covariate. A consortium design should require each partner to include a few common reference compounds, which would amount to a mini round-robin.

### Gaps
- No published EN 13725 or ASTM E679 round-robin involving fragrance-house labs on fragrance materials was found. EN 13725 interlab proficiency tests (n-butanol, H2S) exist but were not fetched here.
- The Olfasense report's per-substance tables were not extracted (only the abstract and table of contents).

## 4. Public funding instruments (Germany/EU) that could incentivize company data contributions

### Takeaway
The best-fitting German instrument is IGF (Industrielle Gemeinschaftsforschung, BMWE) via FEI or another research association. It is precompetitive by design, requires a committee of 7–12 companies (FEI rules), and expects in-kind contributions from participating companies, which can include data and samples. Past FEI/IGF projects already funded aroma/odorant work at DFA/TUM in Freising. EU equivalents (IMI → MELLODDY) show how in-kind industry data can be counted as co-funding.

### Cited Findings
- IGF is a theme-neutral, precompetitive BMWK/BMWE program for SME access to research. Applications can only come from research associations (Forschungsvereinigungen). A project advisory committee (Projektbegleitender Ausschuss) of at least 3 industry representatives, including ≥3 SMEs, steers each project. "All participating companies should participate in the project through project-related in-kind contributions" — [Förderdatenbank](https://www.foerderdatenbank.de/FDB/Content/DE/Foerderprogramm/Bund/BMWi/industrielle-gemeinschaftsforschung.html); [DLR PT](https://projekttraeger.dlr.de/de/foerderung/foerderangebote-und-programme/foerderprogramm-industrielle-gemeinschaftsforschung-igf); [DECHEMA IGF](https://dechema.de/Forschung/IGF.html)
- FEI specifics: the committee must have 7–12 companies, at least 6 of them SMEs. Review runs through FEI formal checks, expert review (6–8 weeks), the Scientific Advisory Board, IGF reviewers, and a final BMWE decision. Projects must be application-oriented, precompetitive and SME-relevant, not basic research — [FEI "Der Weg des Antrags"](https://www.fei-bonn.de/PRINT/gefoerderte-projekte/von-der-idee-zum-projekt/der-weg-des-antrags)
- Precedents for FEI/IGF funding of odorant work:
  - DFA Freising and TUM identified the key aroma compounds of orange juice and their concentration–effect relationships — [FEI project of the month 07/2013](https://www.fei-bonn.de/PRINT/gefoerderte-projekte/projekte-des-monats/2013-07-juli)
  - AiF 17010 N, "Aromawert-Index", for apple juice — [FEI](https://www.fei-bonn.de/gefoerderte-projekte/projektdatenbank/aif-17010-n.projekt)
  - German aroma manufacturers initiated two IGF projects to build a secured authenticity database for aroma substances (Prof. Schreier, Würzburg). This is a direct precedent of flavor companies co-creating a shared reference database through IGF — [FEI best-practice project](https://www.fei-bonn.de/gefoerderte-projekte/best-practice-projekte/bpp-2009-02-aromastoffe-artikel)
  - TUM chair lists several AiF/IGF aroma projects (18626 N, 18563 N, 16717 N) — [TUM IGF projects](https://www.mls.ls.tum.de/en/lms/publications/igf-projects/)
- An IGF committee-meeting guideline (IUTA, 2018) exists for how committee meetings and in-kind documentation work — [IUTA guideline PDF](https://www.iuta.de/download/6/rundschreiben-der-igf/1593/rs_leitfaden_pa_2018-03-26.pdf)
- EU model: MELLODDY's €10M in-kind from companies was matched by €8M EU funding under IMI2 — [MELLODDY/Owkin](https://www.melloddy.eu/blog/melloddy-a-co-opetitive-platform-for-machine-learning-across-companies-powered-by-owkin-technology)

### Inferences
- A concrete German route: an LMU/TUM-Leibniz-LSB group proposes an IGF project via FEI (flavor side) or via a chemistry association such as DECHEMA or GDCh-linked groups (fragrance side). Symrise, other DVRH members and SME flavor houses sit on the committee. Their in-kind contribution is (a) running a common reference panel of odorants in their own labs and (b) federated or local training runs on their internal threshold data. IGF results must be public, which fits publishing the model and method while raw company data stay private.
- IGF's SME requirement means the big four cannot be the only participants. Many German SME flavor/fragrance houses (DVRH, and flavor members of FEI's network) would need to see value, e.g. a free potency predictor they could not build alone.
- BMBF/BMFTR KMU-innovativ, DFG transfer projects (Erkenntnistransfer), Horizon Europe Cluster 6 and EIT Food were not verified in this session. DFG transfer projects generally require a company partner and a cooperation agreement **[unverified]**.

### Gaps
- IGF funding amounts and caps per project, and deadlines for 2026–2027: not retrieved.
- Whether a fragrance (non-food) topic is eligible under FEI's remit, or needs another Forschungsvereinigung: not confirmed.
- BMFTR KMU-innovativ, DFG transfer, Horizon Europe Cluster 6 and EIT Food calls relevant to olfaction AI: not researched within the tool budget.

## 5. Federated learning / secure compute as a way for companies to let a model train on their data without sharing it

### Takeaway
Mature open-source tools (Flower, NVIDIA FLARE, Substra) and commercial orchestrators (Apheris, Owkin) exist and have been used on chemistry QSAR at scale. For an odor-potency model with a few thousand points per company, the technical work is modest. The real hurdles are legal agreements, per-site IT/security approval, harmonizing measurement units and methods, and each company's willingness to be a node.

### Cited Findings
- Flower (research-friendly FL framework) and NVIDIA FLARE (production runtime) are integrated, so Flower apps run on FLARE without code changes — [NVIDIA blog](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/supercharging-the-federated-learning-ecosystem-by-integrating-flower-and-nvidia-flare/); [arXiv 2407.00031](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.00031); [NVIDIA FLARE paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.13291)
- Substra (Owkin) is open source under the Linux Foundation, has a documented privacy strategy, and was used in MELLODDY — [Substra privacy strategy](https://docs.substra.org/en/stable/additional/privacy-strategy.html); [Substra paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.11567)
- Commercial example: the Apheris Gateway keeps data in each partner's environment, and Apheris ran privacy-attack assessments (membership inference, reconstruction) as part of the AISB deliverable — [Apheris](https://www.apheris.com/resources/federated-training-dramatically-improves-the-accuracy-of-protein-ligand-co-folding-on-private-pharma-structures). Apheris markets a chemistry-specific federated platform — [Apheris chemistry](https://www.apheris.com/federated-learning-platform-chemistry)
- Cost anchor: MELLODDY cost €18.4M for 10 companies over ~3 years, most of it in-kind company effort — [MELLODDY/Owkin](https://www.melloddy.eu/blog/melloddy-a-co-opetitive-platform-for-machine-learning-across-companies-powered-by-owkin-technology). AISB fine-tuning took under 10 weeks once set up — [Apheris](https://www.apheris.com/resources/federated-training-dramatically-improves-the-accuracy-of-protein-ligand-co-folding-on-private-pharma-structures)

### Inferences
- For odor potency the data are tiny (hundreds to low thousands of labeled molecules per company). Simpler privacy-preserving designs than full FL may be enough and easier to approve:
  - (a) **Model-to-data evaluation/fine-tuning:** the academic ships a containerized model, the company runs it on-premise and returns only metrics or updated weights.
  - (b) **Federated fine-tuning** of an open base model (AISB pattern) with Flower, which is free and runs on a laptop-class server per site.
  - (c) **Trusted third-party custodian**, e.g. RIFM, which already holds confidential industry data, pooling data under NDA and releasing only a model.
- Small data plus few partners raises the privacy risk of model inversion or membership inference, which could reveal whether a company measured a given (possibly unpublished) molecule. Partners will likely require that structures of proprietary captives never leave, secure aggregation, and possibly differential privacy. Excluding proprietary captive molecules and federating only on commercially available materials would lower the barrier considerably.
- Label heterogeneity (thresholds in air vs water, ASTM E679 vs EN 13725 vs in-house "odor value" or "impact" scales) is the dominant ML difficulty. Multi-task heads per data source, the MELLODDY private-head idea, fit this naturally.

### Gaps
- No public cost figures for Apheris or Owkin engagements.
- No published example of FL applied to sensory or olfaction data specifically was found.
- Company-specific IT and legal requirements (Symrise, Givaudan, dsm-firmenich, IFF) for hosting an FL node are unknown.
