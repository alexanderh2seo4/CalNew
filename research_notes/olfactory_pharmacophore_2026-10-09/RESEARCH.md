# Olfactory pharmacophores: availability, free software, and a calculated local reconstruction

Research date: 9 October 2026, Europe/Berlin. Primary papers, author repositories, data deposits, and official software documentation were checked. Earlier notes in this vault were used as search leads and their claims were rechecked where cited below. This is a targeted research audit, not proof that no other downloadable model exists.

## Findings

**Olfactory pharmacophore models exist.** There are useful receptor-specific hypotheses and fragrance-family olfactophores. However, an open-access article, a downloadable receptor structure, a downloadable pharmacophore hypothesis, and a reproducible calculation are four different levels of availability.

The most useful open coordinate/workflow deposit found is **human OR9Q2**, with representative receptor-ligand models, parameters, and analysis code on Zenodo. For experimental structural grounding, **human OR51E2 (8F76)** and **mouse Olfr412 (9W45)** provide downloadable cryo-EM coordinates. These are inputs for deriving a pharmacophore, rather than ready-made, validated pharmacophore screening models. [OR9Q2 study](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10822-025-00706-0), [OR9Q2 deposit](https://zenodo.org/records/13328860), [8F76](https://www.rcsb.org/structure/8F76), [9W45](https://www.rcsb.org/structure/9W45).

For a compact calculation that can be completed on this Mac and tested against real assay outcomes, **mouse Olfr73** is a practical contender. Its supplement identifies the original known agonists and a separate screen containing both successful and unsuccessful compounds. A subagent was assigned to calculate a ligand-based reconstruction with RDKit and produce molecular overlays, typed feature spheres, scores, and an assay comparison. The reconstruction's outputs and method are documented separately in the local model folder. [Olfr73 paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-019-0384-8).

I did **not** verify a directly downloadable original `.ph4`, `.phar`, `.pml`, Phase hypothesis, or Catalyst hypothesis for the olfactory studies inspected. This is a bounded negative finding. The downloadable PDB/mmCIF/PyMOL files and scripts below make considerably more possible than a blanket claim that no olfactory models can be downloaded.

## What the models describe

| Model class | Input and output | What a successful fit supports |
|---|---|---|
| Ligand-based receptor pharmacophore | Experimentally measured ligands; aligned donor/acceptor, aromatic, hydrophobic, or charge features | A testable hypothesis about the chemistry and geometry associated with activity at that receptor |
| Structure-based receptor pharmacophore | An experimental or modeled receptor-ligand complex; interaction features and potentially excluded volumes | A hypothesis about contacts compatible with that binding pocket and receptor state |
| Fragrance-family olfactophore | Molecules with sensory labels or thresholds; common spatial features | A family- and dataset-specific relationship to an odor character or threshold |
| Pharmacophore fingerprint | Counts or patterns of feature types and distances, used as descriptors | A numerical representation; it need not constitute a single 3D hypothesis that can be drawn as feature spheres |

A receptor-binding fit does not by itself establish activation, inhibition, human odor quality, or a detection threshold. Species, receptor state, concentration, stereochemistry, and the experimental assay matter. Insects' odorant receptor/co-receptor systems also differ from mammalian odorant GPCRs. These distinctions are necessary when choosing the training endpoint, not reasons to abandon modeling. [2025 computational OR review](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0304416525000704), [M2OR paper](https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/52/D1/D1370/7327067).

## A reproducible workflow for this request

1. **Fix one biological endpoint.** For the local run, use published Olfr73 agonist outcomes; retain molecule IDs, names, source pages, and exact chemical identities.
2. **Separate training and evaluation before model construction.** Derive geometry using the prior known ligands. Keep the subsequently screened compounds, including their failed assays, out of parameter tuning.
3. **Calculate conformer ensembles and relax them.** Record seeds, embedding settings, force field, convergence, retained conformers, and energy windows. Explicitly handle stereoisomers/mixtures instead of silently choosing an enantiomer.
4. **Calculate typed features and align molecules.** Preserve the feature-definition file and the transformation used for every selected conformer. An atomic point, ring centroid, hydrophobic group, and projected hydrogen-bond direction are distinct definitions.
5. **Construct a training-supported 3D hypothesis.** Derive centers from aligned features and report support per feature. Tolerance radii are model parameters or estimated dispersion; they are not electronic orbital sizes or molecular electron density.
6. **Screen and export.** Rank by the specified feature-fit function, retain actual aligned 3D conformers, and export all candidates rather than only the attractive examples. Keep force-field energy, fit score, experimental activity, and measured potency in separate fields.
7. **Evaluate and render.** Calculate a held-out ROC curve and show the measured active/inactive outcomes beside the fitted molecules. Render the computed coordinates directly into the interactive viewer and static figure.

These steps define a complete **ligand-based** calculation. They do not include a receptor structure prediction, membrane MD, docking, binding free energy, electronic-structure calculation, or a new biological experiment unless those operations are actually run and recorded. The local README and manifest specify the completed stages.

The original screened Olfr73 compounds were selected by the publication's pipeline, rather than randomly sampled from chemical space. A local evaluation on that panel is informative but cannot establish performance for every odorant or estimate an unbiased library-wide hit rate. Model scores are structural-fit scores, not predicted human smell or potency.

## Reusable data and verified downloads

**M2OR** is a particularly useful source for future human/mammalian receptor-specific modeling: its maintained site supports database download and its author repository includes a fixed CSV release. It stores molecular identifiers, receptor identity, response measurements, and assay context. It is a data source, not a pretrained pharmacophore model. Different assay conditions, isoforms, concentrations, and categorical labels must be reconciled before modeling. [Maintained M2OR site](https://m2or.chemsensim.fr/), [download instructions](https://m2or.chemsensim.fr/resource/how-to), [author repository](https://github.com/chemosim-lab/M2OR).

The research source folder contains the verified downloads, including:

- `or9q2_additional_data.zip`: 89,706,706 bytes; deposited MD5 `10ad4effe77503dc139677312e8be7dc` matched the downloaded archive. Dataset license: CC BY 4.0. The repository deposit date is 2024; the associated article appeared online in December 2025 with a 2026 volume designation.
- `8F76.cif`: native human OR51E2 experimental coordinates.
- `9W45.cif`: mouse Olfr412 experimental coordinates.
- `muguet_s2.zip`: published OR1D2 Bourgeonal docking session and logs, inspected archive listing.
- `slitor25_si.docx`: original moth receptor supplement, downloadable but not an executable Catalyst hypothesis.
- `rose.pdf`: original open CHIMIA paper; page 4 rendered and visually inspected to verify feature types and table/caption ambiguities.

File hashes and sizes are recorded in the research download manifest. Downloads are preserved locally; no user files were uploaded to screening services. Files in these deposits are source data, not instructions to execute their bundled scripts automatically.

## Reading the delivered graphical result

The completed local calculation processed **50 compounds**, with **25 prior known agonists for training** and **25 subsequently screened compounds for evaluation** (17 reported active, 8 reported without an active response). It generated **378 conformers** and retained **328** after relaxation and energy filtering. The resulting reference-seeded consensus contains **six features: one donor, two acceptors, one aromatic site, and two hydrophobic sites**. The donor overlaps spatially with one acceptor, consistent with a shared oxygen feature position; these are not six separate atoms.

The held-out ranking gives **ROC AUC 0.691**, with a deterministic 5,000-replicate stratified bootstrap 95% interval **0.471–0.890**; the top five candidates are all reported active, and eight of the top ten are active. The wide interval includes chance-level discrimination and only samples this same preselected panel. The original panel already has a **68% active fraction**. The fit-versus-potency association among actives is weak (Spearman rho **0.228**, p **0.379**), so the scores must not be used as predicted EC50 or potency rankings. These results are from the actual local computation, not the published Phase model.

The highest-scoring held-out compounds are **4-tert-butyl-2-methoxyphenol (A4)** and **dihydroeugenol (A5)**, both with feature-fit scores approximately **0.998**. This near-perfect geometric fit does not mean a 99.8% probability of activity. All candidates, including high-scoring nonresponders, remain in the exported results.

The completed [interactive viewer](http://127.0.0.1:8766/viewer.html) provides the requested research-style view: computed molecular conformers, superposition of top-scoring screening compounds, and distinct donor, acceptor, aromatic, and hydrophobic feature displays. Visible structures, feature locations, support, scores, and assay labels are traceable to saved calculation files. The viewer also works offline when opened directly from its folder.

All 328 rigid feature-fit optimizations converged, and all selected MMFF structures converged. Independent checks confirmed valid SDF structures, coordinate agreement between JSON and SDF, training-only feature support, exact source/script hashes, the donor-free A8 feature display, and a separately recomputed AUC. The browser overlay and nonhit controls were exercised. See [method and rerun instructions](local_model/README.md), portable reproducible bundle (local artifact omitted from the public snapshot), [calculated scientific figure](local_model/outputs/pharmacophore_panel.png), [validation figure](local_model/outputs/holdout_validation.png), and [independent QA record](QA.json).

Chemical identities are transcribed from the published names. Independent named PubChem graph matching succeeded for 45/50 structures; five remain transparently flagged manual transcriptions. A11 has unspecified E/Z stereochemistry; both forms were calculated and its best score is an optimistic bound.

An acceptor oxygen and a donor hydroxyl may occupy the same position but are different feature types. A model can contain no donor at all; adding a decorative donor sphere would be chemically false. The legend and controls therefore reflect the features that the calculation actually generates. A molecule's electrostatic charges, electron density, and donor/acceptor classifications are different outputs; the displayed feature spheres are pharmacophore interaction features.

For a future exact structure-based project, the most defensible next starting points are the deposited OR9Q2 complexes or experimental 8F76/9W45 coordinates, followed by a separately documented pocket preparation, docking calibration, and held-out assay evaluation. The available downloads make that work possible; they do not make the original conclusions automatically transferable to a different free scoring function.
