# Partnerships, not requests, unlock fragrance potency data

Yes, industry odor-potency data can be reached, but not by asking for a download. Every major house holds the data an odor-potency model needs. Givaudan runs its AI formulation tool on a proprietary "Odour Value Map" built from detection thresholds and vapor pressure. Kao measured dose–response curves for 314 perfumery materials. Symrise calls its formula data "our capital". None of them runs a program that releases raw potency data to outsiders, and no olfaction-, AI- or sensory-data call for proposals was found at any house for 2024–2026. The trade bodies (RIFM, IFRA, FEMA, DVRH) hold safety and usage data, not potency tables. The one exception is RIFM, which has started new human odor-threshold studies with no stated publication plan. Three routes remain realistic for an LMU researcher, in order of reliability. The first is lawful harvesting of what companies already publish (GC-olfactometry thresholds in Givaudan papers and patents, Kao's curves, catalog tenacity). It yields an estimated 1,500–3,000 industry-origin labels and needs nobody's permission. The second is a "model-to-data" or prospective-validation deal in which company data never leaves the company. The third is a publicly funded precompetitive project (German IGF via FEI, with DVRH members on the committee) whose rules make companies contribute in kind. By judgment, the odds that a big house hands over a raw potency dataset are at most 10–20%, and lower still if the data must be publishable. A model-to-data arrangement has roughly 30–50% odds within 12 months. A multi-house federated consortium is under 10% within two years. The fastest door is 20 km from Munich: Leibniz-LSB@TUM in Freising, which curates a threshold database of more than 1,700 odorants and counts Symrise as its top industrial collaborator.

## Every house holds potency data, and every house calls it capital

The data exists, and the companies say so themselves. **Givaudan's Carto tool (2019) is built on the company's "Odour Value Map" to "maximise the olfactive performance in the final formula"** and lets perfumers work with up to 5,000 elements ([Givaudan](https://www.givaudan.com/media/media-releases/2019/givaudan-fragrances-launches-carto-its-artificial-intelligence-powered-tool); [Givaudan Carto page](https://www.givaudan.com/fragrance-beauty/perfumery-school/carto-the-future-of-fragrance-formulations)). Givaudan defines that odour value as "a combination of Odour Detection Threshold and Vapour Pressure" and prints it as an olfactive-impact parameter next to its sustainability metrics ([Givaudan sustainability parameters](https://www.givaudan.com/fragrance-beauty/fragrance-ingredients-business/fragrance-molecules/sustainability-parameters)). **Kao's Sensory Science lab built a dose–response database of 314 perfumery raw materials, rated at several concentrations by 18 perfumers and experienced researchers** ([Kao release 2020](https://www.kao.com/global/en/newsroom/news/release/2020/20200108-001/); [Wakayama et al., IECR 2019](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acs.iecr.9b01225)). dsm-firmenich says it combines "receptor biology, analytical data, sensory data, and consumer insights with advanced computational methods" and works with about 100 outside organizations ([dsm-firmenich IAR 2025](https://annualreport.dsm-firmenich.com/2025/our-company/science-research/our-approach.html)). Givaudan's 2025 receptor programme covered about 400 human odorant receptors, with sensitivity gains of up to 100× ([Givaudan 2025](https://www.givaudan.com/media/trade-media/2025/scent-receptor-research)).

The same companies describe this data as the thing they will not give away. When Symrise built Philyra with IBM Research, it sent recipe data **encrypted and stripped of product and raw-material identifiers**. Its explanation was blunt: "The mixtures are our capital and we don't want to just give it away" ([Symrise](https://www.symrise.com/our-stories/fragrances-from-big-data/)). Givaudan's 2030 strategy lists proprietary data among its unique strengths ([Givaudan 2030 strategy](https://integratedreport.givaudan.com/2025/our-strategy/our-2030-strategy)). Osmo, the AI-native challenger, raised a **$70M Series B in February 2026** on the claim of "the world's largest interconnected scent dataset" ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260204293785/en/Osmo-Digital-Scent-Design-Company-Announces-$70M-in-Series-B-Funding-and-Executive-Leadership-Team-to-Scale-AI-Powered-Olfaction-Technology)). Even its landmark Principal Odor Map paper withheld about 45% of the validation structures and the training code ([Lee et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11898014/)). A further brake on any multi-house data pool is antitrust exposure: Firmenich, Givaudan, IFF and Symrise came under investigation by several competition authorities in 2023 ([Cosmetics Business](https://cosmeticsbusiness.com/news/article_page/Firmenich_Givaudan_IFF_and_Symrise_under_investigation_by_several_antitrust_agencies/207372)).

Industry data historically reaches the public through papers, not data releases. Kao is the only house found to have published a large in-house potency dataset. It did so as an ordinary journal article, and its press release frames the database as internal technology ([Kao](https://www.kao.com/global/en/newsroom/news/release/2020/20200108-001/)). Pyrfume has already scraped the fitted curve parameters for all 314 materials from the supplement, with no licence stated ([pyrfume manifest](https://github.com/pyrfume/pyrfume-data/blob/main/wakayama_2019/manifest.toml)). Firmenich's 2020 "Learning to Smell" challenge released odor *descriptors* for SMILES, not potency ([AIcrowd](https://www.aicrowd.com/challenges/learning-to-smell)). The Keller–Vosshall intensity data behind DREAM and the Monell panels behind the Principal Odor Map are academic ([Science 2017](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aal2014); [PMC11898014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11898014/)).

## Industry bodies hold safety and usage data, not potency, with one exception

**RIFM** is the only body with a live potency project. Its database covers more than 7,000 materials and nearly 200,000 human-health and environmental studies, all toxicology and safety ([RIFM Database](https://rifm.org/rifm-database/)). It sits behind a member login, with a non-member subscription tier whose price and academic terms are not published. The important signal is elsewhere: **"RIFM is now researching odor thresholds, updating earlier studies that looked at the mechanism in humans"**, with modern study designs ([RIFM](https://rifm.org/question/if-i-can-smell-it-is-it-safe/)). Principal Scientist Nikaeta Sadekar presented "respiratory safety and odor threshold research" at RIFM INFOX 2025 in Paris, and Holger Moustakas presented chemical-clustering work at the same meeting, which shows in-house computational capacity ([RIFM INFOX 2025](https://rifm.org/advancing-fragrance-safety-through-innovation-science-and-collaboration-rifm-infox-2025-in-paris/)). Neither the list of materials nor a publication date is public. The set is probably small but methodologically uniform, which makes it more valuable as a calibration and test set than as bulk training data. RIFM's safety assessments are openly published through Elsevier and are a free source of volatility features, not potency labels ([Fragrance Material Safety Resource](https://fragrancematerialsafetyresource.elsevier.com/content/rifm-home)).

**IFRA** holds no potency data, but it has already solved the governance problem any potency consortium would face. Its Volume of Use survey collects confidential per-ingredient data from competing houses every four to five years. Results are "aggregated anonymously and treated under full confidentiality in line with anti-trust obligations" and feed RIFM's safety programme ([IFRA](https://ifrafragrance.org/transparency-list/about-the-ifra-transparency-list)). The 2025 Transparency List names 3,691 ingredients, 3,312 of them used for odor ([IFRA press release](https://ifrafragrance.org/latest-updates/press-releases/ifra-launches-updated-transparency-list-a-comprehensive-overview-of-ingredients-used-in-fragrance-creation-worldwide)). That list is a ready-made definition of the fragrance chemical space a potency model should cover. The VoU mechanism shows that houses will send confidential numbers to a neutral custodian when only aggregates come out.

**FEMA** offers an open Flavor Ingredient Library of identifiers and GRAS status, with no thresholds ([FEMA](https://www.femaflavor.org/fema-flavor-ingredient-library)). Its sensory-testing guidance for flavor modifiers is distributed to members only, and the resulting data serve regulatory submissions, not a shared pool ([FEMA guidance](https://www.femaflavor.org/sites/default/files/2024-06/FEMA%20Sensory%20Guidance%20January%202022%20Member%20Distribution%20020322%20final.pdf)). **DVRH**, the Berlin-based German fragrance manufacturers' association, has 18 member companies covering about 90% of German fragrance firms. Its statutes include coordinating "the joint study of scientific and technical questions concerning fragrances among member companies" ([DVRH](https://duftstoffverband.de/home/dvrh-verbandsportraet/); [DVRH members](https://duftstoffverband.de/home/mitglieder-dvrh/)). DVRH holds no known dataset, but it is the natural recruiting channel for the German SMEs a funded project needs. No sources were found on EFFA or IOFI data holdings.

**Monell's Insiders programme** is often mistaken for a data-sharing door. It lists dsm-firmenich, IFF, Symrise, Takasago and RIFM as members, and its benefits include "special access to intellectual property" ([Monell Insiders](https://monell.org/monell-insiders/)). The data flows from Monell to industry, not the other way. Monell is still useful as a route to collaborators who already hold industry trust, above all Joel Mainland's lab, which ran the Principal Odor Map panel and co-authored the 2025 intensity framework that measured **62 odorants at seven or more concentrations** ([PMC11898014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11898014/); [Pellegrino et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12363845/)).

## Published company data yields an estimated 1,500–3,000 labels without permission

The largest industry-origin dataset available today is the one companies have already printed. **Givaudan's Philip Kraft, group leader for odorant discovery since 2001**, routinely reports GC-olfactometry thresholds in ng/L air for new molecules and their analogues ([ORCID](https://orcid.org/0000-0002-4801-5389)). Examples include an ionone-family dienone at **0.036 ng/L air** and a spirocyclic hydroxy ketone at **0.067 ng/L** ([Kraft 2014](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/cbdv.201400071); [Kraft 2008](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/cbdv.200890108)). Givaudan patents describe the same protocol and report ultra-potent structures, such as a vetiver odorant at **29 pg/L air** ([WO2006133592A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/WO2006133592A1/en); [PubMed 33315304](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33315304/)). Procter & Gamble perfume patents sort raw materials into threshold classes with numeric cut-offs such as ≤50 ppb, which are clean interval labels ([US5919752A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US5919752A/en)). The scientific bonus is that one house protocol means one lab offset, and close analogue series carry structure–activity information that the food literature lacks. The cost is narrow chemistry: mostly musks, ambers, woods and muguet aldehydes.

Catalogs add breadth but weaker labels. Supplier data sheets rarely give a threshold. What they give at scale is **tenacity on blotter** plus vapor pressure: Givaudan's Toscanol lasts 2 days on blotter with a vapor pressure of 0.0113 hPa, Givescone 20 hours, Ultrazur 1 week ([Perfumer's Apprentice – Toscanol](https://shop.perfumersapprentice.com/p-6232-toscanol-givaudan.aspx); [Givescone](https://shop.perfumersapprentice.com/p-6095-givescone-givaudan.aspx); [Ultrazur](https://shop.perfumersapprentice.com/p-5976-ultrazur-givaudan.aspx)). dsm-firmenich quotes FLOROL at 3 days on a smelling strip ([SpecialChem](https://www.specialchem.com/cosmetics/product/dsm-firmenich-florol)). Tenacity mixes volatility with potency, so it belongs in an auxiliary ordinal head with vapor pressure as a covariate, not in the threshold target. Legacy Firmenich compendia remain online as image-encoded PDFs that need OCR ([Firmenich Perfumery Ingredients 2020](https://www.firmenich.com/sites/default/files/Catalogue_leaflet_Perfumery_Ingredients_2020.pdf)). One check is worth an afternoon: Givaudan's downloadable sustainability-profile catalogue may list the odour-impact value per molecule ([Givaudan catalogue PDF](https://www.givaudan.com/files/giv-fb-fragrance-ingredients-sustainability-profile-catalogue.pdf)). If it prints numbers, dividing out vapor pressure recovers a Givaudan-measured threshold for each captive. If it prints only classes, those become interval labels. Safety data sheets are not worth mining, because their "odour threshold" field is almost always blank or copied ([Heliyon 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S240584402500595X)).

The law in Germany supports this route for a university project. Single facts such as "tenacity 2 days" or a threshold value carry no copyright. **§ 60d UrhG** allows research organisations to mine lawfully accessible content for non-commercial research, contract terms cannot override it (§ 60g), and § 87c extends the permission to the sui generis database right ([§ 60d](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/urhg/__60d.html); [§ 60g](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/urhg/__60g.html); [§ 87c](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/urhg/__87c.html)). The Hamburg courts in Kneschke v. LAION read "scientific research" broadly and held that an opt-out written only in natural-language terms was not machine-readable ([Initiative Urheberrecht](https://urheber.info/diskurs/hamburger-olg-urteil-im-fall-laion); [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/blogs/mse-today/2025/robert-kneschke-v-laion)). Givaudan's robots.txt, checked on 26 September 2026, contains no AI or TDM crawler blocks ([givaudan.com/robots.txt](https://www.givaudan.com/robots.txt)). Two limits apply. Extracting a *substantial part* of a protected catalog and republishing it is the sensitive case, so the output should be a fact table of structure, value, unit and source citation, never copied catalog text. And **§ 60d stops applying once a company gains decisive influence and preferential access to results**, after which the weaker, opt-out-able § 44b governs ([§ 60d](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/urhg/__60d.html); [§ 44b](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/urhg/__44b.html)). That has a practical consequence for sequencing: build and publish the public-data model before any company partnership starts, and keep the two workstreams contractually separate. The terms of use of dsm-firmenich, IFF, Symrise and the reseller sites were not read, and LMU's legal office should confirm the setup.

## Model-to-data deals are what companies can say yes to

The contract ladder in German practice runs from a mutual confidentiality agreement, to a data-use or evaluation agreement, to a funded research collaboration or an industrial PhD. The reference templates are the **BMWK model agreements for R&D cooperation (2022)**, which company and university lawyers both recognise ([BMWK Mustervereinbarungen](https://www.bundeswirtschaftsministerium.de/Redaktion/DE/Publikationen/Technologie/mustervereinbarungen-fuer-forschungs-und-entwicklungskooperationen-2022.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=2)). TUM's cooperation brochure, from the neighbouring Munich university, lays out the IP consequences of each contract type ([TUM ForTe](https://www.forte.tum.de/fileadmin/w00bgt/www/Forschungsfoerderung_new/FWK/0083-12-2024_TUM-FWK-Broschuere_dt_engl.pdf)). EU state-aid rules shape the negotiation. If a company wants to own all results, the work becomes contract research and LMU must charge full cost. Shared IP requires a genuine collaboration with shared risk.

The lightest agreement is also the most likely to be signed. In a **model-to-data evaluation**, LMU ships a containerised model, the company runs it on its internal thresholds or odour values, and only error metrics come back. In **prospective validation**, LMU predicts potency for the company's unpublished candidate molecules, the company measures them as usual, and only the hit rate is published. No data crosses the firewall in either design, the company gets an independent benchmark of its own tools, and the researcher gets a publishable result. The negotiating positions that typically hold are a 30–60-day publication review plus up to 90 days for patent filing, no veto over conclusions, the company's data staying the company's, LMU owning code and architecture, and the company receiving an internal-use licence to any weights fine-tuned on its data. The research notes rate the odds of such an arrangement at 30–50%, against 10–20% for handing over a dataset. Both figures are judgment, and the higher one assumes a warm introduction and a working public model to show.

Federated learning is the scaled-up version, and pharma has proved it works on chemistry. **MELLODDY joined 10 pharma companies on 2.6 billion confidential activity data points and 21 million molecules**, with a budget of €18.4M. Every partner improved its own models, but gains saturated as data grew ([MELLODDY FAQ](https://www.melloddy.eu/faqs); [JCIM 2024](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acs.jcim.3c00799)). The closer template is the **AI Structural Biology network**, in which an academic lab (AlQuraishi, Columbia) owns an open base model and five pharma companies fine-tune it federatedly on 20,167 private structures. Correct ligand poses rose from 28.9% to 46.8%, and fine-tuning took under ten weeks ([Apheris](https://www.apheris.com/resources/federated-training-dramatically-improves-the-accuracy-of-protein-ligand-co-folding-on-private-pharma-structures)). The Apheris ADMET Network, launched in February 2026, applies the pattern to noisy cross-lab assay data that resembles odor thresholds ([Apheris](https://www.apheris.com/resources/blog/apheris-launches-admet-network)). The tooling is free: Flower runs on NVIDIA FLARE without code changes, and Substra is open source under the Linux Foundation ([NVIDIA](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/supercharging-the-federated-learning-ecosystem-by-integrating-flower-and-nvidia-flare/); [Substra](https://docs.substra.org/en/stable/additional/privacy-strategy.html)).

Olfaction differs from pharma in ways that cut both ways. Each house probably holds hundreds to low thousands of measured thresholds, not billions of points, so a single on-premise container is often enough and full federation is overkill. Small data also raises the risk that a model reveals whether a company measured a particular unpublished captive. Restricting federation to commercially available materials and excluding captives removes most of that fear. The dominant technical problem is units, not privacy. Company "odour values", GC-O ng/L air, EN 13725 and ASTM E679 thresholds disagree by up to two to three orders of magnitude between labs ([Yang et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13420774/)). Any multi-partner design should therefore use a private output head per data source and require every partner to measure a shared panel of reference odorants. That panel amounts to a small round-robin, and it is worth publishing on its own. No fragrance equivalent of MELLODDY or AISB exists, so this would be a new proposal, not an invitation to join an existing one. Potency and substantivity also sit much closer to perfumery's core IP than most pharma assays do. Framing the consortium around *safety* (sensory and respiratory thresholds, with RIFM as neutral custodian) is the most precompetitive angle available.

## German public funding can make contribution the price of entry

No company-run olfaction grant exists. dsm-firmenich's research grant covers nutrition (up to €50,000) and its venture arm funds startups ([dsm-firmenich grant](https://www.dsm.com/nutrition-research-grant); [dsm-firmenich Ventures](https://our-company.dsm-firmenich.com/en/our-company/ventures.html)). Givaudan's Digital Factory and Symrise's co-creation centres in Singapore and Wageningen publish no calls ([Givaudan](https://www.givaudan.com/our-company/our-2025-strategy/market-trends/digital-innovation-at-givaudan); [Symrise](https://www.symrise.com/newsroom/article/?tx_news_pi1%5Bnews%5D=25647&cHash=1099f3b07865dba543ca7080f7b6d6fb)). The public money has to come from the state, and one instrument fits unusually well.

**Industrielle Gemeinschaftsforschung (IGF)**, funded by the federal economics ministry, is precompetitive by design. Only research associations can apply, and each project is steered by a committee of companies that "should participate in the project through project-related in-kind contributions" ([Förderdatenbank](https://www.foerderdatenbank.de/FDB/Content/DE/Foerderprogramm/Bund/BMWi/industrielle-gemeinschaftsforschung.html)). At FEI, the food-industry research association, **the committee needs 7–12 companies, at least 6 of them SMEs**, and proposals pass formal checks, a 6–8-week expert review, the scientific board and a ministry decision ([FEI](https://www.fei-bonn.de/PRINT/gefoerderte-projekte/von-der-idee-zum-projekt/der-weg-des-antrags)). The precedents sit in Freising: IGF projects funded key-aroma and concentration–effect work at DFA and TUM, and German aroma manufacturers initiated two IGF projects to build a shared, secured authenticity database for aroma substances ([FEI 2013](https://www.fei-bonn.de/PRINT/gefoerderte-projekte/projekte-des-monats/2013-07-juli); [FEI best practice](https://www.fei-bonn.de/gefoerderte-projekte/best-practice-projekte/bpp-2009-02-aromastoffe-artikel)). That is exactly the shape a potency project needs. Committee members contribute reference measurements or local training runs as in-kind effort, IGF rules require public results (the model and method), and raw company data stays private. The SME quota is a feature, not an obstacle: small flavor and fragrance houses cannot build a potency predictor alone and gain most from a shared one. Two points are unconfirmed and must be checked with FEI. The first is whether a fragrance (non-food) topic fits FEI's remit or needs another association such as DECHEMA. The second is current funding caps and deadlines.

A faster, smaller lever is Bavaria's **ESF+ STEP programme: €9M for university-to-SME knowledge transfer in strategic technologies, deadline 31 October 2026** ([LMU](https://www.lmu.de/de/forschung/entrepreneurship-und-transfer/kollaboration-mit-der-industrie/)). That deadline is five weeks away, so it is only realistic if an SME partner is already in hand. The EU route, in which MELLODDY's €10M of company in-kind effort was matched by €8M of IMI funding, shows the model at scale but needs a multi-country consortium ([MELLODDY/Owkin](https://www.melloddy.eu/blog/melloddy-a-co-opetitive-platform-for-machine-learning-across-companies-powered-by-owkin-technology)). BMFTR KMU-innovativ, DFG transfer projects and Horizon Europe calls were not researched.

Stage 1 is the precondition for everything after it. Companies take a meeting when shown a working model with a clearly stated gap, not a request for data. The Leibniz-LSB agreement is the single highest-value step, because the database's terms forbid systematic download without written permission while the group sits next to Munich and inside Symrise's research network ([Leibniz-LSB terms](https://www.leibniz-lsb.de/en/databases/leibniz-lsbtum-odorant-database/copyright-and-citation)). A joint anchor re-measurement gives the curators something they cannot easily produce alone: estimated offsets between the studies inside their own database. For company approaches, contact the publishing scientists before business development, and ask for evaluation metrics and co-authorship, not data. Kao deserves special attention. It has already published once, it is a consumer-goods company for which fragrance IP is less central to revenue, and its lab frames intensity prediction as an engineering problem.

## Conclusion

The question "will companies share their data?" is the wrong frame, because the answer depends almost entirely on what leaves the building. Raw threshold tables will not. Metrics, validation hit rates and fine-tuned weights that stay on company servers plausibly will. The researcher's leverage is not access but offer: an independent benchmark, a model trained on public data that companies can adapt privately, and graduates who can run it. That changes the order of work. A strong public model comes first, partly because it earns the meeting and partly because § 60d protects it only while no company has privileged access.

The deeper opportunity is institutional. The fragrance industry already runs an antitrust-cleared confidential data pool (IFRA's Volume of Use survey) and already funds shared reference databases through IGF. It has never pointed either mechanism at potency. A proposal that reuses those templates around a safety-framed, multi-lab reference panel of fragrance odorants would be the first of its kind. Even at modest odds, its fallback products (a published inter-lab calibration, a Freising partnership, one company benchmark) are worth more than the dataset most researchers wait for and never receive.
