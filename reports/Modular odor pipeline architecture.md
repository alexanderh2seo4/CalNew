# Modularity buys auditability, not raw accuracy

A three-model odor pipeline — quality, potency, combiner — is the right architecture, but not for the reason it is usually proposed. On any single endpoint where a matched dataset exists, the flat end-to-end model wins narrowly: Pellegrino's direct DNN predicts mixture intensity at **RMSE 7.5** while the modular Primacy rule on predicted component intensities reaches **RMSE 8.6** ([PMC12363845](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12363845/)). Modularity earns its place because the label sources are disjoint and none of them contains the target: quality labels (GoodScents–Leffingwell, ~5,000 molecules × 138–152 descriptors) carry no concentration, threshold data (~1,000–2,000 molecules, ~0.7 log units of noise) carries no quality, and dose–response data exists for 62 molecules at seven concentrations plus 314 fitted perfumery curves. No dataset joins quality × concentration × mixture, so an end-to-end model for "perceived profile at a concentration" cannot be trained at all — it can only be assembled. On the featurization question the evidence is unambiguous and cuts against fashion: explicit H-bond donors/acceptors, Abraham solvation descriptors, SMARTS osmophore flags and saturating chain-length transforms are **complementary to learned representations, not superseded by them** — a pharmacophore branch adds +1.2 AUROC points over a graph-only model ([PMC13543736](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13543736/)), and pretrained molecular language models add nothing beyond an RDKit+Morgan baseline on human olfactory ratings ([arXiv:2607.24848](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24848)). The recommended design is a frozen-stacking pipeline with a differentiable psychophysics combiner, scaffold-grouped out-of-fold meta-features, censored-regression losses on thresholds, and Monte-Carlo uncertainty propagation — trainable on a laptop, with the binding constraint being labels rather than compute.

## Explicit H-bonding and chain features recover exactly what fingerprints miss

The strongest explicit structure→potency model in the literature is already a hand-built feature vector. Abraham's linear solvation energy relationship decomposes odor detection threshold into six terms: **E** (excess molar refractivity, dispersion), **S** (dipolarity/polarizability), **A** (summation hydrogen-bond acidity, i.e. donor strength), **B** (summation hydrogen-bond basicity, i.e. acceptor strength), **V** (McGowan volume) and **L** (log gas–hexadecane partition coefficient). Fitted on **353 compounds** harmonized onto the Nagata scale, it reaches **R² = 0.759, SD = 0.819 log units**, with a predictive SD of 0.869 ([Abraham et al. 2012, *Chem Senses* 37:207](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3278675)). That figure sits at the reproducibility floor of the labels themselves, which scatter by roughly 0.66 log units even within one laboratory. The variant that swaps the experimentally-derived L for the structure-computable McGowan volume V loses almost nothing — **R² = 0.735, SD = 0.980** — which makes it the correct default for a pipeline that must accept arbitrary SMILES ([PMC3278675](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3278675)). Descriptors for new molecules come from the UFZ-LSER database, which holds experimental values for **over 7,000 small molecules** queryable by SMILES and falls back to SMILES-calculated values when no measurement exists ([RSC ES&WRT 11:481](https://pubs.rsc.org/ew/article/11/2/481/850181/A-machine-learning-framework-to-predict-PPCP)).

The most revealing part of that model is its correction terms. Four indicator variables — **M for mercaptans, AL for aldehydes, AC for carboxylic acids, UE for unsaturated esters** — carry the extra potency of classes that bulk solvation descriptors systematically under-predict ([PMC3278675](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3278675)). This is the osmophore hypothesis restated as regression: A, B and S do not capture what a thiol does at a receptor. Translated into an ML pipeline, it is a direct argument for carrying SMARTS-based functional-group flags *alongside* continuous physicochemistry rather than trusting a tree ensemble to reconstruct them from bulk properties. Note also that Abraham's A and B are continuous, thermodynamically calibrated versions of RDKit's integer `NumHDonors`/`NumHAcceptors`: a phenol OH and an alcohol OH are both `NumHDonors = 1` but differ sharply in A. Count, strength and charge-weighted surface area (the `PEOE_VSA*` family) are three non-redundant encodings of the same chemistry, and all three belong in the feature vector.

Aliphatic chain length has a known closed form, and it is not monotonic. Across **114 aliphatic compounds spanning nine functional-group classes**, potency follows `pOL = [1 − exp(−k·n^1.4)] · pOL_max` with **R² = 0.90**, where n is the number of non-hydrogen atoms and `pOL_max` is a per-class asymptote ([Zarzo, PMC3355402](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3355402/)). Thresholds fall with chain length, then plateau — for acetates the plateau arrives at **octyl acetate and beyond** ([Cometto-Muñiz & Abraham, PMC2639399](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2639399/)) — and beyond the plateau the cut-off effect takes over, where larger homologs fail to produce a sensory response even at vapor saturation ([Cometto-Muñiz et al.](https://www.osti.gov/etdeweb/biblio/21140906)). The engineering consequences are concrete: feed `1 − exp(−k·n^1.4)` as an engineered feature or fit `pOL_max` as a per-class learned intercept, use a spline or EBM shape function rather than a monotonic GBM constraint (a monotone constraint is actively harmful past C8–C10), and include a `longest_aliphatic_chain × functional_class` interaction, because the cut-off point is series-specific. Zarzo's class ordering of intrinsic potency — **thiols ≪ amines < aldehydes ≈ esters < alcohols ≈ ketones < hydrocarbons** — agrees with Abraham's indicator set on which classes need explicit treatment, and amine thresholds additionally depend on **pKa**, which argues for a protonation-state feature rather than a bare nitrogen count ([PMC3355402](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3355402/)).

Two further structural effects set hard floors. Branching moves potency in a non-obvious direction: **tertiary alkanethiols have clearly lower thresholds than primary or secondary thiols**, and replacing SH with OH or S-methylating the thiol significantly raises thresholds — the SH group is the osmophore, not merely a lipophilicity carrier ([*J Agric Food Chem*, alkanethiol 3D-QSAR](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/jf506135c)). A SMARTS like `[CX4]([CX4])([CX4])([CX4])S` is a one-line feature that a Morgan fingerprint captures only at radius ≥2 and in entangled form. Stereochemistry is the other floor: nootkatone's enantiomers differ by roughly **800-fold** in threshold ([PMC9230113](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9230113/)), and enantiomeric *quality* differences correlate with molecular flexibility ([PMC2610320](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC2610320/)), which means `NumRotatableBonds` can gate how much chirality is expected to matter. Any 2D-only potency model carries an irreducible error contribution from every chiral pair in its training set; the mitigations are chirality-aware fingerprints (`useChirality=True`), an explicit `has_unspecified_stereocenters` flag, and per-molecule stereo-ambiguity uncertainty.

The ablation evidence says combine rather than choose. On **8,681 curated odorants** with three tree models, Morgan fingerprints reached **AUROC 0.828**, molecular descriptors (MolWt, H-donors/acceptors, TPSA, LogP, rotatable bonds, heavy-atom count, ring count) **0.802**, and a SMARTS functional-group fingerprint **0.753**, with the authors concluding that descriptors complemented rather than replaced fingerprints; `MolLogP` (importance ≈ 0.206), `MolWt` (≈ 0.172) and `TPSA` (≈ 0.170) dominated ([*Communications Chemistry* 2025, PMC12462479](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12462479/)). The functional-group block bought interpretability the fingerprints could not: acetate patterns dominated *fruity* (0.26), aldehydes peaked at 0.21 for *citrus*, sulfide motifs at 0.31 for *meaty*. PharmaGNN encodes each pharmacophore region as a **10-dimensional vector** (averaged Gasteiger charge, molar refractivity, averaged logP, local topological polar surface area, centre coordinates and surface distances) and concatenates a 128-d pharmacophore embedding with a 128-d graph attention embedding; combined AUROC **0.8811** versus graph-only 0.8695 and pharmacophore-only 0.8420 on 4,983 molecules ([PMC13543736](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13543736/)). The caveat that matters most for this project: **every one of those ablations is on multi-label quality classification, none on threshold regression.** The marginal value of explicit H-bonding and chain features *for potency specifically* is unmeasured in the literature, and it is the cheapest experiment in the plan below.

## Biology gives one strong stage, one weak one, and one that cannot be calibrated

The transport stage is mature and carries most of the intensity variance. Pellegrino's framework predicts Hill parameters from **127 Dragon descriptors** through a 5-layer DNN at **RMSE 7.1, r = 0.87** on held-out molecules, against a shuffled control at RMSE 20.4 — and a reduced **10-feature** model using molecular weight, vapor pressure, boiling point, MLogP and alpha-area plus five Hill-correlated descriptors matches it at **RMSE 7.2, r = 0.87**. Vapor pressure was the single most important feature by permutation (p < 10⁻⁵) ([PMC12363845](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12363845/)). Losing roughly 1% of performance to gain full mechanistic interpretability is an unusually good trade, and it means the potency stage does not need a large learned encoder. The same physics supports the upstream gate: Mayhew's four criteria for odorousness (volatile enough to enter air, hydrophilic enough to sorb into mucus, hydrophobic enough to enter a binding pocket, and actually activating a receptor) yield a working odorous/odorless classifier from **criteria 1–3 alone**, with the companion tool implementing a logistic boundary in vapor-pressure × logP space and a gradient-boosted variant over MW, Crippen logP, heteroatom count, vapor pressure and boiling point on a curated **1,924-molecule** set ([PNAS 119:e2116576119](https://www.pnas.org/doi/full/10.1073/pnas.2116576119); [github.com/joelmainland/odor-prediction](https://github.com/joelmainland/odor-prediction)). There is also a physical justification for why these cheap descriptors work: the Keyhani–Scherer–Mozell finite-element nasal model routes roughly **10% of inspired flow** through the olfactory airway and produces a total olfactory flux reported to correlate with perceived intensity, with boundary conditions set by odorant solubility and diffusivity in mucus ([PubMed 9219668](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9219668/)). That CFD lineage has never been coupled to a modern perception model, and should be treated as justification rather than as a deployable stage.

The receptor stage is real but bounded by data. Chaining MolOR-predicted OR activations into percept prediction gave **+0.6% AUROC** on GS-LF (5,862 molecules × 152 descriptors), paired t = 5.91, p = 0.004, using a panel of the top 400 most-commonly-bound ORs in M2OR ([bioRxiv 2024.09.16.613334](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.09.16.613334v1.full); published as [Cell Systems](https://www.cell.com/cell-systems/fulltext/S2405-4712(26)00193-6?rss=yes)). With *measured* rather than predicted activations on a narrow chemical family the effect is far larger: an elastic net on the activity patterns of **6 key ORs across 11 concentrations** reached **R² = 0.687 ± 0.059** versus **0.514 ± 0.078** for an XGBoost structure model on 23 eugenol/vanillin-family compounds ([*Chemical Senses* bjaf002](https://academic.oup.com/chemse/article/doi/10.1093/chemse/bjaf002/7994243)). The margin tracks whether activations are measured or inferred, and inference is severely constrained: of ~4,384 documented human OR sequences, only **4.5% have dose–response-confirmed agonists**, 12% have any agonist including screening hits, and **88% remain orphan**, with the complete combinatorial code known for only a handful of odorants ([*Chemical Senses* bjae037](https://academic.oup.com/chemse/article-abstract/doi/10.1093/chemse/bjae037/7821270)). M2OR itself aggregates **75,050 bioassay experiments over 51,395 OR–molecule pairs** ([*NAR* 52:D1370](https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/52/D1/D1370/7327067)), but a predicted 400-dimensional activation vector is mostly interpolated structure, not measured biology. Both successful chained results fed the activation vector *alongside* the molecular embedding, never as an information bottleneck — that is the architectural lesson to copy.

Receptor biology does, however, hand the pipeline two free architectural decisions. Mouse genetics shows that **behavioral threshold is set solely by the most sensitive receptor**, with no contribution from other highly sensitive receptors (deleting TAAR4 impairs phenylethylamine detection; deleting TAAR3 does not) ([*Nat Commun*](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-018-05129-0)). That justifies max- or rank-pooling over receptor activations rather than summation, and primacy coding is the same idea generalized to a top-k set ([*Nat Commun*, primacy code](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-017-01432-4)). Meanwhile the *slope* of the intensity function is a transport property — Stevens exponents show a near-perfect rank-order correlation with water solubility, with fully soluble odorants roughly **2.5× the exponent** of insoluble ones ([*Atten Percept Psychophys*](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/BF03212789)) — while the *position* (threshold) is largely affinity. That factorization, slope from transport and offset from affinity, is exactly how the potency stage should be parameterized. Finally, Hill fits human concentration–response data better than Stevens' power law, Beidler's function or Fechner's log law once the range includes half-maximum intensity ([*Chemical Senses* 23:181](https://academic.oup.com/chemse/article-abstract/23/2/181/541296)), so the dose–response head should be saturating, not power-law.

One handoff cannot be calibrated in physical units. In-vitro EC50s sit in the micromolar range while psychophysical thresholds sit at ppb–ppt, so any concentration passed from a transport stage into a receptor stage must go through a learned or fitted gain, or better, be passed as a *rank* or *relative* quantity — which is conveniently what primacy coding consumes. Assay reproducibility of roughly 0.54 log units on EC50 also caps any continuous receptor-activity regression, which argues for binary or ordinal receptor targets rather than EC50 regression.

## The literature already built the pieces but nobody joined them

Four independent lines have converged on the same three-part decomposition without anyone completing it. Pellegrino 2025 does structure → Hill parameters → mixture intensity, using a **Primacy** rule that averages component intensities evaluated at about **20% of ambient concentration**; it beat linear addition, strongest-component, Euclidean, vector, U-model and competitive-binding rules, reaching **r = 0.85, RMSE 8.6** on 260 mixtures with 7,623 ratings and a panel test–retest of r = 0.97 ([PMC12363845](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12363845/)). Pellegrino 2026 does the quality half: mixture quality is the **unweighted average of component 51-descriptor RATA vectors**, reaching per-mixture **r ≈ 0.71**, statistically indistinguishable from the test–retest ceiling (t = 0.72, p = 0.51), and even fitting eight literature "emergent/suppressive" mixtures at r = 0.84. Crucially, **descriptor-vector length tracks perceived intensity (r = 0.77 single, 0.69 mixtures) while direction encodes quality**, and pleasantness follows the intensity-*weighted* average (r = 0.78) ([PMC13371102](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13371102/)). That geometric result is direct empirical support for factorizing the output into magnitude (potency model) × direction (quality model). POMMix stacks a pretrained POM GNN, a self-attention CheMix aggregator and a scaled cosine head, reaching **ρ = 0.779 ± 0.028** on 743 mixtures versus 0.485 for XGBoost+RDKit and 0.399 for the Snitz baseline — but it states explicitly that it "does not explicitly account for intensity or concentration" ([arXiv:2501.16271](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.16271v1)). And the chemical-engineering tradition got there first: Perfumery Radar chains liquid-phase activity coefficients → headspace concentration → odor value or Stevens' law → olfactive-family quality via the stronger-component rule ([*I&EC Res* 2010](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/abs/10.1021/ie101161v)).

The gap is the join. POMMix and DREAM ignore concentration; Pellegrino 2025 ignores quality; Pellegrino 2026 uses *measured* component profiles rather than a structure-based quality model. Whether quality even needs concentration is worth stating honestly: in Keller & Vosshall's 480-molecule two-concentration study, **98% of molecules perceived as stronger than water were rated more intense at the higher concentration, with a median difference of 33.2 points on a 0–100 scale**, yet descriptor usage was "in general consistent" between presentations, with 2-methyl-1-butanol, isopropyl acetate and thiophene as the notable exceptions ([PMC4977894](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4977894/)). So quality is *mostly* concentration-invariant with a minority of strong exceptions — which is an argument for a concentration-agnostic quality model plus a small learned correction, not for conditioning the whole quality model on concentration. The mechanistic reason the exceptions exist is that each receptor has its own dose–response, so the active-receptor pattern shifts with concentration; the competitive-binding model reproduces receptor mixture responses with median RMSE < 0.1 for 12 of 15 receptors and handles 12-component mixtures at RMSE 0.16 versus 1.91 for summation ([PMC6511041](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6511041/)). Two independent 2026 papers also warn against a shortcut the naive design invites: the odor-strength authors note that detection threshold "does not necessarily equal perceived intensity" and deliberately refused to mix concentration-agnostic ordinal labels with psychophysical data ([PMC13182554](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13182554/)), and the intensity-framework authors state that "suprathreshold intensity cannot reliably be inferred from detection thresholds alone" ([PMC12363845](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12363845/)). A combiner that derives perceived intensity from a predicted threshold alone is building on a link the literature says is weak.

## A recommended pipeline: six stages, three trained, one analytic

The design below assigns each stage its own dataset, its own loss and its own metric, and connects them through interfaces that are either physically meaningful (concentration, Hill parameters) or geometrically meaningful (unit-norm quality direction × scalar intensity).

| Stage | Input → Output | Core features | Loss | Data | Tools |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **S0 Headspace** | SMILES (+ liquid formulation) → gas-phase concentration C_i | Vapor pressure, boiling point, Henry's law, activity coefficients | none (analytic/regressed) | OPERA/EPI Suite predictions; Perfumery Radar thermodynamics | OPERA, EPI Suite KOAWIN, RDKit |
| **T Transport gate** | SMILES → P(odorous), air–mucus partition | MW, Crippen logP, heteroatom count, VP, BP | weighted BCE | Mayhew 1,924 molecules | scikit-learn / LightGBM |
| **A Quality** | SMILES → 138-d descriptor probabilities + unit-norm direction q + 256-d embedding | all six feature blocks below | per-label pos-weighted BCE | GS-LF curated 4,983 (OpenPOM) / 5,862 × 152 | Chemprop v2, OpenPOM/DeepChem |
| **B Potency** | SMILES (+ matrix, + A's frozen outputs) → log₁₀ ODT, ordinal strength, (I_max, log EC50, n) | transport + H-bond + osmophore + chain/shape blocks | Tobit/bounded-MSE + Huber (δ=1.5); CORN; Gaussian NLL on Hill params | Hou 1,003 water; Li 716; Abraham/pyrfume 268 air; RSC 2,393 ordinal; Wakayama 314 curves; Pellegrino 62×≥7 | Chemprop v2 (bounded MSE), LightGBM, coral-pytorch |
| **R Receptor (optional)** | SMILES → 400-d OR activation vector | ESM-2 receptor embedding × molecular graph cross-attention | weighted BCE on binary activity | M2OR 51,395 pairs | MolOR / Receptor2Odorant |
| **C Combiner** | {q_i, Hill_i, C_i} → profile at concentration; mixtures | analytic psychophysics + small learned residual | MSE on intensity; cosine/RMSE on profile | Pellegrino mixtures; Ma 2021 (222 binary, Etalab); Keller 2016 (2 concentrations) | PyTorch, differentiable Hill layer |

The **six feature blocks** feed A and B in common, cached once per InChIKey. First, a *transport block* (`MolLogP`, `MolWt`, heavy-atom count, `TPSA`, `NumRotatableBonds`, ring count, `LabuteASA`, estimated vapor pressure, water solubility, logKow) — the strongest single block and the one reproducing the top-three SHAP importances in [PMC12462479](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12462479/). Second, an *H-bonding block* combining topological counts (`NumHDonors`, `NumHAcceptors`, `NHOHCount`, `NOCount`), energetic strengths (Abraham **A** and **B** from UFZ-LSER with a `descriptor_source` provenance flag), and charge-weighted surface areas (`PEOE_VSA*`). Third, an *osmophore block*: a hand-curated SMARTS vector covering thiol, sulfide, disulfide, thioester, aldehyde, ketone, carboxylic acid, ester, lactone, α,β-unsaturated carbonyl, pyrazine, alkoxypyrazine, furan, thiazole, pyrrole, phenol, alcohols and amines by degree — instantiating Abraham's M/AL/AC/UE indicators and Zarzo's class effects, with RDKit's ~85 `fr_*` counters covering much of it free. Fourth, a *chain/shape block*: longest aliphatic chain, the saturating transform `1 − exp(−k·n^1.4)`, counts of tertiary and quaternary carbons, Kier `Kappa1-3`, `Chi` connectivity indices, `FractionCSP3`, branch points. Fifth, a *pharmacophore block*: Gobbi 2D pharmacophore fingerprints (conformer-free, safe for flexible molecules), with PharmaGNN-style 3D vectors reserved for rigid scaffolds where a single low-energy conformer dominates. Sixth, a *generic fingerprint block*: Morgan/ECFP with `useChirality=True` plus MACCS.

Stage C should be a **differentiable psychophysics layer with a small learned residual**, not a free-form network. Compute each component's intensity as `I_i = Hill_i(0.2·C_i)` and aggregate by the Primacy weighted average; compute the mixture quality direction as `q_mix = normalize(Σ w_i q_i)` with w_i either uniform (the Pellegrino 2026 default) or a learned function of I_i; emit the profile as `I_mix · q_mix`; compute pleasantness as the intensity-weighted average of component pleasantness. Then add a small MLP residual on the component set, and a FiLM-style concentration correction applied only to the hedonic and trigeminal/chemical/decayed dimensions, where intensity-coupling is documented. Using an unweighted average for quality and an intensity-weighted one for pleasantness reproduces the published geometry, but the Pellegrino stimuli were plausibly intensity-matched, so for formulations with very unequal component intensities the weighting must be learned and tested rather than assumed. Replace odor activity value entirely: OAV fails because Hill slopes and maxima differ, so at OAV = 100 odorants span a wide range of intensities ([PMC12363845](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12363845/)).

## Train the stages separately, connect them out-of-fold, evaluate against noise ceilings

The default should be **staged training with a frozen stage-A encoder**, not joint multitask. The only directly relevant experiment — a shared GINE/PNA encoder with separate heads for vapor pressure (1,852 molecules) and odor threshold (1,042 molecules, 598 air / 444 water) — found that naive multitask *degraded* the primary task, with VP normalized MSE rising from **0.210 single-task to 0.224 naive-MT**, attributed to negative transfer under task imbalance. Their "safe-MT" recipe recovered it to 0.208 ± 0.004 (R² 0.904), but the best odor-threshold numbers in that paper remain the **single-task** ones (MSE 0.605 ± 0.017) — multitasking helped the data-rich task, not the data-poor one ([arXiv:2601.16426](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.16426)). If joint training is attempted anyway, port their four ingredients exactly: delayed auxiliary activation (epoch 30, after the primary stabilizes), linear warmup of the auxiliary weight over 90 epochs to λ = 10⁻³, an optional `detach()` on the shared backbone, and per-task checkpointing because the endpoints do not reach their optima at the same epoch. POMMix's experience suggests one exception — end-to-end fine-tuning at a low encoder learning rate *after* staged training lifted ρ from 0.746 to 0.779, mostly on rank metrics — but the same paper shows badly-chosen auxiliary pretraining (GS-LF Jaccard similarities) dropping ρ from 0.746 to 0.628 ([arXiv:2501.16271](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.16271v1)). Negative transfer is real in olfaction, and it is the default outcome, not the edge case.

Connecting the stages is textbook stacking with a textbook failure mode. If stage-A predicted descriptors for a molecule came from a stage-A model that saw that molecule's labels, stage B trains on optimistically clean meta-features and is miscalibrated in deployment. The fix is **out-of-fold generation of stage-A meta-features nested inside a scaffold-aware outer split**, with one global molecule-level grouping — Bemis–Murcko scaffold, or Butina/Tanimoto clusters at ≥0.8 as the odor-strength work used ([PMC13182554](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13182554/)) — assigned once over the union of all stages' molecules and stored in the featurization cache. The two candidate meta-features leak differently: a frozen *embedding* leaks via representation memorization (lower risk, still needs group-consistent folds), while predicted *descriptor probabilities* leak directly and must be OOF. Evaluate both; the probability vector is the interpretable one, the embedding usually the stronger. Report mean ± sd over at least three independent scaffold splits, as the safe-MT paper does, because with n ≈ 1,000 the variance of a single 10% test fold is large ([arXiv:2601.16426](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.16426)).

Losses are stage-specific and mostly solved. Stage A: per-label positive-weighted BCE (`pos_weight = N_neg/N_pos`, capped) — the low-risk default, with focal loss as an ablation, since the odor-strength authors chose plain class-weighted losses and no odor-specific evidence favors focal. Stage B thresholds: log₁₀-transform, aggregate duplicates by log-space median, winsorize at the 2–2.5% quantiles per medium, and train with a **censored likelihood** — Tobit NLL for ">x" and "<x" records, Huber (δ = 1.5) for observed ones. Chemprop v2's **bounded MSE** gives the censored half without custom code ([Chemprop, JCIM 64:9](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10777403/)), while a custom Tobit NLL buys censoring plus a variance head in one. Censored rows must not be dropped or imputed at the bound: ">10 ppb" is real information, and imputing it biases the model toward the detection limit, which is precisely where weak odorants live. Stage B ordinal: CORN (which drops CORAL's weight-sharing constraint and chains conditional rank probabilities) optimized against **macro-averaged MSE** with category-weighted losses ([arXiv:2111.08851](https://arxiv.org/html/2111.08851v5); [PMC13182554](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13182554/)). Stage B dose–response: regress the **parameters** (I_max, log EC50, n), not curve points, so the combiner stays analytic and evaluable at any concentration — and note that log EC50 is a suprathreshold analogue of the detection threshold, making stage-B threshold outputs the natural prior for the EC50 head. Because those three sub-targets are not interchangeable, stage B must expose all of them and let stage C learn which to use, with an ablation quantifying each one's contribution.

Uncertainty propagates by Monte Carlo, not by the delta method, because the Hill curve is nonlinear and errors near EC50 are where the curve is steepest — exactly where threshold-style decisions are made. Draw 200–1,000 samples from stage A's calibrated per-descriptor distribution and stage B's predictive distribution (MVE, evidential, or deep ensembles; Chemprop v2 ships all of them plus Z-scaling, Zelikman-interval and conformal calibrators and calibration-area / expected-normalized-error evaluators — [Chemprop UQ docs](https://chemprop.readthedocs.io/en/main/uncertainty.html)), push each sample through C, and read the empirical quantiles. Deep ensembles (5–10 members across seeds *and* scaffold folds) are the pragmatic default at n ≈ 1,000 with 0.7-log noise, where epistemic uncertainty dominates. Conformalizing each stage separately does **not** give end-to-end coverage: if an honest interval on "perceived intensity at concentration c" is needed, conformalize that final quantity on a held-out end-to-end calibration set using the MC-propagated point prediction as the base predictor. For stage A, multi-label **risk control** (MAPIE's RCPS/CRC/LTT, which guarantee recall at any risk level) is more useful to a formulator than 138 independent calibrated probabilities ([MAPIE](https://github.com/scikit-learn-contrib/MAPIE)). And because calibration data is the binding constraint at this scale, use cross-conformal / CV+ rather than split conformal for B and C. Finally, ship the reference distribution alongside the number, as ADMET-AI does by comparing predictions to approved DrugBank drugs filterable by ATC code ([*Bioinformatics* 40:btae416](https://academic.oup.com/bioinformatics/article/40/7/btae416/7698030)): "15th percentile of known perfumery materials" is honest where a point estimate at 0.7-log noise is not.

Evaluation must be anchored to human reliability ceilings, which are known for every stage: intensity panel test–retest **r = 0.97**, quality-profile replicate **r = 0.74** (consensus 0.84), and threshold label noise of roughly **0.66–0.7 log units**. Stage A is scored by per-descriptor AUROC and AUPRC (macro and micro) under scaffold splits, with OpenPOM's **ROC-AUC 0.8872** under 5-fold CV with 10-model ensembles as the reference point ([ARY2260/openpom](https://github.com/ARY2260/openpom/blob/main/README.md)). Stage B has published targets to beat: XGBoost on 121 descriptors over **1,003 aqueous thresholds** reached **test R² 0.827, RMSE 0.83 log units** on a random 80/20 split, with **87% of predictions within tenfold** on 177 independently measured compounds ([Hou et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13558726/)), while a GBDT on ECFP4 over 716 water thresholds reports **R² 0.94, RMSE 0.44** under a scaffold-aware greedy split ([Li et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12654368/)) — a figure below the known reproducibility of threshold measurement, and therefore something to replicate before treating as a target. Stage B thresholds are scored by RMSE/MAE in log₁₀ units, percentage within 3× and 10×, Spearman, and error stratified by max-ECFP4 Tanimoto to training — the single most informative ablation in the literature, since error rises monotonically as similarity falls ([arXiv:2601.16426](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.16426)). Stage C is scored twice, once with **oracle** inputs (measured component profiles and intensities) and once with **predicted** inputs; the gap is the error-propagation cost, and reporting it is what modularity is for. End-to-end evaluation targets the DREAM/Keller design directly: **476 molecules, 49 subjects, 2 concentrations, 21 perceptual attributes** ([Keller et al., *Science* 355:820](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aal2014)). Report shuffled-label controls at every stage — the intensity paper's 15.8–20.4 shuffled RMSEs against 7.1–8.6 real are the cheapest guard against a low-variance target flattering the model.

## The honest verdict: it will not beat end-to-end on accuracy, and that is fine

On any endpoint with a matched training set, expect the modular pipeline to lose slightly. Pellegrino's own comparison has the flat DNN at RMSE 7.5 versus the modular Primacy rule at 8.6 on mixture intensity, and although the splits may not be identical, the direction is clear. POMMix gained from end-to-end fine-tuning after modular pretraining. Nothing in the literature shows a chained odor model beating a direct one on a shared benchmark — the receptor chain's documented gain is **+0.6% AUROC** on a broad benchmark, and the large gain requires *measured* receptor panels on a narrow chemical family. The transport chain's evidence is that a 10-feature physical model *matches* rather than beats a DNN. Three claims in the modular pipeline's favor survive that. First, on the actual target — perceived profile at a stated concentration, and mixtures thereof — there is no end-to-end option, because no dataset contains that label jointly with structure; modularity is a data-fusion necessity. Second, negative transfer is the documented default when small noisy chemistry tasks are joined naively, so the flat multitask baseline is likely to *lose* to separate heads, which is weak evidence for modularity that the safe-MT paper already provides. Third, modularity buys per-stage attribution: when a prediction is wrong, a modular system can name the stage (A: rare descriptor; B: censoring and 0.7-log noise; C: mixture nonlinearity), and a flat model cannot. For a system whose output will be argued with by perfumers, that is the deliverable.

The experiment plan follows from ranking experiments by how much they change the design rather than by how much they improve a metric.

E2 and E7 are the two that decide the project. E2 answers the featurization question the literature has never asked of potency, and it is cheap: four feature blocks, three tree models, three scaffold splits, a few CPU-hours. E7 is the baseline whose absence makes modularity an assumption rather than a result. E9 is the one to defer — the deorphanization ceiling means its upside is bounded, and its cost is the highest in the table. Compute is a non-issue throughout: the field's best published models are gradient-boosted trees or small networks on 300–2,400 molecules, and the whole pipeline is laptop-scale, with MLflow or W&B functioning not as bookkeeping but as the evaluation apparatus across the hundreds of runs that three splits × five seeds × three stages × ten ablations implies.

## Conclusion

The most useful reframing this research produces is that the quality/potency split is not an engineering convenience but a measured property of perceptual geometry: descriptor-vector **length tracks intensity (r = 0.77) and direction encodes quality**, which means a pipeline emitting magnitude × direction is fitting the data's own factorization rather than imposing one ([PMC13371102](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13371102/)). That single result justifies more of the proposed architecture than any accuracy comparison does. The second reframing is that explicit chemistry is not a legacy baseline to be replaced. Abraham's hand-built six-descriptor equation reaches the label noise floor; a ten-feature transport model matches a 127-descriptor DNN; molecular language models add nothing over RDKit+Morgan; and every attempt to help a small noisy odor task with a large one so far produced negative transfer. The field's remaining headroom is in labels and in loss design — censoring, matrix covariates, hierarchical consensus over conflicting literature values — not in encoders.

What should change in how this project is planned is the ordering. The instinct to build the receptor stage first, because it is the most biologically satisfying, is the wrong call: it is the stage with an 88% orphan ceiling, an uncalibratable concentration handoff, and a documented +0.6% payoff. The instinct to skip the flat end-to-end baseline, because modularity is obviously right, is also wrong — nobody has run it, so running it is a publishable result either way. Build the registry and the feature cache first, run the potency feature-block ablation second, and treat every subsequent stage as something that must earn its keep against an analytic rule and a flat baseline. The pipeline's value proposition is not that it predicts better; it is that when it predicts badly, it says why.
